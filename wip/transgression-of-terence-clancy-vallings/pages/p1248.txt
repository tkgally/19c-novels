Yet she thinks more of Simon at this moment than of herself; hates to give him trouble; staggers along in pain and weariness rather than worry him with a single sigh or complaint.

Arrived at the steps, however, about half-way up the knoll, she was compelled to sit down and rest.

Simon carried the sleeping child in, laid her down before the fire, and returned, whistling softly.

“I wouldn’t sit here long, Joyce; there’s danger of your catching cold.”

She smiled a wan smile, but said nothing.

“Come on, I’ll help you up the steps if you’re tired.”

“I’m afraid—I shan’t be able to move just yet.”
