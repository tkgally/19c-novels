In short, this amateur philanthropist had in various ways contrived to mortally wound Doidge’s self-love. And now that foolish miller, with the eager fatuity of an intense man nipped by jealousy, had come to look upon Mr. Secretan as a possible entangler of his sweetheart’s affections.

It must be confessed that Mary always took pleasure in giving colour to this delusion. To extol the handsome Mr. Secretan was a simple, sure—and, as she thought, perfectly harmless—way of punishing a lover who not seldom required the rod.
