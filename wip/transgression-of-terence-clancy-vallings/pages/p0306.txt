She began to think she had exaggerated her own danger, still more so Terence’s. Why should not Simon’s close friend be her own as well? She was already beginning to look upon Terence as a brother; and surely she could not be so culpably weak or foolish as to be unable to train her heart in this safe direction. She remembered some words from Middlemarch which filled her with comfort, which she could dwell upon and use as a kind of anodyne: “We can keep a guard upon our constancy and our affections as we do upon our other treasures.” She thought to nail her constancy and her affection to Simon as one nails fruit-trees to a wall; and always walked about hammer and knife in hand, so to say, driving in a nail here, lopping off an offending twig there.
