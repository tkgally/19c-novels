[[cont]]Mary, with emotion, “to get clear of Ezekiel by myself. He hangs upon me like an old man of the sea.”

“Couldn’t your father help you?”

“No, no, no! He reveres Ezekiel, and fears him too, and is bent upon my marrying him. You see, Ezekiel owns a good deal of land, as well as the flourishing business of the mill. He is the wealthiest man in Chillington, and hardly considers even the county people above him; in fact, he looks upon your friend Mr. Secretan as a sort of rival.”

Terence laughed.

“Sure the man’s an hysterical fool. Why ever did you get engaged to him, Mary?”

“It was just vanity, I suppose,” she answered simply. “It was so delicious to have the important, clever Mr. Doidge half dying for love of me. Ah, he was in earnest too, and is still. I can’t tell you how he loves me—poor Ezekiel!”
