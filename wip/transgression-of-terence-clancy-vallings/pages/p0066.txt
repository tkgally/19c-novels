Miss Tredethlyn and Mary seemed loth to part this afternoon. The interest of their gossip showed no signs of abatement, for the sufficient reason that each vied with the other in eager putting forward of fresh topics. Judging by their present aspect, it seemed likely that nightfall would find them still talking.

Doidge regarded them with frowning impatience, his every third glance wandering rival-wards. But Simon Secretan, among whose fads the study of astronomy held a high place, was drawing a geometrical diagram on the sandy path, and seemed lost in calculation.
