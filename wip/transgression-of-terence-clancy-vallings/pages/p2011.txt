No upstart from the river was ever admitted into this desirable residential preserve; and even such a traveller of note as a salmon-peal—should he chance to find his way under the canal hatches—was treated with a distant politeness that was apt to send him back, crestfallen and ashamed, and cynically craving for the free-and-easy society of the main stream. There had once been some talk of making the canal free water, of abolishing this house of lords at a swoop; but Mr. Doidge, who owned the water-meadows, put his foot down at once, and wrote a scorching article in his newspaper—and so the scandalous suggestion came to naught.
