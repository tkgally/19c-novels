But Simon was not destined to make even this brief speech; for as he stood looking somewhat haughtily over the heads of his audience, some one on his right also arose, and making his way along the front of the crowd, took up a position beside the reporters’ table. It was Ezekiel Doidge.

There was a profound hush in the large hall, for, though the townsfolk were well accustomed to seeing Doidge constantly on his legs at Local Board meetings and the like, objecting, interfering, browbeating everybody, it was felt that something strange was about to happen now; nor could any fail to perceive that the pale stern man who leaned upon the long table was striving to master some stronger passion than any question of this evening could possibly have aroused.
