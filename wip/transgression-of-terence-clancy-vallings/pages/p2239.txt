“My dear John,” as she herself put it with a <i>naïveté</i> that no foreign travel could obliterate, “advertisement is the soul of modern life; without it you can’t plant a new thing upon the market, anyway. Well, I’m a new thing to society, and maybe not a very high-class article neither—‘seventeen under proof when bottled,’ as we used to say; and advertisement is what I need. Just as you go to the doctor’s to certify the excellence of a new whisky or gin, so must you go to people in society to advertise, and answer for, <i>me</i>. Now, if we go and pitch our tent in a new county, you’ll be free to hunt your fill, no doubt, but who’ll call on your wife, I should like to know?
