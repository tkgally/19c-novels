The speaker was desperately in earnest. Simon knew well that he was telling the truth—at least, in so far as it is given a man to tell it when reviewing his own conduct and motives.

“As for Mary, you’ll think me a common libertine, who compassed her ruin and never gave her a thought afterwards. That’s what all Chillington will be saying of me soon. I don’t know a soul but yourself who, once I’m found out, would endure to let me mention her name or say a word in my own defence. Even you can’t understand my weakness; you never can conceive what a curse to a man this weakness is, how it scorches up every good intention like a flame.
