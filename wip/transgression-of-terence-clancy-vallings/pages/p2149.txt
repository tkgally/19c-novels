The speaker had been describing with much point and sly humour how the great bowler, Dick Yelverton, had missed an easy catch, and in doing so had received the cricket-ball full in his right eye, towards the end of the last match of the season; and he rounded off these remarks with a quotation not a bit too trite for his audience.

“Dick wasn’t pretty to look at, if you remember,” he said with a sly chuckle, “and, faith, there’s a beautiful halo of autumn tints round his eye yet. Look at him, you whose wickets he has scattered so often and so unkindly! Ah, but Dick’s our prince of bowlers still, despite that wicked piece of leather: for what says the poet?—
