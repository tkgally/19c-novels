“Nell, Nell, <i>I love you!</i> For months I have been tortured with this, and speak I will! What has honour to do with it? How could I tell that this fever would come upon me? It possessed me before I was half aware of any danger. You know it well enough; you know I never dreamed of injuring my friend. You’ve led me on without knowing it; you’ve robbed me of my happiness! Is a man dishonourable for being robbed? Nell, there has been no fault on either side, but only <i>nature</i>. You never cared for him, and can never give him any real happiness, though by clinging to him you can make us all three miserable for life. Nell, I will tell him the whole story and appeal to him, and he’ll set you free. I’ll give back his gift, and we’ll go away together; and I’ll work for you, I’ll slave to make a happy home for my beautiful Nell, and surround her with love and sunshine.”
