She felt too much crushed to thank him properly, and had too little natural hypocrisy to feign even so much pleasure as propriety demanded. But he noticed nothing amiss. A sensitive, thin-skinned man in all other relations, he was stone-blind to any shortcoming in this—with the blindness of intense natural loyalty aggravated by an imaginative temperament.

This blindness always deepened Nell’s uneasiness. She felt caged and cramped by this intense belief in her, even to the point of hysterical longing to trumpet forth her unworthiness—to thrust it upon him with harsh words and looks.

“Let us move on again, Simon; I am rather restless to-day.”

The remark was abrupt, the tone anything but kind, but he showed no surprise. “Very well, dear, let us stroll on. What do you say to following the other bank, and taking the woodland path towards Hollacomb?”
