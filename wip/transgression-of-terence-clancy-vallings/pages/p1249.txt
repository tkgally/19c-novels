Then, being freed from the necessity of further effort for the present, Joyce grew a little fretful.

“Mr. Simon?” she said querulously.

“What is it, Joyce? Anything I can do for you? Are you hungry after your journey?”

“Yes, I am hungry. I want my Rosebud.”

“Eh? She’s all right down there by the fire. I want you to rest, not to fidget with the baby.”

“I can’t rest; give me my little one—I <i>will</i> have her.”

Simon laughed, for Joyce’s peremptory tone carried him back to old nursery days, when he, a giant boy of eight years old, had been ordered about by a little creature hardly up to his waist-belt.
