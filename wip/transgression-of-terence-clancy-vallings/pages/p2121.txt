But she was sick at heart, and in need of all her powers for the difficult task of self-persuasion. She had to believe what he said, was resolutely setting her mind to do so; but to accomplish this task it was first needful to crush a powerful sceptical instinct. Terence’s face wore the deprecating, feminine kind of smile that she had learnt to associate with tarradiddles, small or great.

Her looks remaining troubled, her eyes declining to meet his, Terence’s smile faded. He took her hand and kissed it with mournful tenderness. She evidently misunderstood him, his manner seemed to say, but nothing could ever abate his chivalrous affection for her.
