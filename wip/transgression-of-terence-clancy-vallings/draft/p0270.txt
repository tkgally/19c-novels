[[cont]]Sherringham, an invalid friend who lived in the town. She was generally understood to be very kind and attentive to this old friend, and thus earned a good deal of praise on rather slight grounds. To-day’s visit was brief and characteristic; that is to say, she rattled away for some ten minutes, hardly allowing the invalid to get in a word. She was dreadfully busy, was pulled this way and that with her calls and duties and shoppings, and quite grieved that she could spare poor Amy so few minutes. Meanwhile Amy’s trained nurse remained grimly silent in the background. Having opportunities not open to the public of estimating the real worth of this popular great lady, the nurse was quite aware that this friend, who was generally supposed to be so kind and attentive to the poor old invalid, was a grasping unscrupulous woman, whose friendship hinged upon the main chance.
