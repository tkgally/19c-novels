Upon descending from the gallery, Kate was greeted by the man whose profile she had been studying with so much artistic interest, but who had not thrown a single glance her way during the proceedings. His manner was distant and formal, his words few; but he did speak, and did hand her into her pony-carriage. Perhaps he found it necessary to give himself this little treat as a reward for previous resolute conduct.

Yet, though his speech was calm enough, Kate thought Captain Rush must be putting some constraint upon himself. He looked uneasy and excited, and—though she made the admission to herself with some mortification—somewhat absent-minded.
