In truth all Terence’s surroundings combined to inflame him to-night. Peasant-born, and reared in narrow circumstances, he was yet intensely alive to æsthetic impressions. The mellow old dining-room, with its oak panelling and fine pictures, appealed to him keenly; those delicate Claudes and sensuous Correggios, collected by Sir Hamo and his forefathers, spoke to Clancy as to no one else in the room. None of these people, his social superiors, had his innate perception of grand art; almost all of them would have preferred a flashy modern landscape or sickly sweet piece of homely <i>genre</i>, or haply some drama of puppy dogs and milk-cans.
