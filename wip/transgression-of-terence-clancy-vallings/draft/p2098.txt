For as Ezekiel, swelling and scowling with indignation, stood watching the captain’s exit, he put forth his hand to grasp the table, and touched a writing-desk which had been lying there since the beginning of the interview. He looked down at the desk, and his face changed.

“Don’t go, sir,” he muttered brokenly. “Lord forgive me for helpin’ to spoil my poor girl’s chance! Stand by me, sir, even though I be a hot-tongued fool. I ain’t fit to hunt the man down alone; my strength has been failin’ these months; and—and I can’t do nothin’ without help, nowadays. Don’t be afeard that I mean any violence; I tried that tack once and found I had no stomach for’t; too much of a coward for that, now my health’s gone—too much broke and wore out and shattered, I reckon!
