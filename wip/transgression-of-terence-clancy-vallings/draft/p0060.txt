“Look! Surely those two are Mary Pethick and Mr. Doidge?” She spoke briskly, doubtless with a sense of relief at the coming interruption. The strain was removed; they had returned abruptly to the cheerful domain of King Commonplace.

“Simon, I think Mary is unhappy with that man; I do believe he beats her! He’s such a black, sour-looking fellow, that I’ve always been afraid to speak to him.”

“Oh, he’s a good fellow,” quoth Simon, in his large careless way. He hates me like poison, and always shows it, which is a good sign. There’s no sneaking hypocrisy about Ezekiel Doidge; I like a man whose face reflects his heart in that way. Perhaps he’s a bit savage and out of tune with the world because of Mary’s coldness. His sweetheart doesn’t care for him, they say.”

“Do you blame her for not caring enough?”
