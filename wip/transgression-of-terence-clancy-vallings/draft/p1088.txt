Terence made a strained effort to speak: but one word would come from him. “Suicide,” he muttered; then sank down to the roots of the alder, reclining against the trunk.

“Never, never!” said Nell, earnestly. “Why should you accuse poor Mary? She was the happiest of girls, the last in the world to commit such a crime; it has been a sad accident—no more.”

“Accident?” as he clutched at the word and repeated it, his voice came back to him. “Yes, yes, you’re right, Nell, an accident. I never thought of that. I only knew her by sight, and thought perhaps——”

He hardly knew what he was saying. Instinct was urging him to lie; but reason had not yet told him in what direction to begin.
