[[cont]]self-pity overwhelmed him. He sank on his knees beside Nell, buried his head in her lap, and sobbed like a child.

Nell put her arms about his neck, and they wept together, and this miserable moment had a core of strange happiness for them both. The constraint which had been growing between husband and wife was broken down. Nell felt that all his faults were being swept away in the outrush of her pity, that he was all her own, to be cherished and comforted and loved; while he felt that, come what might, she loved him; that this love of a wife, noble and faultless in his eyes, was a thing to lean upon and take refuge in. Could he but keep this, he might hope to bear up against whatsoever shame or disgrace might be in store for him.

“Dearest, tell me your trouble,” urged Nell, with courage in her voice as well as tenderness. “Let me bear it with you. Won’t you let your own wife help you?”
