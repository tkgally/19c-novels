She has been meeting Terence Clancy, basking in the sympathy of a softer, more genial nature than Ezekiel’s—in the friendship of a man, his superior in culture, breeding, and every outward quality that makes for love. She is thinking of Terence now—thinking with gentle melancholy of the parting by the pool, and with a strange fluttering hope of the “meeting on the morrow.” By nature she is wayward, not false; but these two men—one by hard superiority, the other by dangerous, unthinking kindness—have sapped her fealty, prepared the soil of her heart for a crop of treachery to be reaped in due season.
