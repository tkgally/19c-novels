“I’ll never do a shabby trick by old Simon; he has been a good friend to me.”

“And a good enemy to me, Terence, so that I have a fine, round, full-grown spite against him. He has called me names, and hindered my achievement of a practice by his d——d interference with other people’s concerns, and one of these days I’ll be even with him. ‘Hard words break no bones,’ they say; but they are apt to stop the daily bread of a general practitioner for all that—and hunger sharpens a man’s spite, let me tell you.”
