As regards Nell, his critical faculty had positively suffered atrophy from disuse; he had fallen into that fatal obtuseness which is the natural outcome of over-intensity in any passion. As for suspecting his friend, of conceiving him capable of treacherous hankerings after Nell, his mind was not capable of forming the idea, much less of giving it harbourage.

Terence, with his feminine intuitiveness and quick-probing sympathy, had no difficulty in reading his visitor’s thoughts. He knew, as certainly as though Simon had described it, how Nell had rebuffed him; he knew that Simon was even now making reflections upon his own clumsiness and want of tact, that nothing short of brutal frankness on the part of some one or other would ever waken him to the truth.
