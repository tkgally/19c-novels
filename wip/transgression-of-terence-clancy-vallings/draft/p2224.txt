[[cont]]and perhaps outlasting it, and verily ‘the coals thereof are coals of fire.’ . . . But, my love, never give another thought to those jealous ravings of mine—lies, all lies, I say. Your superiority, which I railed at, so far from hurting me, saved me from complete degradation, kept my conscience alive. I’ve never been quite free from remorse; half my late ambition sprang from the desire to stifle it. Sweet wife, kiss me forgiveness of those lies. . . . Ah, Nellie, you’re a good and loving woman!” After a pause he added, “Think how I can best atone to you for those ravings, sweetheart.”

“Do not think of me now, Terence; but —but surely an atonement is due to—to some one else?”

Terence understood her clearly, and the light went from his face; he had not exaggerated the power of his jealousy.

“<i>You</i> can tell him, Nell—that man, I mean—that I am sorry.”
