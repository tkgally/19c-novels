Silence, not a whisper; the hushed woods seemed to listen, but no sound came.

Hitherto, when he had spoken of love, it had been in a tongue strange to Nell; spoken passion being a dead thing to a passionless listener, she had always been able to answer him calmly, without any acute sense of the flatness of her own words; but now an abler tutor than Simon had taught her the meaning of heart-language. No commonplace answer would come now: she was dumb.

Simon was deeply hurt. A draught of chill air seemed to pass into his mind. Nell had been moody and capricious with him at times, had taxed his faculty of accusing himself in order to palliate her faults; but this wounding silence was a thing to cut deeper. It was the first sting of the adder which he had been nursing, the first stroke of the lash his bosom friend had prepared for his back.
