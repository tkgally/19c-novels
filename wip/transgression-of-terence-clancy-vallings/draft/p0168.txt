For poor Ezekiel managed to be over-intense in his humility, as he had been in his arrogance, and as he would be in all things to the end of the chapter. He was a man as little understood by his public as Simon Secretan himself. Some people went so far as to call him half-crazy, hinting darkly that there was madness in the Doidge family; others thought the hot Celtic blood inherited from a long line of Cornish ancestors was enough to account for even his vagaries of passion. At any rate, his fierce devotion to Mary was a kind of furnace in which many of his faults had been burnt up and purged away.
