# Chapter XII.

I<sc>n</sc> comparing himself with the gentler half of creation, man will sometimes set forth—in a condescending judicial way, with due sense of his own magnanimity—the one or two minor points of woman’s admitted superiority. After stripping her of justice, honesty, generosity, and other garments which he has appropriated for himself, he kindly leaves her with such flimsy underclothing as may be woven of sympathy, unselfishness, and a few other trifling woman’s graces. But he seldom realizes how supreme a barrenness must needs ensue upon the loss of these mere femininities.
