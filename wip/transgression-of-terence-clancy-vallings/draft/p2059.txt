Then followed an awkward pause, broken only by a remark or two upon the blossom-laden horse-chestnut under which they were sitting.

Kate had been supporting her friend’s stinging condolences with the kind of stoicism which is apt to end hysterically, and even in a happier mood would have been deeply embarrassed at receiving these two—the tailor whom she had openly scorned, the tailor’s son whom she, the gin-distiller’s granddaughter, had snubbed so haughtily. Had they come to triumph over her she could have faced them without flinching; but she was as clearly conscious of the old gentleman’s kindly motives as though he had expounded them for an hour.
