“<i>Scis horas, nescis horam</i>,” muttered Terence, as he glanced towards it. “Ah, but I <i>do</i> know the hour. The hour has come. The tide of my affairs has already been taken at the flood.”

His gaze wandered away again to the steep oak coppice and downwards to where the tall ashes and alders, which marked the course of the hidden brook, were cut by the line of the garden wall. Presently the whole scene faded again, giving place to fancy-pictures of nobler line and hue; then something recalled him to earth again suddenly. In the boundary wall below him there was a dainty little wrought-iron gate, arched with stone chevrons, and giving access to a woodland path which led past the stables to some cottages higher up the combe. It was the opening of this gate that arrested his attention.
