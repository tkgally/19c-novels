[[cont]]only of an innocent man, but of a friend and benefactor. He could not bear that—could not even begin to bear it; he could not stand upright for a moment under the storm of contumely that was about to burst over his head. The torture would kill him, unless—unless——

He turned his head for a moment, and cast one miserable glance backwards.

Nell was looking towards him, flushed and happy, with affectionate pride in her eyes. She had been listening to his praises—was already explaining away what seemed like hypocrisy in him, by saying to herself, “That was but a pretence put on to try me. He does in reality think with these gentlemen, and will serve their interests loyally.”

His wife’s bright looks seemed to revive Terence’s drooping courage for the moment.

“I’m a coward,” he muttered—“a weak, cringing coward. What proof against me can he possibly have obtained?”
