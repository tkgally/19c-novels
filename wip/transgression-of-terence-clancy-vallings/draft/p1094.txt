# Chapter V.

SOME two weeks had passed, and Chillington was still seething with the tragic death of Mary Pethick.

As to the cause of her death there was no room for serious doubt, no loophole for kind-hearted coroner’s jurymen to ease their feelings by such phrases as “misadventure,” “unsound mind,” or anything else of a softening description. When, after a prolonged sitting, they took their hats and sticks from the parlour-table at Hollacomb Farm and clattered out of the room, they had passed the only verdict possible in face of the evidence, medical and other, laid before them.
