An absence of some months, however, had not softened the stubborn Julius. He listened calmly, asked no questions, committed himself to nothing, whereat the squire’s disappointment was sufficiently patent. His long-cherished desire to have his grandchildren playing about his knee seemed as far as ever from fulfilment; for he was well assured that Julius, if he did not marry Kate, would inevitably die a bachelor. Yet Mr. Rush had not by any means fired his last shot. Under pressure of failure this simple old man was growing cunning.

“You’ve heard nothing of Captain Saunderson and his wife, I suppose?” he presently asked, in a casual voice.
