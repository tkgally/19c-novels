Nor did you ever really love me; you had for me only a kind of spurious affection bred of the desire to free yourself from an irksome tie. Had I ever possessed your love—such love as a woman is capable of, and such as poor Mary gave me—I should have made a clean breast of it long ago. Curious, isn’t it, that this parting scene of ours should be bringing out the truth like this? And not only do I see the past very clearly now, but I also foresee the future. I know precisely what is coming, perceive it as clearly as though it were actually happening before my eyes.”

These last sentences might have given Nell the key to her husband’s evil mood, but she was herself too distracted to see through his ravings. Every word he said—for he seemed to speak quietly and naturally, being too weak to raise his voice—was to her absolute truth; and she could hardly breathe under the heavy weight of his condemnation.
