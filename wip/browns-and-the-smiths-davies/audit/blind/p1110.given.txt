But I have left Mr. Edgar smith and the boy long enough in their most critical pofition on the cliff. The child, as commanded, loosened his grasp dropped with a heavy, exhausted fall. It was a terrible moment to the anxious spectators when, instead of alighting in safety, he missed his footing, and seemed as if he should have dragged Edgar with him in his precipitate slide downwards. But with one of those extraordinary acts of exertion, which I will nor call superhuman, since in moments of utmost need humanity seems so generally capable of making them, Edgar continued to support the weight of the boy till he regained his lost balance, and with the help of his other hand placed himself in the place of comparative safety. He was a fine; bold, but ragged little fellow, and his face was now white with terror and exhaustion.
