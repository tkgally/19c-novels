Hannah felt as she walked out of the simple little church, beneath the bright summer sky and under the golden hills, that it was altogether, in spite of the inferiority of the building, not only more romantic than the church at Goslingford, but that, at that moment, it seemed more favourable to the devotional feelings. The high-crowned hats of the Welshwomen seemed more in harmony with pious thoughts, than the gay bonnets and fine flowers of Miss Westcote and the Miss Splints. But remember, reader, I do not quite uphold Hannah in this sentiment. It is the business of religion to convert the outward things of life into its own essence, and not to be converted by them.
