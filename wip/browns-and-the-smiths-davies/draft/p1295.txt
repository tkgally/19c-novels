But notwithstanding Mrs. Westcote’s laughter and high spirits, everybody said she was disappointed—and when, about a fortnight after the announcement of Edgar Smith’s marriage, Miss Westcote’s to Mr. Frederick Splint was also announced, everybody said so still; and those who had blamed Edgar Smith were now inclined to excuse him. In short, the tide of popularity, which had set in so strongly in favour of the Browns at the time of their losses, now set in as strongly in favour of the Smiths. A grand armistice seemed to have been tacitly agreed upon by the two factions—the prelude to a permanent peace, to be cemented, as among other high contracting parties, by a matrimonial alliance.
