The Smith dining-room was probably as handsome as that of any Montague in Verona, though, to confess the truth, it had nothing in the least ancestral about it, except a rather vulgar portrait of grandfather Smith, representing a stout, florid, middle-aged gentleman, with a powdered wig and pigtail, dressed in a white waistcoat and blue coat with brass buttons. In short, the whole aspect of Tudor Lodge was undeniably modern, or, to speak plainly, bran-new—an aspect which, to some minds, is as objectionable in a residence as in a coat.
