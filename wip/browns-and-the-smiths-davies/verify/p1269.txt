In country towns the doings of our neighbours are, by no means, absolutely indifferent to us. We have not attained to that frozen self-sufficiency, common only in great cities, which makes it of no consequence whether the bustle next door is occasioned by a wedding or a funeral. We yet take a deep interest in the destiny and the doings of our neighbours, and, to my prejudiced mind, even at the risk of, perhaps, not a little gossip, there is something human and kindly in this
