Then she had to calm the fears of her friend, Miss Harriet Richards, which, to tell the truth, though she would not have confessed it, she in some degree shared. In the opinion of that good soul, railways were one of the crying sins of the age. Although she was too amiable to have left her dying testimony against them, as did Alexander Campbell, one of the “Men of the North,” against “the ships that keep their course in spite of the weather—that presumptuous sin,” yet she did not the less think them an impatient tampering with the source of all evil.
