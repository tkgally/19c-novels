[[cont]]Mr. Brown repeated, with the eagerness of a man anxious to convince himself. “She must see, Clara, that there was nothing but kindness for her in my motives.”

Mr. Brown had perhaps never in his life before, or, at least, never since the death of Mrs. Brown, been so communicative of his own feelings to any human being; but Clara Wellby had a way of gaining people’s confidence. She was so frank and outspoken herself, so kind-hearted; and, above all, nobody was afraid of her, in spite of her sharp tongue.

“Very likely she sees it all, Mr. Brown. But suppose she does, I do not see that there is anything very cheering for her in the view. Perhaps a little change——”
