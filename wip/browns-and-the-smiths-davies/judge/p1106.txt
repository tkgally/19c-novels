Edgar Smith had ascended several of the Swiss mountains. He had not, like his celebrated namesake, “Albert,” ever accomplished the ascent of “Sovran Blanc” himself—indeed he had never tried; but he had more idea than an untravelled Englishman, if out of Goslingford there be such a person, how to climb an icy, snow-clad rock. As he ascended, he prepared his footsteps with his hands till he stood in safety, at a height from whence he could reach the legs of the poor child.
