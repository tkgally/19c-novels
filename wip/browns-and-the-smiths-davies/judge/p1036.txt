“Oh, yes, you would, unless”—with a smile—“something had thrown glamour in your eyes.”

Mary Westcote smiled too—a languid smile of pretended indifference.

“I don’t think many people handsome—men, I mean.”

“Oh, but he had such splendid dark eyes, so melancholy and poetical, and beautiful black hair.”

“Oh, but I always preferred blue eyes and brown hair for men—they are so much more—more—I like them better—I do, indeed.”

“It is a good thing,” remarked Miss Venetia, with more philosophy than originality, “that there are varieties of taste; but, for my part, I think I never saw anyone look so fascinating as this young clergyman.”
