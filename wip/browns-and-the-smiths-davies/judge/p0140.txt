They were thought “foreign,” and the Goslingford public yet rejoiced unfeignedly in the sturdy English belief that “foreign” and “contemptible” were quite convertible adjectives. But Miss Westcote, who was an original thinker, if not as far as moustaches in general, but at least as far as this moustache in particular, was concerned, sat awaiting the arrival of its owner with much complacency. Was not she the belle of Goslingford? Had not her handsome-worked muslin dress been made at Buttonborough at Paris House? And was not Mr. Edgar Smith a person of unexceptionable taste? It had never struck Miss Westcote that taste could reach beyond dress and appearance. Perhaps she did not know there was anything beyond.
