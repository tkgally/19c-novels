Hannah had all her life been afraid of her father, and yet he was a fond father, and not by any means a bad-tempered man. If she could only have coaxed him a little when he was irritated, all would have been well. But she could not do that, she was too frightened. It was this fear which had all her life weakened her love for him—this fear, coupled with a feeling, only partly acknowledged, that he ought to have done more to make her life pleasant.
