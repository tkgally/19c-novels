VOL. XIII.—DARIEN. BY ELIOT WARBURTON,

“This last production, from the pen of the author of ‘The Crescent and the Cross,’ has the same elements of a very wide popularity. It will please its thousands.”—<i>Globe</i>.

VOL. XIV.—FAMILY ROMANCE; OR, DOMESTIC ANNALS OF THE ARISTOCRACY. <sc>by</sc> SIR BERNARD BURKE.

“It were impossible to praise too highly as a work of amusement this most interesting book. It ought to be found on every drawing-room table.”—<i>Standard</i>.

VOL. XV.—THE LAIRD OF NORLAW. <sc>by the author of “mrs. margaret maitland.”</sc>

“Scottish life and character are here delineated with true artistic skill.”—<i>Herald</i>.

VOL. XVI.—THE ENGLISHWOMAN IN ITALY.

“Mrs. Gretton’s work is interesting, and full of instruction.”—<i>The Times</i>.

VOL. XVII.—NOTHING NEW. <sc>by the author of “john halifax, gentleman.”</sc>

“We cordially commend this book. The same graphic power, deep pathos, healthful sentiment, and masterly execution, which place that beautiful work ‘John Halifax,’ among the English classics, are everywhere displayed.”—<i>Chronicle</i>.

VOL. XVIII.—THE LIFE OF JEANNE D’ALBRET,

“Nothing can be more interesting than Miss Freer’s story of the life of Jeanne d’Albret, and the narrative is as trustworthy as it is attractive.”—<i>Post</i>.

VOL. XIX.—THE VALLEY OF A HUNDRED FIRES. <sc>by the author of</sc> “<sc>margaret and her bridesmaids</sc>.”

“If asked to classify this work, we should give it a place between ‘John Halifax,’ and ‘The Caxtons.’”—<i>Herald</i>.

VOL. XX.—THE ROMANCE OF THE FORUM. <sc>by</sc> PETER BURKE, SERJEANT AT LAW,

“A work of singular interest, which can never fail to charm. The present cheap and elegant edition includes the true story of the Colleen Bawn.”—<i>Illustrated News</i>.

VOL. XXI.—ADELE. BY JULIA KAVANAGH.

“Adèle is the best work we have had by Miss Kavanagh; it is a charming story. The interest kindled in the first chapter burns brightly to the close.”—<i>Athenæum</i>.

VOL. XXII. STUDIES FROM LIFE. <sc>by the author of “john halifax, gentleman.”</sc>

“These ‘Studies from Life’ are remarkable for graphic power and observation. The book will not diminish the reputation of the accomplished author.”—<i>Saturday Review</i>.

VOL. XXIII.—GRANDMOTHER’S MONEY.

“A good novel. The most interesting of the author’s productions.”—<i>Athenæum</i>.

VOL. XXIV.—A BOOK ABOUT DOCTORS. <sc>by</sc> J. C. JEAFFRESON, ESQ.

“A delightful book.”—<i>Athenæum</i>. “A book to be read and re-read; fit for the study as well as the drawing-room table and the circulating library.”—<i>Lancet</i>.

VOL. XXV.—NO CHURCH.

“We advise all who have the opportunity to read this book. It is well worth the study.”—<i>Athenæum</i>

VOL. XXVI.—MISTRESS AND MAID. <sc>by the author of “john halifax, gentleman.”</sc>

“A good, wholesome book, gracefully written, and as pleasant to read as it is instructive.”—<i>Athenæum</i>.
