But to return to Edgar. It had struck that astute youth that as he must go to Miss Richards’ party, it would be as well not only to do it with a good grace, but to leave no stone unturned to make the evening as agreeable as possible. He therefore set out to call on that good simple soul with a diplomatic end in view. Edgar Smith, though a conceited youth (and we all know that truly great minds are never conceited—<i>vide</i> Socrates, Sir Isaac Newton, &c.), was not without the heroic quality of being able to make the best of things as they are, and could turn even his misfortunes to account.
