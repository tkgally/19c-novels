“Oh, no, Mr. Smith, I could not—you must not, indeed.”

Edgar Smith laughed heartily. He knew just as well as Hannah the sensation such a proceeding would have produced, but he would have liked nothing better. He did not wish to annoy her however, and he read her feelings at a glance.

“Well, if the prejudices of society, or your own prejudices, are against me,” he added, a little more gravely, “let me set a chair for you at the table.”

“Do, Hannah, dear,” cried Miss Richards from behind the tea-urn, “come and sit at the table. Here is a nice quiet place just beside me.”
