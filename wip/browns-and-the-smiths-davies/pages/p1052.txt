“Oh, I am not saying anything against Mr. Greenfield, except that I am astonished; but, of course, people know their own interest best.”

“And their own duties,” said Edgar.

Edgar Smith had, what to many was a very provoking way of snubbing persons when they were arguing as they supposed so as to please him, and to no one did he oftener display this peculiarity than to Mrs. Westcote.

Edgar Smith, as we have seen, could be unjust himself, but he would allow no one else to be so. He was lamentably ungrateful to anybody who attacked his supposed adversaries.
