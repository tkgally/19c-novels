The tide of public feeling, that is, the feeling of the polite world of Goslingford, for a time rose decidedly on the Brown side.

“Old Mr. Brown,” said Miss Clara Wellby, to her friend Miss Harriet Richards, “would never have permitted such new-fangled nonsense. But people now-a-days must all be finer and wiser than their fathers. Perhaps next time the meat will be all carved at the side-table.”

“It is sad to see so much vanity and display among people professing Christian principles,” rejoined Miss Richards. “We shall hear of them playing cards next.”
