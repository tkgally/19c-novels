Since then, I have learned to perceive the greatness of my crime; but then, I was so unconscious of any guilt, that before I crossed swords with my enemy, I knelt upon one knee, and offered up a brief and humble prayer. Digby made no such sign, but he turned aside, perhaps in courtesy. We then addressed ourselves to our task.

On measuring weapons, Digby’s appeared to be half a foot longer than mine. He placed it under his foot, to break off the point; the grass, however, was soft, the steel was strong, and it would not break. I interrupted his further attempt, by saying, that I considered the shortness of my sword as an advantage.

“Be it so!” he said, as he advanced; and the next moment we were engaged.
