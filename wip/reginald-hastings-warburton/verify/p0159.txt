My attention, however, as well as Digby’s was rivetted to a small table near us, where sat two persons engaged in close conversation, and inaudible to every one except ourselves, who were just behind the speakers. To my surprise, I recognized in one the visage of Sir Janus Demiroy; and though his cloak was drawn across the lower part of his face, and his hat slouched low upon his forehead, I could distinguish in the other the lurid eyes peculiar to Hezekiah Doom. The latter seemed engaged in the hopeless attempt to bring Sir Janus to a decided answer on some matter of importance, and was winding up his argument, in a low but vehement voice:

“And thus, my worthy patron, thou hast placed thyself in a position of double danger, from which nothing but prompt and fearless decision can save thee: thou hast advanced great sums of money to the men of the Parliament, and earnest proffers and promises of service to the man Charles.
