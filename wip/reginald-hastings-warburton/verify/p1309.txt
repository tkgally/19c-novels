I need not describe the gratitude we felt for our deliverance and restoration and security and comfort: the poor frightened ladies especially; for I fear that a long succession of hairbreadth escapes had rendered Bryan, and even myself, less thankful for and observant of such blessings than we might have been.

The Hague is called “the largest <i>village</i> in the world,” containing more inhabitants and greater wealth than many cities of repute. It maintains, however, a sort of village character for gossip and idleness, engendered by the absence of all municipal institutions and occupations that keep citizens busied about their own affairs.
