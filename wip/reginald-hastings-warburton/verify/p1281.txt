The reputed share of her husband in Waller’s plot afforded an excuse for every species of annoyance and spoliation of his family: day by day, the good lady’s cherished stores of household linen and worsted stuffs—even her poultry and her fatted lambs, were claimed and carried off by the insatiable troopers. The poor housewife-heart of the lady was rent and torn by these spoliations—this ruin of a life-long industry and pride. She succumbed to her sickness, and when she died, she had scarcsely enough left of all her once-boasted linen to make her shroud.
