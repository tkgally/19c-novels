I was now requested to depart; with all imaginable kindness, however, on the part of the good merchant, who explained to me that he was anxious his house should seem quite silent up to the moment of his household’s departure. “For we do not know,” he said, “in times like these, what snares may be employed against us.”

He left me alone with Zillah; but only a few minutes intervened before I rejoined him. I did not dare to press my suit under Zillah’s sad circumstances, and my own penniless condition; yet I was cheered and comforted by those few minutes, and I found myself walking with a prouder step than I was wont to do.
