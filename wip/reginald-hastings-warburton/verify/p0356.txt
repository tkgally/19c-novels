[[center]] THE WILMINGTONS.

[[center]] By the Author of “Emilia Wyndham,” “Mordaunt Hall,” &c. 3 vols.

“A clever and most interesting tale, written with excellent feeling, full of charming traits of character, and developing a highly instructive moral.”—<i>Examiner.</i>

“‘The Wilmingtons’ contains scenes which no living author save the writer of ‘Two Old Men’s Tales’ could have written so well. Few will be able to escape from this new story when they have once entered upon it.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

[[center]] PRIDE AND IRRESOLUTION.

[[center]] By the Author of “The Discipline of Life.” 3 vols.

“‘The Discipline of Life’ is, we believe, the first production of Lady Emily Ponsonby. It cannot fail to place the authoress in the first rank of female novelists.”—<i>Britannia.</i>

[[center]] LEONARD NORMANDALE; OR, THE THREE BROTHERS.

[[center]] By the H<sc>on.</sc> C. S. S<sc>avile.</sc> 3 vols.

“This novel has many claims on attention. It is full of incident, contains a considerable amount of anecdote and adventure, and is written with and for a purpose. We recommend it to all our readers.”—<i>Weekly Chronicle.</i>

[[center]] THE PETREL.

[[center]] A TALE OF THE SEA.

[[center]] By a Naval Officer. 3 vols.

“The author of this interesting work is, we believe, Admiral Fisher, who commanded at the blockade of Alexandria in 1840. By his skill in depicting life on the ocean he will, we doubt not, supply the place in nautical literature vacant by the death of the lamented Captain Marryat. His life of the ship is equally forcible, dashing, and free.”—<i>Morning Post.</i>

[[center]] PASSAGES IN THE LIFE OF

[[center]] MRS. MARGARET MAITLAND, OF SUNNYSIDE.

[[center]] Written by Herself. 3 vols.

“The most gratifying work of its class since the great delineator of Scottish manners ceased to write.”—<i>Tait’s Magazine.</i>

“We have read this book with unusual pleasure. It has points of merit which may compare with Miss Ferrier, Galt, Lockhart and Wilson.”—<i>Examiner.</i>

[[center]] ADVENTURES of a GOLDFINDER.

[[center]] Written by Himself. 3 vols.

“We do not overrate this book when we place it amongst the most entertaining works which the season has yet produced. It cannot fail to obtain a large extent of circulation and popularity. In the scenes and adventures in California, the writer produces passages which may stand without disadvantage side by side with the most credited writings of Cooper, or the redoubtable Monsieur Violet himself.”—<i>Weekly Chronicle.</i>

[[center]] THE MAID OF ORLEANS.

[[center]] By the Author of “Whitefriars,” “Owen Tudor,” &c. 3 vols.

“An excellent novel. The character of the ‘Maid of Orleans’ is drawn with a glow and fervour, a mixture of elevation and simplicity, which are alike powerful and attractive.”—<i>Athenæum.</i>

[[center]] THE OLD WORLD and the NEW.

[[center]] By M<sc>rs.</sc> T<sc>rollope.</sc> 3 vols.

“A very clever novel, presenting in marked contrast the ‘Old World and the New’ during the eventful epoch out of which we are emerging.”—<i>Post.</i>

[[center]] ROCKINGHAM; OR, THE YOUNGER BROTHER.

[[center]] Second Edition. 3 vols.

“We beg to call our readers’ attention to ‘Rockingham; or the Younger Brother,’ a book which, from internal evidence, must have been written by a person constantly mingling in the highest English society. The work abounds in interest, and, indeed, we should be at a loss to name another recent novel that shows anything like the same power of painting strong passion.”—<i>Quarterly Review.</i>
