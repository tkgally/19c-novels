I felt towards his Majesty at that moment, something of the sentiment which John Bunyan has since told me he experienced at the sight of a bishop in his robes; the idea of the man was entirely absorbed in that of his august function. I read in the mournful look that he cast on his saluting people, a vision pregnant with prophetic power, informed by vast and secret knowledge of what was then preparing against him and his kingdom. I saw, as I thought, the destiny of England incarnate in his sacred person.

My reverence was deep as it was sincere, and the courteous bow with which it was acknowledged became a nod of recognition, as Digby whispered my name in the royal ear. In a few minutes the cavalcade had passed, and I joined myself to those who brought up the rear.
