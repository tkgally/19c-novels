Not so, however, in the eyes of Mistress Phœbe: she hated or adored every living creature that came within the ken of her ardent spirit, with a sincerity very trying to her parent. Phœbe held the King in enthusiastic reverence, and proportionably detested the Puritans, and every man, or measure, that was opposed to the royal will. The Rev. Mr. Doom was her especial object of dislike; not only because he was in her eyes a downright rebel, and little better than a heretic, but because he was a sort of rival to her dear old chaplain, who had christened and catechized, comforted, and counselled her in all the little sorrows, and difficulties that her young life had ever known. Hugo, of course, had been inoculated with Phœbe’s distaste for the Puritan teacher, and had pictured him, in his imagination, as a mixture of Calvinist, Jesuit, and devil.
