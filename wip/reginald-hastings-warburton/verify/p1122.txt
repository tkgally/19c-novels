I spoke to him of his young patroness, and his manner changed: he said vehemently that he himself had been infatuated by her—he called himself unworthy—a broken branch. “‘And will she marry Hastings?’ I inquired.

“‘Never,’ he replied, triumphantly. ‘When I reproached her with preferring her own passion to the interests of her cause and her faith, she replied proudly that I was presumptuous. That if she could not control her emotions, she was at least mistress of her actions, and could and would afford a life-long proof of her devotion to the cause.’
