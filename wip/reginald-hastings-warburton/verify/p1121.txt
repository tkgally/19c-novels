“I will tell you,” resumed Falkland. “This is the last time that we shall be together, I feel well assured; and I have no desire for concealment, especially as I may unconsciously have wronged you. You know that mysterious man whom they call Hezekiah: of his personal history I have no right to speak, for his dreadful secret is his own; but of his relations with one whom I now hope you may one day possess, I mean to make no secret. I have already told you how I first became acquainted with this man. He was grateful to me, as far as gratitude was compatible with the stronger impulses of his nature. With an eye which nothing escapes, he perceived the more than interest that I took in the person of whom we spoke. He dared to tell me of it, and to reproach me with it as a sin. I never felt as if such pure and purifying thoughts as I cherished towards her <i>could</i> be sin; but, perhaps they were so.
