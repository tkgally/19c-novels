I had let fall Bryan’s letter, as I was reading that from Hotspur; they had both been addressed to “Master Neville,” by which name alone I was known to the old goldsmith. He picked up the letter I had dropped, and as he handed it to me he hesitated, and at last said timidly:—

“You will pardon me, I hope, for having accidentally seen the name by which your correspondent addresses you. I have had inquiries made frequently for Lord Hastings, which I was unable to answer. Dare I do so now?”

I answered eagerly in the affirmative, buoyed up with a rising hope.

“It was only an old man,” the goldsmith resumed: “a very old and reverend-looking man, who was making inquiries in that name.
