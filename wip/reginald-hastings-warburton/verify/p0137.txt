By this time the party had cleared the pass, beyond which lay an open country, for many miles, almost as far as the river Witham. Following close after the Roundheads and their late prisoner, I found Blount stationed at the mouth of the pass, sitting stiffly as beseemed a sentinel, with his carbine at the recover. As I passed him he drew up at my side, and whispered, “I was forced to cleave yon runagate’s skull in two, for he was about to fire, and would have brought his party upon us before you had cleared the pass. Now, in our Lady’s name, let the Pedlar, or whoever he be, make the best of his way, for there is at least five score of Roundheads by yon farm-house.”

It was too true. A few hundred yards off, there was a large watch-fire blazing high among the trees, and ever and anon, dark figures crossed it to and fro.
