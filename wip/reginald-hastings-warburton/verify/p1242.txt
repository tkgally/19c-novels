I resumed my preparations for departure, and then waited with impatience for the gaoler’s last evening visit to be ended. This poor fellow had, however, been kind to me, after his fashion, and I felt some remorse as to the consequences he might suffer from my escape. When he appeared, I asked him if he had ever lost a prisoner? He looked at me very knowingly, as if he had detected me in making overtures to him;

“No, no,” said he, “it won’t do. I never lost a prisoner, and I never will.”

“But,” I persisted, “suppose he lost himself, would you suffer for it?”

“Why, no,” he replied, thoughtfully, and scratching his head—“I don’t s’pose I should; for I’m no regular gaoler like, but only doing the business out of my common duty as a soldier, to oblige the Governor.
