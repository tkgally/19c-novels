At once for trial and defence, I was merely asked, “whether my name was correctly stated, and whether I was not found in company with the frizzled Madam, called d’Aubigny, when (taking traitorous advantage of the Parliament’s safe conduct) she was detected, as being mother to the damnable plot.” I stated the case as simply as I could, and called upon their own minister, Hezekiah, as witness to the truth of my assertion, that I had scorned the office of conspirator.

One of my judges, named Hewson, spoke for all the rest, and thus delivered his judgement: “This Philistine scorneth to conspire, forsooth; but the scorner is an abomination unto men; yea, more especially when he escorteth foolish women, whose heads and hair are filled with deceit and danger to the Commonwealth.
