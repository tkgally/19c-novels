Yes, there are letters, but they are so few, that I prolong the pleasure of my suspense, and husband the treasure of something new whereon to speculate. And now I gaze again, and scrutinize; but, surely, my eyes are dim, and I have a strange wandering in my thoughts. I sit down to soothe myself, and then again strain my eyes to those faint letters.

Now I can read: “<i>In the left corner, beneath the bed, a small square stone will——</i>” My sight fails me again, and at the same time the gaoler enters with my daily meal.

It was not my accustomed attendant, but a soldier almost as silent. Eagerly as I strove to obtain information from him, I could only learn my former warden had sickened of what was commonly called the gaol-plague, and died that morning; and surely I, too, must have caught it: my eyes are yet more dim, and my brain reels—I shall hide away my writings, and—
