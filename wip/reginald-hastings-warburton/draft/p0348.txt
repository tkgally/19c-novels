Phœbe seemed like a ghost, desirous of speaking, but unable to do so until first addressed. But gradually the mysterious agency of the features was mediatizing between those two disturbed young souls. Hugo’s wore an expression of pained and almost reproachful inquiry, to which Phœbe’s replied by an air of frank and fearless confidence, touched with deep tenderness and sympathy. Hugo took her hand, and clasped it in both of his.

At last Phœbe said, softly, “Dear Hugo, are you suffering?”

“Not so much as when last we met,” he replied.

“What, at Lady d’Aubigny’s?” rejoined Phœbe. “Surely you must be under some strange illusion; and, now I think of it, so seemed your brother too.”
