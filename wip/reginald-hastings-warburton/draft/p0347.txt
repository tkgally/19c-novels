Then observing a very unsympathizing expression on the tough young Cornet’s face, she added timidly: “Besides—besides, I have known him before; we were children together long ago.”

Seldom has beauty pleaded in vain, especially in manly England, to a youthful heart, and under the romantic spell of moon-lit glistening tears. The young Cornet, to do him justice, tried not to look conscious, and gave the order as Phœbe desired; assisting her, however, into the carriage with something of a martyr’s air.

Again the waggon moved on, and Phœbe and Hugo were left together. The waiting-woman, in addition to her former terror and fatigue, shrunk with dread from the corpse’s resting-place, cowered by Hugo’s feet, wrapped her head in her wimple, and soon sobbed herself to sleep, or at least to perfect silence.
