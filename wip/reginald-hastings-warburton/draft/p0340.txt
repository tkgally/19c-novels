Poor Challoner and Tomkins were executed at once; but Waller was reserved for a worse fate—to endure a prolonged life, branded with dishonour, and stained by the basest treachery. The leading lords were saved by their great influence and professed penitence.

The accidental battle and victory at Chalgrove proved of great interest to me. Two officers of rank had been sorely wounded and made prisoners; being unable to move, they had been granted parole by Prince Rupert, and this they basely broke. Essex, however, who was of a nobler nature, on remonstrance offered to liberate any two royalist prisoners in their place; and my brother Hugo was thus restored to me.
