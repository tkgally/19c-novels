When the troubles broke out, a legend of this transaction reached the ears of Hezekiah, whilst he was in our neighbourhood. He had traced that legend till it assumed the likelihood of a truth, and, finally, he had possessed himself of this document after the sack of our house: with the views he then held, it had promised to be of importance to him.

“All this,” he continued, looking at me with the same unmoved aspect, “I thought it expedient to declare in the young man’s presence, in order that he may hold to no vain hopes of becoming once more a dweller in the house of his fathers: it is desolate, and he is a beggar.
