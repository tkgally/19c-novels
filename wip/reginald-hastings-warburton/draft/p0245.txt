The very evening of our arrival, my brother and I went to visit Phœbe; Hugo proved the change that a year had wrought in his youthful sensations by a nervousness till then unknown to him. We found her in the apartment she had mentioned, in Merton College. When we entered, she was sitting in a window’s deep recess, and Lady d’Aubigny sat, or rather lay, upon a cushion at her feet. They formed a picturesque group, strongly defined by the light of lamps within, and a bright moon without, each making strong shadows, as their pale and red rays gleamed over cheeks, and hair, and gracefully draperied forms.
