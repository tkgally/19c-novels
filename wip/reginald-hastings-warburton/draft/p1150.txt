# Chapter X.

<verse>
Which, when the pensive Ladie saw from farre,
Great woe and sorrow did her soul assay,
As weening that the sad end of the warre,
And ’gan to highest God entirely pray.
</verse>
[[right]] <sc>Spenser’s “Faerie Queene.”</sc>

O<sc>n</sc> my return towards the town, I met Blount with half a score of my dismounted troopers; they had been searching for me all over the field in vain, and were now bending their steps in the direction whence they had heard Prince Rupert’s bugle.
