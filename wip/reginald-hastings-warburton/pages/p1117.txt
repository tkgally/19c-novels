It is, God wot! not with vanity, but with shame, that I record these reckless acts. A mere animal, if goaded with half my inward pain, might have done as much. Never shall I forget the self-reproach, the rebuke that I felt, when soon after, in the fight, or rather the pursuit, on Auborn Chace, the Prince rode up to me, and laying his hand upon my arm, exclaimed, “Enough—remember you are not a mere trooper.” It was perhaps the only time that Rupert ever attempted to restrain an officer in battle.

[[fn *]] A similar case is related in Campden, of Edward Stanley, at Lutzen, in 1580: he mounted on his enemy’s pike and won the town.
