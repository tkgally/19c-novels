Without waiting for a reply, the Prince motioned to my guards to advance, and in a few moments I stood before the King; without any trepidation, certainly, but I hope also without any approach to effrontery. In fact, I had little to hope, and fear has never been my failing. I was sorry for the King’s displeasure, but I had not yet learned to deplore the cause of it: I was well content to serve his Majesty as long as his standard stood, but I should not have much regretted a temporary suspension from that privilege.

The King regarded me for a moment with a countenance that was rather mournful than stern, and though my eyes were cast down, I could feel that his glance then wandered almost nervously away. After a few minutes’ awkward pause he broke silence thus:
