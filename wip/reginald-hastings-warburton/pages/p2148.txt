Coming opposite the appointed spot, he observed the barge waiting there for its precious freight, but he sped on, and close to London Bridge he found a galliot lying at anchor with her sails loose; he pulled along side of her gently, and explained, in a few words, that he was sent before by Master Crisp to see that all was ready. Then he took leave of the good Archer, who pulled away, and left him to his own resources.

He had not forgotten his seamanship, and he straightway set himself to examine the galliot with such an air of confidence that the skipper formed a very favourable opinion of his skill. He found her a long low vessel, such as was often employed to carry despatches, and for secret service—she drew but three feet of water, and was adapted for oars as well as sailing.
