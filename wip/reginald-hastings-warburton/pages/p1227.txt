Why, sir, when we were all assembled in the hall to hear him expound and pray, I thought him the mildest and resignedest Christian I ever beheld, and as for his eyes, I could see nothing but the whites of them, and that same half lost under his big eyebrows. But, save us! when I saw him next—(you see when the others had left the hall, after the expounding—which, indeed, I didn’t overmuch understand—one or two of us, that are in trust, were bid to wait for orders), when, our governor said something to this new Colonel about your being half-released from bondage, I looked for his answer, and his look a’ most made one stagger back. Why, I’d take my oath on’t (if swearing wasn’t unlawful for a Christian man,) that no ten men, no, and their wives to boot, ever put so much anger into a single look: and it wasn’t the anger only, but the strength that was in it, that made me pity our poor governor, and wonder he could stand it.
