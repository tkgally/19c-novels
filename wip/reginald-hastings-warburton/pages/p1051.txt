“Well,” said Hugo, “be he man or fiend, he once saved my life, and he has proved my friend this day; I scarcely think, therefore, that he would lend himself to my destruction.”

“Be not too sure,” said the old soldier, shaking his head and looking very grim. “He doesn’t move after the laws or customs of this world. I verily believe that if he thought it would advance the cause one dagger’s length, he would fling his mother from the top of the White Tower as pleasantly as I would crush this empty glass. Oh, if thou hadst heard him preach, as I have done, on the goodly text, ‘Cut off thy right hand and cast it from thee,’ it’s little thou couldst say that a head or so would stand in his way; no, not if it were that of anointed Majesty itself.”

“Why, you would make him out little better than a papistical Jesuit,” said Hugo, interested in spite of himself on his behalf.
