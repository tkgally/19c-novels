“As she spoke, her eyes filled with tears, and she turned them towards the sea, with a fond but unconscious gaze. A dark suspicion, a most desolate feeling then seized my heart; her love was not mine; she was lost to me, and if to me, then to herself! Heaven is my witness, that even in those moments of agony I felt more for her than for myself—for her stained truth, her lost honour, her deceitfulness. Oh! I loved her far too well for anger—for reproach; yet I knew not which way to turn—how to escape to solitude and silence, much less how to speak: it was surely Digby’s letter that she treasured!

“And as I stood, thus racked with torturing doubts, she still gazed calmly on the distant sea, forgetting my very presence in the visionary thought that she had conjured up and gloated on. After a fierce struggle with a selfish fiend, I seemed to conquer.
