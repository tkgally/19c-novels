For action and meditation are almost incompatible, and when we are abroad and free among the brave, the beautiful, the dangerous, among those who can injure us and those who can reward, we little think of recording what we have scarcely time to perform. Hence it is that all our descriptions seem so tame—our ruminations so insipid compared with what we fed upon at life’s high festival. How different is even the voice of wit or the touch of pathos when “coldly furnished forth” upon mere paper; and far, far more so the soul-thrilling thoughts that require all the assistance of passionate eye and utterance, to express themselves even to the quickened perceptions of those we love.
