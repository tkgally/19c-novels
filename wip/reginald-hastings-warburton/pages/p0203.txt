[[cont]]of the best accounts I ever heard of this memorable battle; from my brother Hugo to the King, every one had a different version of it. Digby indeed swore that Prince Rupert lost the battle, and Mr. Hyde was ready enough to show how it might have been won, and Sir Philip Warwick swore it <i>was</i> won. All I know is, that nothing—not even defeat—could have been more disastrous to England than this undecided battle. I verily believe that the Parliament, if then victorious, would have been forced by the country to give the King better terms, than now, after all their defeats they are disposed to offer. Our men fought only too well, and forgot the better part of valour—that discretion and presence of mind which alone can dignify and turn it to a successful issue.
