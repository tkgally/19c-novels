“Except the tongue,” observed Hugo, “and I would fain be informed by yours to what motive I am indebted for your visit.”

But the dwarf was in no hurry to reply: he was supremely happy, seated in a high-backed chair, with one beaker of wine down his throat, and another sparkling before him. He had cocked one little leg over the other, and was admiring its proportions, when Hugo repeated his question as courteously as before. Never yet was courtesy wasted or misplaced. It is the due of heaven-born man to man—the benevolence of manner; ennobling a benefit conferred, and removing all humiliation from a benefit received; it is the soft tongue that breaks the bone, repels insult with dignity, and gives protection, without patronage:—to the orator in the forum, the warrior in the fight, the lover in the bower, the noble in the hall, the citizen in the street—it is equally the test of the superior man.
