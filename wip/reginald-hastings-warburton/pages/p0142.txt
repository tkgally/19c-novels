And so—but whisht! by the powers, there’s more of ’em!” So saying, Bryan placed his ear to the ground, and in a few seconds we could all distinctly hear the tramp of armed men. We sprang to our saddles and rode away hastily, followed by Blount, and soon afterwards by Bryan, who had leisurely waited to recover and put in order his saddle-bags, which he now re-strapped upon the pony as it was cantering along.

Shortly afterwards, the first dawn revealed to us the fine old castle of Newark, where we soon arrived without further incident, and were heartily greeted by the loyal garrison.

I have dwelt too long on the trifling incidents of our march, but these were my first adventures, and made more impression on me than many a pitched battle has done since.
