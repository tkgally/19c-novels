And then, be it borne in remembrance, the man whom the Cavaliers call King is ever ready to forgive and receive the righteous and the patriot into a favour which enslaves the body and destroys the soul; while the Parliament never forgiveth, nor is there power given unto them to do so.”

I was induced thus far to remain a listener, in the hope that Sir Janus would, by his answer, vindicate himself from the dangerous imputations in which the Puritan had involved him; but when I heard him begin his reply with: “Well, I will go no farther in this business until I have spoken with Lord Essex—” I thought it high time to interrupt him. I therefore merely said to Digby: “This Sir Janus is well known to me: he is a kind, good man, but easily led.” Abruptly leaving the little chamber, I then entered the parlour, walked up through the various guests, and offered my hand to my old friend.
