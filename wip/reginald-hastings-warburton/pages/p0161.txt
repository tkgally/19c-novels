I had scarcely exchanged salutations with Sir Janus, when the Cavalier whom I have before mentioned as the proprietor of Satan, entered with half a score of troopers, and walking straight up to my two acquaintances, arrested them in the King’s name.

Sir Janus started, and looked at me suspiciously, as if I had betrayed him. The Puritan let fall his cloak from his face, and drawing himself up to his full height, exclaimed:

“Spies and eavesdroppers as ye are—gentlemen as ye call yourselves—have ye so little regard to the character of an ambassador and to your own, as to seize on a man bearing messages of peace and good-will into an enemy’s camp, though he be furnished with letters of safe-conduct?”
