# Chapter XV.

<verse>
You have stooped my neck under your injuries,
And sighed my English breath in foreign clouds,
Eating the bitter bread of banishment:
Whilst you have fed upon my seignories,
Disparked my parks, and felled my forest-wood;
Razed out my impress, leaving me no sign,
Save men’s opinions, and my living blood,
To shew the world I am a gentleman.
</verse>

How vainly we attempt to mete out our lives by spaces of time—hours, weeks, days—as measured by the clock. In the soul’s life, a few minutes may do the work of years, whilst a lapse of years may pass us by unnoticed.
