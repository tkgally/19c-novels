Phœbe said all this, with something of her old smile upon her lips, although a tear in her anxious eyes told Hugo how true was the allegory. What his reply was, may be imagined, but Phœbe, as soon as she recovered from its consequent discomposure, resumed, more thoughtfully than before.

“Dear Hugo, it cannot be; my father is old and his wishes are sacred to me; he is ill and that makes them the more solemn. I have not Zillah’s firmness or lofty character, but I, have a wayward little will of my own, which, when it is by chance in a right direction, is very difficult to shake.
