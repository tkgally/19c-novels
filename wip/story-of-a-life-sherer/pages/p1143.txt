It was a Friday, I remember, a day on which the women usually go abroad, and walk among the tombs, and deck the graves of those they mourn over with flowers.

In veiled groupes, or in couples, you see them on such occasions, walking unnoticed;—their shrouded forms all carefully concealed. It was also a festival day in the city, and all the Mahometans in my service were gone there. I was walking in my garden alone, and the gate stood open. With as quick a step as their cumbrous garments would allow, two female figures glided in at the gate, and passed on swiftly to the house.

I was startled, and taken captive at the prospect of adventure, whatever it might be. As I came down the garden walk, I closed, and made fast the gate, and followed them into the hall.
