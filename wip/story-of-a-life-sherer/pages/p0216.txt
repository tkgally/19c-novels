If the Senhor will but just step up into St. Mark’s he will hear the high mass, and I will go up with him, or wait for him on the quay;—my comrade will stay with the gondola.”

“Thanks, friend, I will go, but alone: but tell me what is that little high screened bridge between the walls of the Doge’s palace, and those of the fine building next to it?”

“Oh, that,—it is the Bridge of Sighs.”

“The what?”

“The Bridge of Sighs.”

“What! are there any sighs in Venice?”

“Aye, Senhor, and tears too;” and then he sung again his merry air, and broke off to bid me look at a passing bark, where a fat monk was shaking with laughter at a clown and a harlequin, who were playing off their tricks and antics in the same boat.

“But the bridge, my friend; why do they call it the Bridge of Sighs?”
