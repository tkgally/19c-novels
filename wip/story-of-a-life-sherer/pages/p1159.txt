[[cont]]fair young thing—soon, very soon. Two years we lived together. She bore me a beauteous boy: it was not that she merely nursed it; it grew on her as the bud upon the parent rose-stalk. I loved it, as though it had been a little winged cherub sent on earth to live with me and sooth me; I did not love its mother less; nay, more—but a new feeling was planted in my heart—parental love. Long years of woe, of trials, and of tears, had softened the hard soil, and the plant sprung up, in size and strength like the enchanter’s tree. Fair little silken curls fell over its young eyes of blue. It stammered—toddled—dealt its pretty blows, playful and laughing ever. It lived a life caressed, and we caressing. There was something very like happiness in this; but I had other thoughts when these innocent beings lay sleeping. Memory was to me as the beak of the fabled vulture, and in the still silence of the night her voice was sounding in my ear like a raven croak—

<verse>
“Deep written in my heart, with iron pen,
That bliss may not abide in state of mortal men.”
</verse>
