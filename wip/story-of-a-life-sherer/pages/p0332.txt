Cheerful I rose, and strong—smiled my refusal out; yet spoke it resolute. Three times that day the slave-whip drew my blood; twice I fell faint beneath the heavy burden of a weighty stone; but my heart was light, and presages of happiness played round my wakened fancy. Yes, I was now a sufferer, in some degree, for conscience sake. The tempter had been with me, and been rebuked. I might think again of home, and of pure things—of Edward in his grave; of Agatha the shade; of Maria Cecil, a living blessing wherever she dwelt. Thus, for many months, my life rolled by, and in patience I possessed my soul.
