I broke bread, and drank cup after cup of wine, in nervous haste, and thought aloud, and talked comfort to myself, and was glad to feel myself in Rome, and took my lamp, and went to my couch, and put aside the heavy damask curtains, and laid me down, and slept profoundly and long.

“I am in Rome,” was my first thought when I awoke,—“My Agatha—my phantom of delight has fled me, is gone down with her beauty to the grave; how perishable are these bright things with breath! The morrow’s accident kills the young bud born only of the hope of yesterday.
