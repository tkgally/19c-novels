“Agatha,” said the Prince, “you quite neglect your harp.”

“I have, lately,” she replied, as she rose and moved slowly towards it. I saw her eyes raised upwards, and marked that chill shuddering of the frame which tells of settled sorrow. I felt low, and I dared not ask for my favorite air. Two or more she gave in a style, for her, of cold indifference and tameness; at last, preluding in low, sad, wandering tones, as if she were playing a something never regularly composed, she sung these words:—

<verse>
“It never dies—a broken love,
For its nest is a broken heart,
It lives, it plains, a lonely dove,
Till the soul and the body part.
O sweet is the coo of a lonely dove,
O sweet is the grief of a broken love.”
</verse>
