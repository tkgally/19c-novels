“Not far from the tree under which Mirza was seated, he observed a small shrine, sacred, as he knew, from its symbol, to the god Siva the Destroyer; and he saw coming towards it, through the small grove behind, three females. Mirza was a man who venerated all religion, who was better pleased to see a human being prostrate in humility before a stone or a lump of sun-dried mud, than walking, lofty in his pride, and with a haughty glance, as of defiance looking up to the starry sky, and laughing at the unseen God—so he moved to the other side of the tree that he might not interfere with or disturb their devotion; but, curious to see the manner of their worship, he kept his eyes fixed on the shrine and on the approaching groupe.
