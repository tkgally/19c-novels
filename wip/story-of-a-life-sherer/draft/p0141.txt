Every thing that could reassure; every thing that could cheer, was acceptable. He had met me with a smile on my face, and therefore had asked me to dinner. I well remember the party. The acknowledged wit of it; the man, who filled the right-hand seat at the top of the table, who was helped to the very choicest morceau of the “Dindon farcie aux truffes,” and had the virgin bumper of every bottle of champagne, was a Monsieur Beauregard, a French gentleman nearer sixty than fifty years of age; a hungry anatomy of a man with thin sallow cheeks, a nose of caricature aquiline, darkly begrimed with snuff, and adorned with that brown, pendent secretion, so abominably offensive to the eye.
