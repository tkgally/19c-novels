The Prince and Agatha were to dine with the Count after the ceremony; and, in the evening, we were all to attend the Opera together. With so much actual and so much prospective enjoyment I was highly excited. I could not sit down. I washed, and changed my dress, walking about my chamber all the time. I sipped my chocolate in the like restless manner; had a smile for the merchant’s daughter, and forgot, of a truth, that any body was to be or could be miserable that day, till the sound of the Count’s voice and that of the benevolent monk, in fervent prayer, coming from the Oratory near, checked the vain flow of my spirits; and, as I heard them put up a solemn petition for the poor wretches who were that day innocently to suffer, I felt reproved for my levity, and my heart beat a sincere amen.
