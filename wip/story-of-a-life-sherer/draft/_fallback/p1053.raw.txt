I wandered down narrow streets; among dead _ bodies and dogs, who snarled at me as, with paws ‘and teeth, they tore the flesh from human bones; and the glaring eyes of cannibal concealment looked out on me from sitters-by cloked, and with abony gauntness of visage just shown above —on, on I went regardless; with a heart emptied of every hope. I did not feel as if I could live; ——I gained the shore of the sea—blue it looked, and beautiful —I could not bear its smile—I turned away—I wandered to Vesuvius, and reached its ashen side, and lay me down breasting that black, and barren bed. Till the night came I lay there moaning—and then I climbed to the summit,- heedless of the path—careless if I perished, and I gained the little plain above, over ground, perhaps, before untrodden.
