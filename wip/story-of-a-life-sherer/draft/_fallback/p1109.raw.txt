Malek, too, was of all the Mamalukes the mildest, when by your side he sat in sweet, ob- ~ serving silence. He would read for me the Arab scroll, and tune the kitar, and sing, in strains new, wildly new, the love songs of the desert. 'To Malek, moreover, I was an object _ of deep and lively interest; for, as a youth he had been, as he called it, in the Christians’ land. ;

Sometimes when I was busily engaged with books and papers, he would come and sit opposite me, and gaze; and call upon me to forgive him.

s¢ Let me,” he would say, ‘let me only look at you. I trouble you, but it makes me happy, so happy. I can think better about the Christians’ land. Sometimes I wish that I had never seen it. I had heard that it was a land of gloom, and clouds. It wasnot so.
