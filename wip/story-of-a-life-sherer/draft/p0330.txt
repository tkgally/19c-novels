[[cont]]few perhaps would have borne a better part; but here I felt, although in the extremity of wretchedness, the truth of that mighty master’s verse:—

<verse>
“The weariest, and most loathed worldly life,
That age, ache, penury, and imprisonment
Can lay on nature, is a paradise
To what we fear of death.”
</verse>

and thus I bore with my chains and stripes.

It chanced one morning, as the Dey rode past us, that he was struck by my form, and features; he sent to me an officer, and I was called, all soiled, and dripping from my muddy labours, and told that if I would only assume the turban, and turn Mahometan, I should be received into the Turkish guard, and might make my fortune. “No,” I said, “I was born, and would die a Christian.” They bade me think of it, and answer on the morrow; and again, that night, I went home to the locked and crowded khan, the filthy straw, the scanty black bread; the noisome stenches, and the constant
