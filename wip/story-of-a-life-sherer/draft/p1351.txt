When, at last, I thought it dead, and was preparing for my foul meal, its mouth opened, and its heart beat; at length it lay quite still. I gashed about it then, and lay me down, and sucked the blood, and hacked the flesh, and fed upon it with fierce haste,—what could the wolf or tiger more? What had the Brahmin said to see the Christian feeding thus? and yet what of this?—why, there are in our world cannibals. Heavens! what a thing is man,—man, <i>the animal</i> alone! nothing is more abject; but then man, the rational <i>living soul</i>, how mighty, how majestic! Day after day, for a long, long time, of which I kept no count, I fed thus upon turtle, and the half-eaten carcases of my victims allured the ravenous sea birds in their flight, and sometimes we fed together.
