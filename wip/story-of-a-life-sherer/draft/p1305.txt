I was now sent for by my father home, that I might be present at a great family-festival: my brother was about to be married: I returned. Short as the distance is, I had never once been at Ferrara, since I left it for the University, neither had my father nor my brother once visited Padua, to see the devoted young Ambrogio. I had thought this unkind, and now I returned with an ill grace, and a very reluctant feeling; moreover, I felt sad at the idea that I was about to be present at a marriage-ceremony, a scene of smiles, and songs, and scattered flowers. My ordinary cheerfulness, such as I indulged in among my fellow-students, forsook me all at once. It seemed as if a veil were suddenly withdrawn, and that, for the first time, the realities of my future life lay before me clearly seen in all their bleak and barren desolation.
