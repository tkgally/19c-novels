She was the daughter of an English secretary, well-connected in Ireland, where, indeed, she had herself been educated as a child. That he was of a catholic, she of a protestant family, proved no bar to their union; they were young, loved, overruled or disregarded all scruples of their friends, and married.

“My mother’s friends, after a while, neglected her; and my father, who was in some things a mere man of the world, and bent entirely on the pursuit of his own ambitious schemes, began to tire of the restraining presence of a wife, and, taking her to Ireland, fixed her down in the most painful sort of widowhood in the house where I, the third and only surviving child, was born.
