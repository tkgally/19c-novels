The curtain rose: nothing could be more joyously brilliant than the music. The opera was all of serenading, and stolen meetings, and duped guardians, and cheerful love-making. The <i>prima donna</i> was a sparkling stage beauty, practising every lure that innocence does not know. There was a ballet too after it; all arm-entwining and graceful bendings; and the sway, the pretty sway of that fly-away-follow-me sort of tripping; and the amorous glances and the close embrace; and garlands and shawls held up by snowy arms in fanciful festoons; and lively airs throughout the whole action of the ballet.
