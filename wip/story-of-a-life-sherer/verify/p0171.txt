For weeks I neither saw nor spoke to any of my acquaintance. Once only I admitted Father Antonio, whose efforts to rouse me were all vain. The restorer of my mind’s tone was a weak instrument—a little child, just at that age when the stammer is intelligible, and the totter fearless. It tapped with its little hand at my chamber door, and when opened for its weak short-reached little arm, ran in and round my table, to look for something to amuse its young wondering mind; then clasped my knees, and looked up in my face, with a playful beseeching; and pouted its little lip, to offer the rewarding kiss. Fondly I did kiss, fondly press it to my bosom; and, from that hour, I scarce suffered the little creature to leave me while I remained at Cintra. I tossed it in my arms, horsed it on my knee, leaned down my cheek to its pretty pattings of mock punishment, sung to it, laughed for it, loved it.
