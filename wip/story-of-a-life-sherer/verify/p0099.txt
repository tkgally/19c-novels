I remember, too, that whenever I entreated her to favour me with an air, or music, her selections were designedly disappointing; they were of a light and cheerful, or of a cold and scientific character. Once only as I looked a deep-felt reproach upon her, she sung, in sorrowing tones, a something to calm my diseased mind; and her eyes had the soft expression of one who was saddened, pityingly saddened, to see the strong impression that she made.

Heavens! What I suffered through that short period of my first love—for love it was, though not, alas! that fond, requited feeling—that pure leaping of one young heart to its virgin fellow.

It was a deep, passionate admiration—a panting of the sick heart—a burning of the fevered brain. Her image was ever present. My walks, my lyings down, my days, my nights, my dreams were full of her, were haunted by her form.
