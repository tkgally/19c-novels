[[cont]]soundly at nights upon the bare earth; and awoke light, refreshed, and grateful. On the evening of the sixth day I sat me down at the foot of that tall obelisk which stands before the noble church of St. John Lateran; and I took from my wallet the last morsel of my bread.

“Give me,” said a pale and perishing wretch, as he crawled forward at the sight; “give me, I pray you, a mouthful only of that bread. For two days I have not tasted food, and I am dying. They drive me from the church doors, and the convent gates; and the very beggars will not share with me, because I am of Venice.”

“And you ask succour of me! Do you know me?”

His eyes dilated; his lean, and melancholy jaws stood agape; his flesh trembled; he could not speak.—It was Giacomo Brunelli, my faithless servant, who kneeled before me in convicted guilt and dying terror.

I threw him down the bread; “Eat, miserable being—eat, and strengthen yourself to tell out to me the tale of your crimes. Eat.—I will go pray.”
