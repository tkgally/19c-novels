I shuddered; there was something so awful in his melancholy tone of firmly persuaded woe. I had felt a kind of cheerful, gay contempt for the sentiments of the old Frenchman. I had felt a deep horror when this young German enthusiast had dared arraign the goodness, and dispute the power of the Creator. But when he made me this deep despairing confidence, I felt penetrated with sorrow.—Certainly he was deranged. I was sure of this; but yet I had not come off an unwounded listener; something of fear mingled with my incredulity. I looked up at the stars, and I wrapped my vest over my chilly bosom. I thought of the wild tempest, and the yawning waves—of the rocking earth, the falling city, and the perishing thousands—twice rescued—twice preserved.—Was I a child of Fate?—’Twas strange—very strange.
