[[cont]]was a gala at the court, and just as I had gained the middle of the city, the bursting clangor of a martial band broke grandly on the silence of a gathered, whispering, expectant crowd: the guards came marching forth, and following behind a long train of young nobles of the city. I was in a student’s garb, and mounted on a long-eared heavy mule, and the crowd struck it, and pressed us both against the wall, to let the <i>cortège</i> pass. Such a sight was one to make a young heart leap, and play in the bosom lightly, but far different were my feelings,—gall was in them. I looked steadily at the noble youths, and among them I saw and recognised my brother:—he was much altered, but I knew him instantly; as a man he was still silken; his garb was of the richest; his hair hung about him not with a manly wave, but in little chamber ringlets.
