“News! is Messiah come? is there a gathering of our people?”

“No, my dear Deborah, but we are going up towards Jerusalem, that we may live and die there, and leave it no more.”

“My blessings on you, Benjamin; you will then take my bones in a chest, and lay them under the shadow of the wall of Rama.”

“Do you think you can bear the journey?”

“Can dry bones feel, my son? ten, twenty years, a hundred years, a watch; a little watch of the night, and then you will carry up my bones.”

“She does not heed,” said Rachel, her large eyes dim with pity. “She does not understand, father,” and then again she leaned down her face of beauty, and kissed that withered cheek, and whispered that dull ear.

“Mother, we are going a journey, a long journey over the desert.”
