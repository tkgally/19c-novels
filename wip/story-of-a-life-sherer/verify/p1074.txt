It was in vain I related the hardships of my captivity in Algiers, and the scenes I had witnessed there. He denied that the Moor of Africa resembled the Mahometan of Asia Minor, and contended that their treatment of the captive was only imitated from the cruel example of Genoa and Venice. He appealed to me, however, as to many customs even there; and asked, if, to the reflective and contemplative life of a thinking being, the silence and seclusion of the haram and the garden were not congenial. Nothing was more mistaken, than supposing that all the hours of seclusion in the haram were devoted to idleness and dalliance. It was in such sacred retreats that the Arabian sages, the glory of Cordova and Grenada, had penned their noblest works.
