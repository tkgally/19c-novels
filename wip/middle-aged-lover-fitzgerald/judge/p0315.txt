“That looks rather like it,” said Mrs. Gardiner. “None of the people in this place have sense enough to put such rhymes together.”

“Still, he wouldn’t describe himself in that way; as ‘Old Doughty,’ and all that——”

“It matters little,” said Corinna. “All I ask is this, and I implore it of you. Do not let me be dragged through the mire in this way—be offered to the best bidder to be rejected contemptuously any more. I cannot bear it. It must not be. If you love your daughter, respect her, or wish her to be respected, you will leave me out of these wretched, restless plans. It is contemptible, unworthy, to be using your child as a stepping-stone!”
