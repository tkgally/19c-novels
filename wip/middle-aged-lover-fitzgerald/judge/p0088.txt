“Mud-cheap, mud-cheap, sir,” he said contemptuously to the imaginary listener who attended him in such discussions. “But I’m not going to do grinders’ work now. It’s high time to stop all that. I’ve let myself down too much. No, no! that must come to an end. I’ve given myself and my ‘method’ mud-cheap, mud-cheap, sir!” This was a favourite expression of the music-master’s.

And in truth things were promising well for the family. Various proprietors of the chimneys and the vast mills of brick were driven, by the overpowering importunities of Bill Gardiner, to engage so famous a professor for their children, and very soon he was assuming the air of an overworked cabinet minister, striving to make his engagements compatible with each other.
