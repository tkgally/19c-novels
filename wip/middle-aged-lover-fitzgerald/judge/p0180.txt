“Doughty seems to have taken a fancy to Miss Corinna; but you musn’t reckon on his marrying. She ought not to give up the old love, who has given up so much for her.”

“Oh, I don’t know about that,” said Mr. Nagle, loftily. “It would be a very poor thing for a girl of her talents and prospects. The young fellow has quarrelled with his parents. That’s not the sort of thing that will do for us, you know.”

“Why, God bless me, man alive,” said Will, in a burst of warmth, “you are not going to play shilly-shally with your girl’s affections in this style! You are not going to fly her like a hawk, at every sort of game!”
