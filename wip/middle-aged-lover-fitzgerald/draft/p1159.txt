Indeed, as it is,” continued Mrs. Gardiner, unable to repress her natural inclination to give a thrust to any fellow-creature when she could, “it rather surprises me that she should stand aloof in this remarkable way, as if she were afraid of catching the plague. To some people it would seem ungracious; only we, of course, know her better.”

“You do not,” said he quietly, “and some people—most people, indeed—cannot understand such things. However, if she could only be brought to pay one little visit to a poor shattered invalid, out of charity, even as a sister of mercy, which she is, I shall be for ever grateful.”
