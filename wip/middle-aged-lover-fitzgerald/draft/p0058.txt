This “miserable bog,” as he was fond of calling it, did not even afford subsistence to an organist, as the population were chiefly dissenters, and relied on their own unaided throats. As for Corinna, her charms of person and voice were equally thrown away. There was no one, indeed, to admire. Corinna, and a venerable “Broadwood” of angular build, and of buzzing interior sounds—that in forte passages rose into a jangle—were the only treasures and two most valuable pieces of property possessed by the family. “The Broadwood,” and “my girl Corinna,” were the words oftenest in Mr. Nagle’s mouth.
