“The Sawpits?” said Will Gardiner.

“Yes, you know what I mean—where they grind old fogey music—rasp up and rasp down—all grave as undertakers, working as if they had saws and planes in their hands.” Then confidentially, “There’s neither tune nor money in it, sir—no money in it.”

“Oh, indeed,” said Mr. Gardiner, greatly impressed.

“As for your friend Doughty, he is, no doubt, a creditable amateur. But, my dear sir, all the amateurs in the empire, boiled down and melted together, wouldn’t be equal to one of the trade. It’s all poor, my dear sir—wants the real beef.”

“I see,” said Will; “there’s no money in them.”
