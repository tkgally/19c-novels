“Well, my good sir, what is it now? I am in a great hurry.”

Old Doughty’s eyes were fixed with a curious, anxious look on the pair; an almost angry look. He walked over to Corinna.

“Have you thought over my little plan? I have been dreaming of it ever since. It will be a great opportunity,” he said, “and you will carry them all by storm. There is the song—I have transposed it. It will suit your voice to perfection.”

Corinna was embarrassed and distressed. Matters had entirely changed. She had no object now in seeking to wring her lover’s heart, or to make an exhibition on the platform.

“I am afraid, Mr. Doughty,” she said, gently, “we must give that little plan up. Papa has settled everything, and will not have his programme altered.”
