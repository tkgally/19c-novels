The party was now breaking up for supper down-stairs. The musicians have generally to escort each other, the languishing baritone offering his arm to the stout soprano. The young lord, who sang and played the violin in the ranks of “the Macallum Minstrels,” offered his arm to take Corinna, and was not without disappointed competitors. He told her he was enchanted, and that everybody was enchanted, and that she must really sing at the next concert of the Macallum Minstrels. “She was just the thing for them,” he added, “clinching” his compliments.

They had reached the hall, and were turning into the supper-room, when a servant came forward with one of those brick-coloured envelopes which always cause a flutter, and are opened with eagerness, no matter how familiar we may be with their reception.
