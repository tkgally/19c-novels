The price of this rather hazy “course,” including singing, playing, dancing, and, we may presume, the musical glasses, if the parents required it, and for the foreign languages, organ-playing, &c., was certainly reasonable—some five “geeneys” a year. Then “associates,” who did not desire to study under Mr. Nagle, could be affiliated for the modest sum of “a geeney” a year, for which they were admitted to all the concerts, to af the rehearsals, to al the Harmonic Matinées, to al the Soirées Musicales and tuneful “Aprés-midis,” and to the occasional dances—certainly a most generous and handsome “geeney’s” worth.
