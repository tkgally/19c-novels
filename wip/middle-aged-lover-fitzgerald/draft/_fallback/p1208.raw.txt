She had bitter suspicions of Lady Duke, and believed that her “game” was to secure everything for herself, taking possession of the unhappy Doughty, and stripping him of everything, whether he were dead or alive, sane or mad. Were they not as nearly related to the patient as were the Dukes, and did not Mr. Gardiner’s position as a barrister and judge, seem to mark him out as the suitable person to have charge of the matter?

The husband and wife had many conferences on this head, he hanging back with a not unnatural scrupulousness, the lady urging him forward with arguments and taunts, somewhat after the model of those used by Lady Macbeth on a more
