CHAPTER X.

LADY DUKE’S MOVE.

YEA)

HE music-master’s daughter, so long the heroine of Brickford, was no mere romantic dreamer,

dwelling on her own sufferings with a soft

complacency and satisfaction, but was determined to carry out the scheme she had announced. There was in her mind, too,

a lofty purpose of self-chastisement—a

calm resolve to suffer the fully stern penalty

for the light trifling, the vain, careless encouragement by which she had brought

suffering on two persons who loved her.
