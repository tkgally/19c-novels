“But surely,” said the reverend gentleman, with some hesitation, “as his clergyman, he being one of my own flock, really I have some right to look after his spiritual state. If his situation is so critical, it is a duty, you know——”

“Utterly impossible,” said the doctor. “I veto it entirely. I shall let no one see him for the present, except, of course, Mr. Nagle, who has been here from the beginning. I take it all on myself, as his medical adviser.”

“There,” said Will Gardiner, a little maliciously, “you can take back your calf’s-foot jelly, and give it to one of the children.”

“You need not direct us as to that,” said his brother, tartly; “but I begin to understand what is going on here. I assure you no trickery will do. Doughty is as much our relation as yours.”
