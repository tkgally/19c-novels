He would sit at the instrument, allowing his fingers to stray away into mournful harmonies, while he dwelt on the suddenness, the <i>terrible</i> suddenness of the illness. “A man in the prime of life, a fine nature, and such a friend! I am not equal to this sort of thing: it has given me a shock: quite upset me,” &c. He was, however, “equal” to booking the interview as a formal lesson, to be duly charged for. At the end of the jeremiad he looked at his watch. “Bless me, I must hurry off. We’ll just try through the ‘Swan’ once.”

Mr. William Gardiner, who had been absent from Brickford, on the day after Mr. Doughty’s return, met Doctor Spooner in the street in the afternoon. From him he learned the dangerous nature of the malady. and hurried home to Mrs. Gardiner with the news. The lady, already in possession of the story, was much excited.
