“I’ll take my affidavit to it,” said Mr. Nagle, with importance.

“Oh! if so, then you will not be likely to let the recollection slip out of your mind. It really shows great sagacity in you to have noticed what might have properly occurred only to a physician. Good-bye.”

Rather soothed at this compliment, Mr. Nagle went his way. The sense of being the depository of such valuable information, and the longing to find people to whom he could impart his griefs, his sense of cruel desertion, and a wish to prove that he was still in communication with his late patron,
