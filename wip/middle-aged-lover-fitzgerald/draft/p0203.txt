Mr. Nagle was enchanted. He would help in <i>any</i> way that her ladyship thought necessary.

“Of course your daughter is out of the question—I would not ask her for the world, unless, indeed, Alfred can persuade her.”

“Don’t let your ladyship be disturbed,” said Mr. Nagle, soothingly, who had latterly assumed quite a free and familiar manner that contrasted oddly with his former obsequiousness. “She is a child, the merest child. Here, come here, Corinna——”

“Hush,” said Mr. Doughty, warmly, “you surely would not do such a thing. You cannot force a high spirit to undergo such a degradation.”

Alfred Duke and Corinna had now come over.
