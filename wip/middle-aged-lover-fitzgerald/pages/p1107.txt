“Never, never,” said Mr. Nagle, returning this testimonial of affection with delight. “You may depend on me.” He gave a triumphant glance at the physician, who, however, responded cordially.

“Mr. Nagle is indispensable to us,” he said, “and has been most assiduous in watching over you through the crisis. But you are not to talk too much. By-and-by you can do that.”

And he gently withdrew Mr. Nagle from the room.

“Now you understand me,” he said to him outside, “though you put a wrong construction on the step I was obliged to take yesterday. We must go quietly, and not too fast. The grand point is to protect him from the well-meant irruptions of visitors and others.”
