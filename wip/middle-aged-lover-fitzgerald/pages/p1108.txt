“I quite understand you,” said Mr. Nagle, with renewed fervour. “The relations are so pushing, there is no keeping them back. They seem to have no decency. They must be kept out at all hazards. What would you say to this,—we might divide the duty,—take it in watches? I would keep guard when you have to go away; you would relieve me, and so on.”

“Oh, that’s too serious a way of taking it,” said the other, coldly. “We don’t want to <i>keep guard</i>, exactly. People would naturally think we had designs of some sort. No, I’ll take care that he shall not be disturbed; and in my absence I can depend on my sister. But as I said, the effect of your name and presence occasionally—and I need not tell you why—will be very valuable.”
