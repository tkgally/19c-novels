This would have been a cruel profanation: for the legend ran that the great Braham had once carelessly strummed upon its keys—now grown as yellow as an old set of teeth—the first rude outlines of “’Twas in Trafalgar’s Bay!”—since become immortal. It was scarcely wonderful, then, that this relic should be regarded with veneration, or that like “my daughter and my ducats,” which the Jew was so concerned about, Mr. Nagle should have always coupled his daughter and his piano.

Mr. Nagle boasted of being connected with a gentlemanly family, though after a hazy fashion.
