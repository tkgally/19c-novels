Mr. Doughty found himself somehow placed beside Corinna, but that young lady, to her father’s annoyance, was cold and reserved. All, however, were in good spirits, save Mr. Duke, whose great sacrifice, defiance of his family, etc.—which seemed to him the grandest, noblest, and most heroic act in the world—appeared now to be accepted tamely, and quite as a thing of course. Indeed, there was room for no one else but the centre figure. Every one felt more or less under a sense of constraint in the presence of the shy and modest person who was yet in possession of such a sum as two hundred and fifty thousand pounds.
