And the enchanting Corinna herself, how was she regarded by the fickle swain whom she had enslaved? Had the general <i>désillusionnement</i> affected her? No one could have guessed from her manner or bearing that she noted any change in her admirer. Her own family could only see that she had the same calm, impassive, impenetrable air, behind which there was no piercing; that she seemed to be calmly awaiting some issue, whatever that might be, whenever it was to arrive. That issue was now not very far off.

It had come to a certain Monday morning, when Mr. Nagle, paying his accustomed visit to his opulent friend, found him a little unwell. He had a bad cold—had not slept very well during the night. Instantly the visitor was in a state of bustle and agitation.
