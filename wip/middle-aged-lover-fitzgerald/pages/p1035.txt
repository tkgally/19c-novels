No serious proposal had, indeed, been offered, no formal declaration of the gentleman’s love had been made. Still he had “gone far enough,” as it is called—quite far enough to make him feel guilty. He was something of a gentleman, too, and he felt that if it once came to explanations so delicate, all would be over, and he must declare himself plainly. He did not relish the idea of the blood of all the Dukes commingling with that of “a common music-master;” he turned from the notion of Braham Nagle as a father-in-law with horror and disgust, and had Corinna been a real angel instead of merely enjoying the complimentary title, he would have shrunk from so terrible a contamination.
