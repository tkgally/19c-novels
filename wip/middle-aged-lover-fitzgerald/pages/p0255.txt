“It may seem odd to you, I know, after the way in which I have behaved; but one of these days, perhaps, I may tell you the whole.”

Mr. Nagle always felt that when his daughter spoke in this tone, it was to be an ultimatum. He was inexpressibly disappointed, “put out,” and bewildered. Such a blow when he thought everything was settled so nicely, and going on “so swimmingly.” He was, however, of a very buoyant temper, and before the day was over would be reconciled to the idea. He was now on such free and familiar terms with his “opulent friend,” as he called him, that he was entitled to sound him on this mysterious subject.
