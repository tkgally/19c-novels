Braham’s scholar began, as some considered it, to give himself airs. He positively declared he could not go through the drudgery of dealing with raw, untrained organs, and such he forthwith bade go to Dobson, or Jones, or “some local stonebreaker,” as he styled the inferior members of his profession. “I assure you,” he would say, good-naturedly, “Dobson is a very good, honest, hard-working fellow, with a wife and six children”—as though they formed part of his musical gifts—“and you couldn’t do better than go to him.
