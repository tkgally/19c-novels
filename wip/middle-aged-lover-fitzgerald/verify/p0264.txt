Meantime, the little ant-hill bustle and intrigues of Brickford began to go forward in their ordinary course. Various parties had begun to congratulate themselves on the discovery of the state of affairs between Corinna and young Duke, which left Mr. Doughty, as it were, free, and a fair object of competition for others. Mr. Gardiner, the clergyman, with his lady, had long resented the matter of course style in which their claims on this rich relation’s good offices had been put aside by more pushing persons, and the reverend gentleman had been stirred up by his lady “to make some exertions for his family.”
