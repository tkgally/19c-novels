One of her daughters having thus taken away a modest portion, her son being expensive in his tastes, her own tastes and habits costly, she was but ill-furnished to encounter difficulties; and already old debts and coming obligations were pressing on her. The danger, therefore, of her son’s marrying “a low girl without a shilling,” filled her with a natural consternation, and with this, and other difficulties on her hands, it was not surprising that she should think of coming to Brickford to watch over her offspring, and cultivate the acquaintance of her newly-enriched relative. This intriguing lady had, in fact, laid out already a daring and comprehensive design.
