This was brutal, and Mr. Nagle winced.

“I am <i>not</i> sent to the right-about, as you call it. The real danger is that the man may not recover, and they may be concocting wills, and deeds, and all sorts of knavery.”

“It won’t stand,” said Will, excitedly. “I tell you it shan’t stand. If I was to spend every halfpenny I have on earth I’ll upset it. The relations shall get up a fund, and work heaven and earth. No court would tolerate such an iniquitous plot. But do you know, Nagle, in any case things look rather blue for you, my boy. And after all your trouble, too!”

“Yes,” said the other, “my practice ruined—my professional emoluments dwindling away——”

“Dwindling!” said Mrs. Gardiner, in good-natured surprise. “Why, every one in Brickford believes that you are coining.”
