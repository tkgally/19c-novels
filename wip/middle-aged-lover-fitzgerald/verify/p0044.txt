Other songs were called for. Some, notably William Gardiner, wished for more of the old English ballads; Mr. Alfred Duke pressed for an Irish melody. Corinna owned that she thought she was more familiar with that department, and Alfred Duke was heard going round the room:—

“Miss Nagle is going to sing, ‘When through Life once blest we roam.’ Such a treat! Her father says it is her grandest performance. Now, <i>pray</i>, let every one keep silent, because all the effect depends on the words.”
