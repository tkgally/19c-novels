You think everybody in the world is innocent. You’ll never do anything for your daughter or your family. I wish you’d find some way of getting money for us.”

Will Gardiner grew gloomy and disturbed. “That Nagle,” he said, “does think that Old Doughty and all his money belongs to him, as his private property. He <i>has</i> some extraordinary influence over him—of that there’s no mistake.”

“And what business has he in the house, pray, at all? A mere adventurer that has dropped down here from the skies! What unparalleled impudence to thrust himself in between a sick person and his blood relations!”
