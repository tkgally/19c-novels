Mr. Nagle was in his greatest vein, and recurred, now plaintively, now triumphantly, to the great master who had formed his voice. He told new stories of Grimani, and explained that “it was hopeless now to look for style in the world,” and that the “art of oiling the voice” was now confined to those few who had the tradition. “A few of us,” he said, “can do it, but it will soon be utterly lost.”

Doctor Spooner asked innocently: “And can you really oil the human voice?”
