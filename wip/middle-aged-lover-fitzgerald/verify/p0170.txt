“Just look there, Nagle. There’s the lucky man of the day! Why Crœsus himself will be a pauper to him.”

Mr. Nagle started, and looked round a little wildly. Crœsus! What? How?

“He has a telegram in his pocket at this moment, worth—how much would you suppose? What would you say to two hundred and fifty thousand pounds?”

“Gracious Heavens above us!” cried Mr. Nagle, as naturally as though he had heard the house was on fire.

Will Gardiner was right. The great news was out, and it was known just before the concert began: an old club friend, who had made money in India, and who had lived a bachelor life like himself, was dead, and by his will had made Old Doughty his heir to some vast amount!
