“He did not,” said Arthur; “he took the timber and oars from Dick just after they washed in, and carried them to the watch-house.”

“My father said he saw your father cutting it up for firewood,” persisted Peter.

“Then your father told a lie,” said Arthur hotly.

“And your father is a thief,” retorted Peter.

“I’ll hit you if you say that again.”

“Your father is a thief,” repeated Peter deliberately.

Arthur’s blood boiled at this aspersion upon his father, whom he looked upon as a personification of bravery and goodness, and without considering Peter’s extra four inches of stature, he struck him a blow in the breast with all his might.
