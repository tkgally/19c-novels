Jenifer had raised various objections when Esther proposed that she too should go a-fishing with Mark, but Esther had made up her mind to go as soon as she heard Jenifer asking Miss Jink for leave, and, therefore, overruled them all. Esther managed to leave her work earlier than Jenifer; “her tooth,” she said, “was so painful, that she could scarcely hold up her head; might she go home?”

“By all means,” said the good natured mistress, “and I hope you’ll be better by the morning.”

Mark had planned to take no one with him on this expedition but Jenifer, and he determined to get rid of Esther by fair means or foul.

“Well, Mark,” said Esther, “I think you might have asked me to go, but as you didn’t, I’ve asked myself.”
