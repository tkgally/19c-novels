He had always been a diligent reader, and, in spite of the smallness of the home library, had acquired a fair stock of general knowledge. There were lads who excelled him in penmanship and in arithmetic, but his superior ability in “answering questions,” as one of the boys put it, enabled him to hold his own with the best of them. At the first annual examination he was bracketted, with two others, above all his fellow-scholars, and carried home, to the delight of his proud mother, a beautifully-bound copy of Milton as a prize. This precious volume was ever regarded as too sacred to be touched by profane hands.
