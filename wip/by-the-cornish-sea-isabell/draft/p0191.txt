The rocks which formed its limit seawards were hard and rugged, as many a shipwrecked sailor had found to his cost, despite the bell-buoy which rose and fell with the waves within a cable’s length.

Within this long sweep of land lay, to the west and north of Pendruthan, the magnificent harbour of Penmaen, of which Pendruthan was only a creek, capable of furnishing anchorage-ground for, not a fleet merely, but a navy, and that at all times of the tide. Two ancient castles, like grim sentinels, stood guarding the entrance, Penmaen the one, Pendruthan the other.
