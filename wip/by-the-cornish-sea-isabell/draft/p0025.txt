Her acquaintance with plants, if it did not range from the cedar-tree that is in Lebanon unto the hyssop that springeth out of the wall, certainly embraced most of the herbs and simples used by amateur practitioners of the healing art, while for nursing she was absolutely without a rival. Whether the malady was a fever, a wound, or even a broken bone, before a word was said about the doctor some one was certain to cry out, “Send for Betsy Polsue.”

Even when the doctor had come and done his office, his patient often preferred Betsy Polsue’s advice to his.

“Paw, Paw,” Betsy would say in contempt, “what do doctors knaw? All they’re good for es to mend booans. You hearken to me, my dears.”
