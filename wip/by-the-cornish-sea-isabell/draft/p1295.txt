[[cont]]The snails in my bit of garden do worrit my life out, and as for slugs, they’re like boy-constructors in a way of spakin’. I had a geranian like a pictur’, and they’ve a ait he like hawly-work, or embroider’, I believe they do call it.”

“Lime is very good for ’em,” suggested the captain.

“’Tes my belief they’d awnly laugh at it,” said Mrs. Craddock; “they’re the gashliest disposition snails I ever meet weth. Why, there’s my satin gown, I’ve had he forty years, and—would ’ee believe it?—I went to teeake un down from the nail this mornin’, where I’d hanged un to smooth out the craises, and ef there wodn a great shiny track all up the middle of the back. Ef a snail’ll do that, he’ll do anything.”

“The snails are evidently very ferocious in these parts,” observed Captain Penbole, winking at Daniel.
