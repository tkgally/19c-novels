Mrs. Warder, it has already been intimated, was about fifty years of age. Her education had been somewhat limited, but what she lacked in culture she made up by a natural shrewdness and force of character. Her work would now probably be called preparatory, for it consisted for the most part in laying first principles. Her pupils, if not exactly babes, were certainly not out of the milk stage of education.

The interior arrangements of the schoolroom were of a primitive order. The seats consisted for the most part of planks laid on sections of a tree, each about eight inches long. Desks there were none.
