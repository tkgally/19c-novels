A Cornish sailor known to the writer, selects names for his offspring long before they are born, and all the entreaties of wife and friends avail not to change his mind when once it has been made up. But this summary way of settling the matter is not to be commended, and is one likely to be attended by a considerable amount of domestic friction. Most mothers like to have their fair share of the preliminary weighing of the names suggested as appropriate to their babes. And one cannot in fairness say that they are unreasonable, for to say the least a moiety of the children is theirs.

As soon as it was finally decided by the Trewhellas that the little orphan should be adopted by them, the question naturally suggested itself, “What shall she be called?”
