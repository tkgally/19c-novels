“Good morning, Rebecca,” said Mrs. Dunstable, a stately lady of about forty years of age.

“Good mornin’, ma’am,” said Rebecca, curtseying as she spoke.

“I am come to see about this baby,” said Mrs. Dunstable; “what a sweet child it is. I asked Mr. Carnanton to mention that anything you wanted you might have from the Castle, but on second thoughts it occurred to me that it would be best to send the child up to us; we could care for it so much more conveniently than you. Of course it will be only for a short time until inquiries are made and the child’s friends found.”

“Thenky, ma’am,” said Rebecca, “but if I may be so bowld, I should like to keep the dear cheeld weth me. I’ll teeake ceeare of it, ma’am, you may be sure.”
