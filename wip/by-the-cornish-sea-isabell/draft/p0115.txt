Arthur Treleaven was not a lad to miss a good thing when it was to be had for the asking, and he was therefore soon initiated into the mysteries of smoking. Like many another novice he discovered that the thing he had longed for was not all his fancy pictured; nay, he even hated his former love. Inward qualms, not of conscience, superinduced bitter remorse.

This was partly owing to the fact that Arthur had no judicious friend to suggest a proper method of acquiring the difficult art.
