He hinted grimly sometimes that it would give him very great pleasure to perform a <i>post mortem</i> upon her ancient frame; he suggested to Betsy’s patients that she was not over clean, which was certainly true; and among his own particular friends he described her with strong emphasis as, “that confounded old witch.”

To call Betsy Polsue “a confounded old witch” was scarcely fair to that estimable person, for Betsy’s exploits in necromancy consisted only in the use of sundry charms to stop bleeding, and to cure erysipelas and similar diseases.
