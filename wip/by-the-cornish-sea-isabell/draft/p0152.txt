He had his eye on a suitable individual, Mr. Simon Beckerleg, a person the shady side of fifty, a widower with one daughter, who would be able to help him in his business. Mr. Simon Beckerleg had passed most of his life in the humble occupation of stone-breaking, but that was nothing to his disadvantage—he was a very erudite stone-breaker, and, besides, his profession would have taught him to wield the birch with good effect, to hit, in fact, the most telling blow in the most fitting place. Mr. Simon Beckerleg therefore, upon an early day, as parliamentary usage puts it, assisted by his daughter Jemima, assumed the function lately performed by the lamented Mrs. Thornlake.

The building was calculated to inspire a great awe in the minds of all but hardened reprobates like Jemmy Nash.
