“You seem inclined to poke fun at me,” said Mark, rather irritably, “but I want to speak seriously to you, if only you’ll listen for a minute.”

“What do you mean by seriously? Is it really a sermon you are going to deliver?”

“Now be quiet a moment, do, Jenny,” pleaded Mark. “What I want to say is this—I’m going away to-morrow from St. Enodoc, and before I go I want to be sure of one thing. When we were little we used to call one another husband and wife, and since we have grown older we have been fond of one another—I think I may say that—and lately you must have seen, Jenny, that I cared more for you than for anybody else. Will you promise me that when I am out of sight in Bristol you won’t forget me, and that when we are old enough to marry you will be my wife?”
