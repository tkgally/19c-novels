Mrs. Warder was a lady of about fifty years of age. Her husband, a master of a small coasting-vessel, had died six years after her marriage, leaving her at thirty totally unprovided for. Mrs. Warder was not one to sit down quietly when there was anything to be got by standing up, and with a promptitude characteristic of her she founded, a month after the decease of the lamented Captain Warder, an establishment for implanting in the minds of juvenile Pendruthan the rudiments of useful knowledge.
