“Thank God,” said the doctor, “he will get on now. Take great care of him, Mrs. Treleaven, but I need hardly tell you that. What in the world was old mother Polsue about, as I came in last night? What extraordinary antics was she performing in the corner there?”

Mrs. Treleaven, in reply, repeated Betsy’s story respecting the cause of the sickness, and of her charm to remove the ill-wish.

Dr. Anderton could not control his indignation. “She ought to be sent to prison for a rogue and a vagabond,” he said, “and I am greatly surprised at a sensible woman like you, Mrs. Treleaven, believing in such rank nonsense. Give God thanks for restoring your child, and don’t listen any more to impostors like old Betsy.”
