It was situated on a very high mound, almost a hill, commanding a beautiful prospect; in front was the Bristol Channel, in clear weather bounded by the flcecy hills of Glamorganshire; to the right was the mouth of the Parret, with the Islands Flathomes, and Steepholmes, between the shores of the Channel, and the coast of Glamorgan.

The hill on which the castle stood was watered at its base by a small river, which in feudal times had served the purpose of a moat, but was now a beautiful clear trout stream; near the castle was the of ruins a priory of Benedictine monks, built by the same Sir William de Mohun, and a small and insignificant town of the same name with the castle, was situated behind it. But the important place of supply was Minehead, a borough town about four miles off.

In the year 1825, one delicious evening in July, Lord Dunster and his sister, Lady Gertrude Mohun, were placed opposite a window, commanding the prospect we have just described, with the beautiful knoll on which the castle stood, clothed with the finest trees, and the windows of the room opening on a terrace the whole length of that side of the building.
