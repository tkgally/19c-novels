Lord John held Mrs. De Cameron in too great contempt to answer her, excepting by a low, solemn bow, and turning to Emma, he said,

“I hope I shall not undergo persecution without the reward of feeling that one lady, at least, will not be in danger of getting cold.”

Emma felt all the delicacy of this reply, and walked on, in compact with the rest of the party, till they came to the church-yard, which was surrounded by such a wall as country church-yards usually are, and which was much higher on the outer side, than on the inner.

The sexton’s cottage was enquired for, and his wife came out, but she could not give the key of the gate. Wilson, the carpenter, was at my Lord’s putting up a play house, and had it in his pocket. This difficulty was at last obviated by the gentlemen removing a few stones, of a heap that was against the wall, and forming some steps, by which the party easily mounted, and walking a few yards on the top of the wall, got as easily down by a tomb-stone.
