I hope I have not lost it in the park, I would not have it picked up by one of the club-men, for the world; for it has got on it the name of our institution, and it would be talked of over half the town, and a betting book opened on its success: oh, here it is! Now, give me, <i>me only</i>, the credit of the name, I will allow no one an atom of a share in it. You will never guess it, if I give you fifty years to do it in. The <i>Intellectania</i>. Is not that charming? Then <i>you</i> are, at all events, to be Vice Presidentess, and your friend, Miss Briggs, Secretary <i>ess</i>; there are a thousand other things to arrange—our seal, and motto, the first must be highly <i>imaginative</i>, the other <i>classical</i>; Lady Olivia will furnish them. Another season we must have a house, for I foresee our establishment will be very numerous; but for the latter part of this year, Lady O—— B—— and you, will, I dare say, allow alternate meetings at your houses: we mean first to elect the members, then the the managing committee, and then form the rules by which our society is to be governed. No one can belong to it who has not had an original work appear <i>in print</i>; a sonnet, or copy of verses in one of the Annuals, will do; but it must be well authenticated, as her own.
