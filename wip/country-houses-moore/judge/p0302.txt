“It was so <i>susceptible</i>, or he would not have recommended it to her ladyship.”

Throwing herself back in her chair, Lady Anne exclaimed, “What, under heaven, can you mean by a <i>susceptible pig?</i>” and bursting into a more violent fit of boisterous laughter than any woman beyond the vulgar, excepting one of the highest rank and fashion, had dared to indulge in, she called on the poor Count for an explanation, the laugh was catching, even where the joke was not heard. The Count looked as if he had committed a crime, his very <i>toupée</i> standing upright with alarm: when the mirth had a little subsided and the servants had composed the muscles of their faces, he was called on to explain; afraid at first to trust himself again in English, he said he meant the pig was <i>tendre</i>, or he believed <i>touchy</i> was the word.
