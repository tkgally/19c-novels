[[cont]]Mrs. Villiars, have survived the loss of an excellent husband; you will, with the blessing of God, support that of your son, if H<sc>e</sc> ordains it should be so.”

“But is it not very hard on me, that I should be called to such trials?”

“It is by trials that we are fitted for Heaven; we grow too fond of this world and its enjoyments; the loss of them carries our thoughts and our wishes to a better; and we are sometimes punished by our Heavenly Father for our too great love and dependance on perishable things.”

“I cannot believe,” said Mrs. Villiars, “that we can love our children too much.”

“Oh! we can love any thing too much, that takes us off from our dependance on God, and places our happiness in the things of this world; there are many events, that we short-sighted mortals think sorrows, that are blessings in disguise. You, my good friend, are ready to murmur at the prospect of losing a beloved son; think for a moment how much worse than the <i>loss</i> of him, would be seeing him drag a life of misery and debility: after the severe blow he has had, the understanding is often impaired, or annihilated, or the limbs paralized; death, under such circumstances, is a happy release.”

“Do not talk to me of a happy release that would deprive me of my darling child!”
