“My aunt Beaumont, I fancy, though I am no judge, has got but an indifferent set of acquaintance. I think so because aunt Shirley will not mix with them.”

“You cannot follow a better judgment than that of your excellent, your <i>incomparably</i> excellent, aunt Shirley; I know no person so nearly angelic as she is.”

“You have amply gratified my curiosity about aunt Beaumont; I now understand her, and her Briggs, but I wish I dare ask for the same gratification about my <i>own</i> particularly dear aunt.”

“Oh, no!” replied Mr. Melbourne, “her sorrows are sacred, I dare not approach their shrine.”

“Has she, indeed, had such very deep sorrows? I know she lost a darling nephew.”
