General Dorozenski, and Count Wszebor, were to have left Eastbrooke Park early on Monday morning, but, unfortunately, the paper-mill they went to see, on Saturday, was not at work, as the men had a few days’ holidays at Christmas: all that could be seen of the machinery was carefully examined by the General, who, like most Russians, was engaged in some mercantile speculation, and had been very successful with steam-boats. He had now another object in view, and came to England to pick up what information he could. His military appearance, and his numerous orders, deceived the honest manufacturers. They never dreamt that, like his Emperor, and the Duchess of Oldenburg, he was stealing their trades, and, therefore, readily showed him every thing.
