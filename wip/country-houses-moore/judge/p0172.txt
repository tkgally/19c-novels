“Then I understand,” said Mrs. Graham, “you think that this introduction may be advantageous in a matrimonial point of view, to your daughter; and therefore, I ought not to throw in a hint of objection: but do favour me with another perusal of that letter, I may have misunderstood its meaning.”

She again read the letter, and, returning it, said, “No! I was not mistaken! I had heard, though I did not pay much attention to the report, as having no interest in it, that there are going to be private theatricals at Stoke Park; and I see Lord Tintern confirms the report, and hints that your daughter may be called on to assist at them.”

“Oh!” replied, Mr. Legh, “they are only sort of holyday things; I suppose got up by the children, and perhaps they may expect her to play some quadrilles, or some such thing—indeed, in a childish thing, amongst themselves, if she did take a part, it is what she has done at school, and you know even boarders in a convent act those sort of pieces.”
