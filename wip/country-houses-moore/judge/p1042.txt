“Aye! aye! Emma, they are fine folks, and to their misfortune, have nothing to do; so they try to make something out of nothing; poor work in my mind.”

Emma, in her turn, enquired after her flowers, her pets, and above all, her friends the Grahams; of whom Mr. Legh could tell her nothing; for he had not seen them since she had been from home; but she might ride over as soon as she pleased, and enquire after them, and tell them all she had seen and heard. But this was not what she wished to do, though she thanked her father for the proposal. She must get her heart and its feelings under more control before she could submit them to Mrs. Grahams’s scrutiny; in her present state she would detect the whole at a glance.
