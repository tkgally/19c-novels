Caroline, only used to the small establishments of her aunt, and Mrs. Lomax, was astonished at the number of servants that appeared, night and morning, in the library, when Mr. George Worthley read family prayers; and was still more surprised, to learn from her aunt’s maid who dressed her, that only one half of the under servants attended each time, and that the household usually consisted of forty persons.

How was all this kept in order by the <i>quiet</i> lady Worthley, who seemed to sit down to her carpet work like other people!

Miss Shirley enlightened her niece: it was the pervading <i>mind</i> that did it. Lady Worthley looked into everything, but it was <i>regularly</i>, and <i>systematically</i>. And, in addition to her own numerous household, Caroline sometimes accompanied her to her village, where she had her schools, and her alms-houses—the most unruly of her various cares; but all was regulated with the most exact attention, and the most perfect composure.
