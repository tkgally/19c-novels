“But he did go at last to Italy,” said Miss Briggs, “and we heard of him in the gallery of the Vatican; he was speechless with admiration.”

“But he went there,” said Crayon, “too late to do him any good, or his life was not prolonged enough for him to shew it. His great misfortune was not painting really from nature.”

“What can you mean,” said Miss Briggs, “he had always a living sitter before him?”

“But it was not <i>real nature</i> he saw; it was <i>artificial</i> nature, if I may use the expression, or embellished nature: if he had taken a match-seller, or a primrose girl, out of the street, he would then have <i>painted</i> nature, as Sir Joshua did: instead of <i>rouged</i> ladies trying to look <i>pretty</i>, and statesmen endeavouring to look <i>wise</i>. I was never more disappointed in my life, than when I went into Lawrence’s show room: it contained from two to three hundred half finished portraits, some, indeed many, of them admirably painted.
