Mr. Dupré almost convulsed the party with laughter at his description of the rage of “Mine Hostess of the Crown,” at their refusing to pay the exorbitant price she put on her horse: he shewed his powers as a comic actor to great advantage—it made a good story, though the horse came to life, and the man recovered, having been more frightened than hurt, and served as an excellent joke, till the parties met with another adventure of the same kind.

Lady Tintern inquired where Mr. Edward Fermor was; he had taken the part of Georgibus, and was a material person.

“He could not come with us, he was on guard, said Mr. Duprée, but he will ride down to-morrow, and bid us assure you and Lord Tintern he would be here to breakfast.”

Lord John Leslie was just returned from a tour that had not only taken in part of Greece, and the Islands of the Archipelago, but had extended some way into Egypt. He was, therefore, a worthy member of the Travellers’ club, whilst Mr. Duprée, who had only come lately from Paris was quite in his noviciate.
