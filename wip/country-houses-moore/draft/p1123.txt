# Chapter IV.

A party of twenty in a boat, like a party of the same size at dinner, must separate into groups and converse with near neighbours, for what is said at one end, cannot by any possibility edify those at the other, without an exertion of lungs no one likes to use. Colonel Blackburn’s auditors were in the middle of the boat, and whilst they were listening to details of India, Lady Honiton, Mrs. Murray Williams, Mrs. Gordon, and Lord Frederic Mountjoy, were discussing a recent elopement, that had taken place within their immediate circle.
