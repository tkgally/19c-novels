Lady Anne played ecarté whilst the elderlies were at whist, and the men gathered round her, and betted. The Misses Riley and their protegé took possession of the piano forte. Lady Tintern had her work-table, and employed Emma to draw a pattern for her; they had not been long seated when Lord John seceded from the ecarté to the work-table, and placed himself <i>not</i> opposite to Emma, but obliquely, so that he had a constant view of her, whilst she only saw him when her head was raised from her employment; he rested his elbow on his knee, and his head on his hand, which brought him still more on a level, or rather below her.

It is inconceivable to those who have not studied such matters, what importance there is in <i>position</i>: what would be nothing, said with the stiffness of ‘<i>attention</i>,’ at a drill, melts quite into the tender, and steals softly on the ear, when brought into such contact as we have described, with the person to whom it is addressed.
