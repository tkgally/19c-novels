After a long silence, as Lord Dunster seemed lost in thought, Lady Gertrude took her usual evening walk on the terrace, to which she stepped by an open window.

That some unusual visiters, or rather inmates, were expected at the castle she knew from her maid, as a wing of the building once allotted to her, when young as a nursery, had been carefully and expensively fitted up; but no one could guess for <i>whom</i>, though abundant were the random guesses of the household.

Lady Gertrude had implicit confidence in her brother; she knew his honour and integrity, and she therefore determined to give way to no conjecture, time would show <i>who</i> this person was—the like forbearance had not been practised in the lower departments of the establishment.
