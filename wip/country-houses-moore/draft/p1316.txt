When Lady Clara and her friend were taken to the stall where the tables were, it was impossible to choose; the chess table was so useful, and the other so beautiful, and seeing Sir Godfrey much inclined to the latter, she said,

“I do think you must have both, indeed a choice is quite impossible,” and turning to the young lady who presided, she said, “Sir Godfrey will, I am sure, take <i>both</i> these tables.”

There was no resisting this, and he paid his ten guineas for them, satisfied that he had gained much <i>eclât</i> and consideration by having them chosen by Lady Clara Aston; they then returned to the Duchess, who immediately began,

“Sir Godfrey, if you do not take the waistcoat this instant it will be gone; I would not let it go to a gentleman who was here just now with his hand in his pocket.”
