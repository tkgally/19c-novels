A party on the water having been mentioned during dinner, Lord Honiton said, “it is a fine evening for a sail on the lake, there is always wind enough on the water, and if the ladies choose to be on the alert after dinner they may have some amusement, as my boats are going out fishing, and the trout in this small lake show some play;” and turning to Sir Henry Willoughby, said, “mine are very experienced water-men, <i>we</i> need not go, but if the young men prefer water to cool claret, they are free.”

Of course the young men all offered themselves for the water party, the jaunting car carried the ladies to the water-side, the party enjoyed the fishing scene, and the distant clarionet, was multiplied by innumerable echos; thus occupied and amused, they remained on the lake till after the sun set, leaving it with regret, that there was no moon to tempt prolonged enjoyment.
