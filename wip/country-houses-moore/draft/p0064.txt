On the latter inspecting the new purchase, he set the stable yard in a roar, by asking if it was a natural or a painted horse? when this was a little subsided, he asserted on <i>his honour</i> that both the Arabs and Persians, who were very particular about their horses, and amongst whom he had served, <i>always</i> painted theirs when they wanted to sell them, or to make any fine shew; and moreover put on false tails and manes. Provoked by their incredulity at this assertion, he offered, “if <i>Monsieur</i> pleased,” to prove its truth, and make an old gray mare, who went in the market cart, a proper match for the new purchase; all the stable establishment declared themselves eager for the experiment.

Arthur was highly diverted, and in the simplicity of his heart sought out Frederic before he dressed, and repeated all this, as a capital joke.
