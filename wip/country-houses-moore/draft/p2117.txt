“And always failed,” replied Crayon, “it was not <i>in</i> him, if it had, it would have forced its way out, <i>genius</i> will not be fettered, it breaks down all barriers, but I allow your favourite <i>exquisite taste</i>, and a particularly quick and keen eye in discovering that minutiæ of character in a countenance, on which the likeness often depends, and the most delicate hand in pourtraying it, that even artists were surprized at: his eyes were <i>superb</i>.”

Miss Briggs liked nothing so well as such conversation as that now carrying on; and as Mrs. Beaumont had found out some friends she had met with at Naples, Sir George and Lady Thornby, with whom she was laughing over their adventures together in a voyage to Ischia. Miss Briggs went on with the subject of Sir Thomas Lawrence, by saying, “surely you think his Satan <i>historic</i>, and a fine picture?”
