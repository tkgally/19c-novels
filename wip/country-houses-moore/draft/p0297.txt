Lord O’Donnel represented him to Lady Tintern, as “a young man who had distinguished himself at Eton, and had expectations <i>(anglicè) hopes</i> of getting a stall (minor of course) in some cathedral; he had so anxiously wished to be presented to a person of Lord Tintern’s influence, and was himself so acceptable in society from his talents, Lord O’Donnel could not resist bringing him, and again implored Lady Tintern’s forgiveness for having taken such an unwarrantable liberty.”
