[[cont]]open a letter from Christmas till February, that I may not encounter a tender epistle from my taylor, or boot maker, with “hard times,” “a large family,” “heavy payment to make next week,” when one has not a shilling in one’s banker’s hands; and the rascals are only making up a purse for Brighton, instead of coats or boots for their customers: and of all this misery, stupid souls wish you <i>joy!</i> Is not it so Ma’am?” addressing his mother, Lady Vernon, who was deeply engaged, writing tickets, for clothing for the poor.

“Indeed, my dear Frederic, you forget that Christmas is joyful in a religious point of view; it is the season which opened the gates of everlasting life to mankind, and on that account it calls on those who have affluence to open their hearts and houses towards those who feel the temporary evils of cold and poverty, which are those only we have the power to alleviate; the good old times are gone by when open house was kept, perhaps it is better for the poor to have more permanent comforts than beef and ale given them, yet I own I regret they cannot have both: it must be a long purse that can clothe and feed also.”
