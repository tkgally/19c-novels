Religious prejudice sours and narrows the mind, and unfortunately no boon granted to the Catholics can alter that feeling; all they receive they consider as a <i>right</i> long <i>unjustly withheld</i>, and short,—far short of what they are entitled to.

In his new situation in the boat Mr. Dallas was placed opposite to Miss O’Brian, whom he immediately addressed by

“Pray, Miss O’Brian, what have I done to offend you, that you gave me such a dead cut at Lady Allenham’s the other night?”

“I never meant to cut you,” replied Miss O’Brian, “but you seemed so determined not to see <i>me</i>, I would not waste a bow on you, but I know why you so resolutely looked away, you were afraid that Mrs. Barton, or I, whom you saw was in distress for her carriage, should ask you to enquire for it. Oh, I know you young men of old.”

“If we are young, how could you know us old?” said Mr. Dallas, “that is something I should like to have explained.”
