I really felt very much for Mrs. Beaumont, but a kind legacy of five hundred pounds to Miss Briggs put <i>her</i> in the best possible mood of administering consolation to her friend.

“When I went the next morning to take leave of Mrs. Beaumont, I found her table covered with prints of costumes, and I afterwards heard she took her weeds from those of Mary Queen of Scots, and Catherine Par in Lodges’s portraits of eminent persons. That there was any <i>heart</i> in her grief is hardly fair to expect; her husband had never sought to gain her affections, and of course she never gave them; he treated her with indulgent kindness, though rather as a spoilt child; she was satisfied; but this unlucky clause which said, “you shall not be <i>happier</i>,” did away for a time with the recollection of what had been content, if not a warmer feeling.
