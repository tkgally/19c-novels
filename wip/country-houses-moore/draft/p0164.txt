Mr. Legh was a shrewd man, and he belonged to a profession that called this quality, as well as caution, into daily, nay, hourly, exercise; he therefore kept this letter, and its contents, to himself, till he had consulted a friend in whom he had great confidence.

This friend was a Mrs. Graham, the widow of a neighbouring clergyman, a well-educated woman of good family, who had preferred happiness to wealth, and united herself to a most amiable man, though not largely beneficed. They lived in retirement on his living, and, whilst every day added to their mutual happiness, it contributed to the improvement and embellishment of <i>her</i> mind, by sharing the thoughts, and, in many instances, the studies also, of a man whose learning and taste were not often excelled.
