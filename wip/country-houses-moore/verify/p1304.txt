Soon after the library party returned to the drawing-room, Lady Clara said,

“Oh, Ottery, how I shall like to see you dance again; you used to enjoy it so! and I rejoice there will be an opportunity so soon as to-morrow night.”

“I fear, dear Clara, you will lose that gratification, if it be one, for my dancing days are over, and I don’t go to balls.”

“Astonishing,” exclaimed Lady Clara, “bless me, what an <i>old</i> gentleman you are grown, Frederic! however, I don’t mind what you say now, for you have not been in a dancing country; when once we get you to our ball, we shall soon overcome your resolution.”

“But you are not likely to get me there; I never go to such places.”

“Why, then you must stay at home by yourslf; I am sure you won’t turn sulky; that’s not your nature.”

“I hope it is not; but nevertheless, I mean to stay at home; I am not afraid of my own society, and I have letters to write.”
