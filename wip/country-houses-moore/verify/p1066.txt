“But why,” asked Emma, “does she write her name as if it was a French one?

“Oh!” returned Mr. Duprée, “that originated in a mistake of Dunois in reading her card as Mrs. De Cameron, and she was so pleased with the conceit, and struck with the vanity of appearing to be of Norman extraction, she, by a little flourish of her D confirmed it—but here she comes,” and, making his bow, he disappeared.

She would perhaps have passed Emma, if she had not seen her attendant.

“Miss Legh, I protest! and in her usual bloom and beauty! I see one of your swains at least, is faithful, hold him fast, he is worth ten of your false one.”

Emma tried to laugh, but she could hardly do more than colour, and rejoiced at some acquaintance of Mrs. De Cameron’s coming up, and engaging her attention, so that they separated.
