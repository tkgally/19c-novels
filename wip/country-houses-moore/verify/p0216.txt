Mem. 3.—Never to omit an early walk before breakfast if possible, but not to go rambling by yourself: join either the governess, or even the nurses and children for protection.

This injunction Emma had strictly followed, and had enjoyed the fresh air, and amused herself with the children.

Mem. 4.—To let me know if you want any thing, are ill, or not happy, though the latter I think impossible.

“My dear, dear, father! mentally said Emma, how little do you, in the innocence and affection of your heart, guess what your poor girl feels, and how possible it is to be <i>miserable</i>, surrounded by all that ought to make me happy!”

Mem. 5.—Write to me often.

“This I can do, though I cannot comply with the injunction before it.”

Writing to her father, and its difficulties occupied her mind; and after a longer period of watchfulness than belongs to youth and health, she fell asleep in the midst of composing this letter.
