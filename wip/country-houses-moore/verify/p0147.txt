# Chapter I.

<verse>
In Britain’s Isle, no matter where,
An ancient pile of building stands,
The <i>Hunsdens</i>, and the <i>Hattons</i> there
Employ’d the power of fairy hands.

To raise the ceiling’s fretted height,
Each panel in achievement clothing,
Rich windows that exclude the light,
Cold passages that lead to nothing.

Full oft within the spacious walls,
When he’d full fifty winters o’er him,
My grave <i>Lord Keeper</i> led the brawls,
The seal and maces danced before him.

His bushy beard, and-shoe-strings green
His high crown’d hat, and satin doublet,
Mov’d the stout heart of England’s queen,
Though Pope and Spaniard could not touch it.
</verse>

“Walls have ears,” says the proverb, and voices too sometimes—it is well not always, or strange histories might be told; and characters that stand high in historic recollection, might sink an hundred per cent. in the scale.
