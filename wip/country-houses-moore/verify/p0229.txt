“Now for the epilogue,” said Lord John. “Duprée, you can help us there, I know you write such things.”

But he declared he had torn up, in despair, all his attempts, he could only speak,—not write, one.

Mrs. De Cameron caught at this opening—she had made an attempt, and would fetch it. Whilst she was gone,

“I wonder” said Mr. Fitz Gibbon, “how you, Lady Tintern, can tolerate that abominable woman, I am sure she would set nations together by the ears; she wants to manage every thing, and put every body else aside; do let us agree to vote her epilogue <i>infamous stuff</i>!”

“She ought to have fair play,” replied Lady Tintern, “you have had your turn, though, to own the truth, I don’t expect much from her performance, for she is one of those people, who, from trying at <i>every</i> thing, seldom attain to excellence in <i>any</i>, or I may say, success.”
