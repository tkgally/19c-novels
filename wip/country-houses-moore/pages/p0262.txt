“I believe you, from the bottom of my heart, in that particular,” said Lady Anne.

And even Lord John, who had that command of countenance which belongs to those who live much in the world, could not preserve his unmoved under this speech, and the pointed tone in which it was made: he coloured slightly.

Lady Tintern, with her usual tact, turned the conversation, by asking Mr. Manton some question about one of his parishioners: but Lady Anne’s manner raised a suspicion in her mind that something more than the fatigue of the ride had overcome Emma, and she regretted that when she had gone into her room before dinner she had been so easily satisfied by her assurance that she was only a little fatigued, and should have needed no rest, if it had not been for the representation in the evening: the moment she returned to the drawing-room, she lighted her candle in order to go to her protegée, and then see how the theatre looked, and if there was light enough in it.
