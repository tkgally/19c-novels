# Chapter XIX.

W<sc>e</sc> must now return to our friends at Inglewood; Lady Honiton had been chiefly occupied since we left her, with watching most anxiously her son’s health, and following all the various and opposing symptoms of his treacherous and flattering disease; she was just at this moment cheered by delusive appearances; though Lord Ottery had not <i>really</i> improved, he had for several days <i>seemed</i> better, and more cheerful.

One morning, contrary to Lord Honiton’s usual habit, he breakfasted in his own room, and sent to his assembled family their respective letters, and the papers. Lady Louisa took up one of the latter, and the next moment dropped, apparently lifeless, from her chair.
