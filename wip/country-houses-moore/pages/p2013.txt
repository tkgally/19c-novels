# Chapter XVIII.

T<sc>he</sc> day after the arrival of the foreigners, Comtesse Rosenberg’s maid was so ill, from the effects of her journey and voyage, it was necessary to send for Mr. Watkins, the apothecary of Minehead; he promptly obeyed the summons, and after seeing his patient, went to Mrs. Bartlet’s room, and to her medicine closet to mix up the requisite remedies.

“What,” said the good lady, “do you think of Mademoiselle Josepine?”

“Oh,” replied the man of medicine, “she will do very well with a little of your good nursing, the <i>gastric</i> powers are a little disturbed; but how does the great lady do? I have seen the second person in the establishment, and an odd looking person she is!”
