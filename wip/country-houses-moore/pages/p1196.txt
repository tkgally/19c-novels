“My dear,” replied Lady Honiton, “I am quite easy as to any thing she may <i>feel</i> on the occasion. She not only appears to me not to care a straw for St. Leonard, but rather, like her father, to think him silly: but I don’t mind that, if I can make him bite, I could soon persuade her into the match: high conditioned girls ought to have none of your vulgar <i>love</i> notions; <i>establishment</i> is all they <i>ought</i> to care about.”

“But,” said Miss O’Brian, “how can you keep them from thinking of love, it is so natural at their age, to like the <i>tender passion</i>; however, you, as a mother, and so sage a mother, must know best, though I own it has sometimes surprised <i>me</i> that you like to have that Dallas so constantly about Lady Adelaide and Lady Louisa; he is certainly handsome; and reckoned agreeable, though <i>I</i> may not think him so.”
