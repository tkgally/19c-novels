I was with my family visiting at Wedglock park, when Miss Briggs, in full <i>legacy mourning</i>, with blackened coach and servants, arrived: before me she was a little cautious in expatiating on the <i>grief</i> of the widow, but ventured to ask Lady Worthley, as a most accurate judge in those matters, how long she thought Mrs. Beaumont ought to keep on her mourning? The visit was paid, and the reported answer to this question communicated to Mrs. Beaumont, who started when she heard, “certainly not <i>less</i> than two years; Lady Derventdale, the richest of all widows, mourned three, if not four, years.”

“But she was not restrained from marrying again,” was the reply, “that is the unkindest cut of all: but it does not signify, I know I must die of this seclusion, and I am <i>resigned</i>.”
