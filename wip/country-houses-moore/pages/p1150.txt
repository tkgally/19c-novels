All the rest was charming and flattering, and went well to the landing. It was as dark as a July evening could be, for the moon was only just rising, and throwing false shadows.

In getting out of the boat, what had been wickedly intended at getting in, really, and accidentally took place; for Miss O’Brian’s foot slipped, and went into the water: she made as much as she possibly could of the accident, and, though really only one foot was wetted, lamented over herself: at last, having got it partly dried in a neighbouring house, and declaring it was impossible she could go to Mrs. Gordon’s, it was arranged that Mrs. Murray Williams should set her down in Green-street, as she was going to take her eldest daughter home, who was out of health, and rather fatigued. Lady Honiton begged her to set down Lady Clara also; as she was going on a journey the next day, she must go to rest early, and offered to take care of Miss Fanny Williams till her mother returned.
