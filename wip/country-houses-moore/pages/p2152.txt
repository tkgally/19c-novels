It is wonderful how habit and kindness endear opposite characters to each other. When Dr. Johnson was asked what could attach <i>him</i> to such a man as Boswell, his answer was, “<i>kindness</i>, sir;” and this mutual feeling brought Miss Shirley to her friend at Marchwood.

The evening was passed by the two friends in recollections of early days, or in histories of all Mrs. Villiars’ joys and sorrows about her children. Her daughter had married a very amiable Col. Forester, though her fond mother thought her worthy of a Duke; but her daughter had been used to have her own way, not for the reason that Dandie Dinmont’s wife gave her children their’s because she “had nothing else to give them,” for they commanded every indulgence that an ample jointure could command.
