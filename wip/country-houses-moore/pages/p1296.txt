At an early age, Lady Emily Bourke was sent to the convent of St. Catharine’s, Bruges; and of all the instruction to which the name of education is given, that of a convent is the <i>worst</i>—the most defective—the nuns, who are teachers, are ignorant of every thing but curious needle-work; what can they teach? they enter the cloister themselves at an early age, and all books but one or two on devotion are forbidden; and of the world they know nothing. Yet there are <i>two</i> things to be learnt in a convent, <i>obedience</i> and <i>regularity</i>; but they are both taught under such irksome forms, that they create disgust, rather than form useful habits; it is not the obedience of the heart and affections which springs from love and duty, and which is so dear and precious to a parent, and such a reward for all the anxious cares of childhood.
