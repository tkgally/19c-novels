“Light minds are pleased with trifles,” observed Mrs. Beaumont, “when you are older, you will judge more accurately of persons and things.”

“I wish I was never to grow older,” replied Emily, “if age is to take off my zest of such enjoyments as fall in my way; I don’t mean real down-right old age, that I know stops all enjoyment, but, I mean growing a little older and a little wiser.”

“A little wiser you had need grow,” said Mrs. Beaumont rather peevishly, which put an end to the conversation till the party set Miss Talbot down in Park Lane, and proceeded to Stanhope-street.

The following morning Mrs. Beaumont was obliged to devote to her man-of-business, he brought her leases to sign, and various other matters of business that belong to large landed property to inspect; she was sufficiently clear headed and alive to her own interest as generally to understand, and to the necessary degree, attend to such details.

It is impossible to <i>get away</i> from such occupation; your men-of-business are the most determined people in the world, they push lease after lease, deed after deed, under the hand of their unhappy and wearied client.
