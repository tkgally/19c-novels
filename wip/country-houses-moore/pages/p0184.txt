Mrs. De Cameron knew the influence both of these had on this occasion, and when they arrived, made her court first to Dunois, and so flattered him on the score of a tragedy he had been vain enough to pretend to write in English, that at last he inflicted the penalty on the party of hearing an act of it read. He even proposed to translate <i>the Critic</i> into French for the present occasion, but as Lady Tintern meant to “buy golden opinions from all sorts of persons,” one part of the entertainment was to be national.
