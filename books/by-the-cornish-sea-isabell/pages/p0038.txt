“Lord, love her,” said the kind-hearted woman, “I never seed such a little darling in my life. Hold her a minute, Dan, while I run upstairs and fetch some clothes belonging to my poor little dear thas gone—I never expected to use them no more—they’ll be jist the thing for her.”

It was fifteen years since Daniel Trewhella had held his own child in his arms, but the thought of that dear one, loved and lost, was borne back to him by the waif from the sea, and the hot tears rained down his weather-beaten cheeks as he rocked the child to and fro. Trewhella had a rough exterior, but a braver, more tender heart never beat than his. He was, indeed, a splendid specimen of the old Cornish race. Standing six feet in his stockings and being broad in proportion, Daniel possessed a mighty strength which equalled that of two ordinary men.
