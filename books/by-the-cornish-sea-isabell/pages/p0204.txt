Arthur’s hands were as yet somewhat tender, and the rough bricks had a tendency to remove the skin in a decidedly disagreeable fashion. Mrs. Treleaven’s tender heart and affection for her boy prompted her to suggest, when he came home at the close of the day with bleeding hands, that the work did not suit him. But Arthur was a boy of spirit, and made light of such matters.

“My dear,” she said, “your hands are quite raw. I don’t think you ought to go to-morrow; I’m sure your poor father would grieve to see his boy doing work like this. I’m sorry now I agreed to Uncle Arthur’s proposal.”
