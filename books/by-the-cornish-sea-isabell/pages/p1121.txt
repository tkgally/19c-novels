“We are quite rich, mother, arn’t we?” he said.

“Yes,” replied the widow, “God is very good to us. We must let the money remain in the bank if we can. You’ll want some of it, perhaps, to furnish a house. ’Tisn’t likely that you’ll stay with mother all your days, or natural either for that matter. We shall have to pay rent for the house now Uncle Anthony is gone, and it is hardly the place to bring a young bride to.”

“Why, mother,” said Arthur, laughing, “we need not look forward quite so far as that, I’ve no bride to bring home,—I haven’t even got a sweetheart.”

“No, perhaps not,” said Mrs. Treleaven, “but my eyes are not so old but that they can see which way you are looking. I am very glad, my dear, she is a good girl, and will make a good wife.”
