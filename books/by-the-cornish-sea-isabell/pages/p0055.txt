“I’ll tell ee why all they neeames weeant do. The beeaby is become to me so dear as my own cheeld Jenifer who was carr’d to her greeave sixteen eear agone, and I want to have her called Jenifer too. P’raps she’ll make up to me for the wan thas gone.”

And so it was resolved that the child should be baptized Jenifer—a name lightly esteemed by the ordinary Englishman, but sweet to the Cornish ear, for was it not the name of their own Arthur of Tintagel’s peerless queen—the loved, the fallen, the repentant, the pardoned Guinevere?
