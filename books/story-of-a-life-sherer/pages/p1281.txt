“The old man stood long, statue like, in silent agony; then starting from his fearful reverie, he looked up imploringly to Heaven, and down, with a reproaching frown, on Feizi. Not one word did he utter, but seizing a creese, which lay concealed in the folds of his girdle, he would have driven it into his own bosom. The terrified youth caught his uplifted arm; then, kneeling at his feet, swore to expiate his meditated sin by all the severities of penance, if he would promise never to attempt any thing against a life so sacred as his own. The venerable Brahmin now burst into tears, and, trembling as he wept, asked in an agitated tone, two requests—“Grant these,” said he, “and I will live and pardon thee.”

“With eager transport, yet solemnly, he swore to observe his will.

“‘Never translate the Vedas, and never reveal to any living soul the mysterious symbol of our holy faith.’
