Plague in the tainted air, hanging with its menace, over a sickening city,—the enemy at the gate;—these are evils terrible to man, but light to famine. Those whom the pestilence may spare, eat food, and give it to their children. In the pauses of the battle thunder, the full feast is spread, and the wine cup is gaily pledged; but famine is a yet more awful scourge. The infant cries at the dry, unyielding breast of its pining mother. Children of the romping age sit still, and colourless. Men move about as wolves, and prowl for prey; tear food from the feeble; fight for it with the strong; and go apart with the prized morsel, and face some lonely wall, and with an eye glancing all restlessly, from side to side, in ravening haste give it all to their own wild craving.
