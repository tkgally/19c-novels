It may be supposed that I found the young and attached Malek a companion to my heart. We were much together. My progress in the Arabic was rapid; and there was hardly a question my mind could frame, but he, or my Arab instructor, or the story teller, or the merchant’s lady, would fully answer for me.

Nothing, perhaps, is more desolately dreary in its aspect than the sandy, and barren plain around Alexandria.

Who could look upon it and live, if the chill, and gloomy vapours of a northern climate frowned above it? But in Egypt, a sky eternally serene—an air dry and pure, give life and lightness to the spirits and the frame. Often I went out on long solitary rides far from the city walls; and, though alone, I was never, never sad.
