“Ibrahim will not return to-night,” said my conductor, “I saw him passing in at the <sc>Victory Gate</sc>, as we came out of the city; we may sleep here.”

I found this Ibrahim was a Sauton; this his hermitage, and never, perhaps, was any situation better chosen for a life of seclusion, and penance.

My anxious desire was now to learn something from my conductor, concerning Malek, and our present destination. He was provokingly brief. “Malek is at Djidda. I have sworn to him by the black stone of Mecca to bring you safe to him; what need you know more?”

I have never met with a man so taciturn. He did every thing as silently as a mute; if he spoke to me, it was with the eye, or the wave, or beckon of the hand. His look was ever very expressive; his form spare, and lean; but his limbs all sinew, and strength.
