I was pondering these things in idle wayward mood, when I observed a young and gentle female approach the window of the inn, and extend her hand with alms to a poor <i>cretin</i>, who lay basking under a sunny wall opposite, and who rose at her motion, and went over to receive them. She dropped a piece of silver, but so slowly, into his tattered hat, that I could plainly see the pity moistening her eye was busy in her heart. The poor <i>cretin</i> fell upon his knees and gave an idiot laugh, and there was an unusual light in his dull eye. Still she continued to look on him with a sad and pitying kindness, and she was questioning the <i>fille paysanne</i> of the rustic <i>auberge</i>, who stood bright and cheerful by her side, informing her, as she evidently questioned her, about the poor wretch below. Yes, incomparable angel! then I knew thee not. Little I marked thy comeliness, for the moral charm did shine so brightly in thy piteous looks, that I did think of heaven, not earth, as I then gazed on thee.
