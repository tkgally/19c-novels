They had never been actually a part of his domestic establishment, but had been so liberally paid, and encouraged for their performances before him, that the merchant, who farmed out their talents and their charms, brought them to Egypt as a speculation; thinking to delight the Beys, and the great lords of Cairo, and trusting to his Turkish patron for a favourable word at the palace of the Pacha.

These girls were one evening hired to dance at the house of the merchant; and I was present. I was richly clothed, and had a seat on the left hand of the master, so that, according to custom, in all their songs and dances,—the eyes and words,—the movement and gestures, were all directed towards me.
