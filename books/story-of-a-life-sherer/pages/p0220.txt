I found a table awaiting me, at my return, covered with delicacies, and the choicest wines were sparkling in crystal vases. I sickened at the sight; a something tasteless I ate; a something strong I drank;—but I was <i>alone</i>.

I looked out from the casement, and saw two beggar-brats at play; and the ragged father sat sunning under the wall, and laughing at them with a parent’s pleasure; and there was a gray old man in rags, smiling at what that father said; and I could plainly judge, from features, that three generations, all rags and cheerfulness, were below me there. Ah! then how my heart smote me;—alone—I had sealed my own fate—even so—had sold myself to solitude—sold away my parentage of heart, as well as name and blood and country. Sold all this! and for what? “This night, thou fool.” For what?—the tasteless banquet, and the aching heart.
