Padre Ignazio is often of the party, and he also visits me every morning for an hour, or more—and speaks to me, and reads—and leaves books for me; but, I cannot fix my attention—my thoughts wander—always, always to you.—I have been very unhappy—I do not think I shall live long—I am sure, I hardly wish for life, if I am not to see you. Henry,—my beloved—another time—” (Here the letter was broken off, and seemed to have lain by for some time and again resumed, and continued.)

“I was forced to leave off, my head was so giddy from weakness, and, from that hour, I have never had an opportunity of getting a pen till to-day, and must continue it on the same sheet; but I am much better, dearest Henry, and you, I am told, have been ill; thank God, I did not know that: you are well, and out again; thank God for that blessing. I find too that my father and you have been writing to each other. Oh! if it should be so—perhaps they told me so to fool me—and yet it may be.
