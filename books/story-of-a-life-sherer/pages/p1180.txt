[[center]]<i>Henry to Agatha.</i>

“I write, my angel, in haste and anxiety—I am bewildered—terrified—I have just seen your father—I checked my horse to salute him, and would have spoken.—He returned my salute with a haughty unbending coldness, and rode away, spurring, as if moved to sudden anger or aversion. What can this mean?—my faithful boy Brian will wait for your answer at the bottom of the lane, if it be all night.—Bid Dennis be careful.

“I saw you, my dearest Agatha, yesterday, many times. I was far off on the green hill above the slate-roofed cottage. What a delight it was to catch your moving form; you came often to the window, that looks on the lake, and stood there long; you went in and out of the house often, watering your plants, and walking to and fro on the lawn by yourself, unbonnetted.
