We rode for eighteen hours, then halted six under a tall rock, which threw out its afternoon shadow long and cool. Here we ate of the cold hard eggs, provided as our food; drank from the warm water skin, smoked the refreshing pipe, and slept. For fifteen hours rode forward, then again alighted for three, near to two small wells of discoloured brackish water, and snatched a short slumber under a lone Acacia tree, thorny and spreading out umbrella-like above, but giving only a slight, thin, and broken shade.

Another harassing stage of sixteen hours brought us to the coast. A little bay it formed just where we came on it—a lonely unfrequented spot—no shed, no tree, no water, all sand and rock around; but out upon the shining wave, joyously rocking, lay a large black bark with a tall mast, and a wide yard.
