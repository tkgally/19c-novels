I took with me the piece of sugar cane to my cell in wonder—what could the Arab mean? Malek was near perhaps; some kindness was intended me, I felt certain; Malek was about to effect my release, but how? it mattered not, I should learn more—enough I knew to build an airy castle on. The hope of freedom awoke, and filled my heart with joy. Sugar cane was a common thing for one poor Fellah to give to another in kindness, and there was in its juice a pleasant sweetness, that helped me to pleasant thoughts; soon again, perhaps, I should look some field green and gay with them. As thus I thought, a hollow juiceless joint broke between my teeth, and instead of the soft pith, something tough resisted them; it proved a small scroll of thin parchment, I durst not read it then, I hid it till the night, and it was by the bright light of a full moon, which hung, for a few moments that happy night in its passage over my cell, that pressing close to the bars I read the following note in Arabic:—
