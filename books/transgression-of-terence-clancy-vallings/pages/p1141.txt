As the chairman had encouraged free discussion of one or two moot points, there then followed a desultory debate, with fragmentary speeches put forth here and there by mumbling old men or stammering young gentlemen.

When this had progressed for some time without much advancement of business, Lord Bridistow rose on the chairman’s right hand, and plunged with characteristic abruptness into speaking.

His lordship was a square, bluff, ruddy-faced man, much respected not only as the chief magnate of the neighbourhood, but as a practical common-sense landlord who lived on his estate, and managed it better than most men.
