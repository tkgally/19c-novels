But that would be made up in due course, and then Secretan would come forth to resume his old position, the passing disgrace being slipped off like a worn-out garment.

But poor shattered Ezekiel was not cut out of the stuff Nature uses for the making of assassins, so that he could in no wise bring his mind—except in theory—to the cutting-off of his enemy in cold blood.

The very facilities offered by Secretan’s lonely ramblings in the woods helped to make such a task an impossibility. He seemed to have none of the characteristic fears of a guilty conscience; nay more, his apparent blindness to his own danger induced in Doidge a hesitation amounting almost to doubt of his guilt.
