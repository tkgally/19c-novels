The uses and functions of the building were manifold. It would serve as a concert-room for the Amateur Musical Society one night, for a religious gathering the next; for a public sale in the afternoon, a private ball in the evening. Every one with any pretensions to be a public character had stumbled through a few halting sentences in the big room over the pillars; indeed, its stage had been trodden by the shivering amateurs, musical, dramatic and other, of several generations. Of the well-known voices uplifted from time to time in the town hall, none was more popular with an audience than Mr. Tredethlyn’s. Standing upon this familiar platform, with a Chillington gathering spread out before him in one broad smile, the squire-parson was at his best.
