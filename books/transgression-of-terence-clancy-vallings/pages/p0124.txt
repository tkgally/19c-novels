In plain truth, Terence Clancy was the son of a small farmer in County Cork, whose only tenants were a few cottiers; and who perhaps received about £20 a year from this source—when rents happened to be paid. As the clever one of the family, Terence had, by dint of much scraping and the assistance of friends, been sent to an English public school, and being now fairly launched on the world, was expected to make his fortune, and become the mainstay of half a dozen little brothers and sisters.
