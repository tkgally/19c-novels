“I am sorry,” Nell rejoined with difficult steadiness.

He seemed so generous and noble, so ready to sacrifice himself, that it was very painful to have to dismiss him thus; nor had Nell any fear of herself now, feeling, as she did, so braced up for duty, so sternly and invincibly loyal. She was about to dismiss Terence for his own sake alone—and thereby injure him with her father? Mr. Tredethlyn, for all his bland good nature, was thoroughly accustomed to having his own way, and quite capable of calling in another doctor, if Terence chanced to offend him; and one seceding patient is apt to draw others after him. Once more, there was Simon to be considered. What would he think when Mr. Tredethlyn told him, as would certainly be the case, how Nell had chilled Terence into refusing this invitation? Would not even Simon’s suspicions be aroused?
