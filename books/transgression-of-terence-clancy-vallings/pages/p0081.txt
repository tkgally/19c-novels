As patron of the living, too, Simon Secretan contrived to give much offence. For on the death of the old vicar, a good easy man and true Chillingtonian—who did nothing, and died universally respected—he must needs put in Frank Nelson, an energetic young Cambridge man as exacting and tiresome as himself. Between the two Chillington grew harassed and irritable, like an over-drugged patient; and soon found itself deprived of that repose which it valued even more than mild lambing seasons and sunny harvests.
