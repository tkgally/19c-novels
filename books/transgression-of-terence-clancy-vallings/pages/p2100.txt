“I’m cool enough now,” said Ezekiel, “and I shan’t break out again. I’m gettin’ hopeful, and that does me good. With the help o’ you two I’ll get through this job; after which I don’t care what comes to me. Look here, sir”—he turned to Captain Rush as the latter resumed his seat—“you can guess what things I have put away in this desk?”

The soldier nodded gravely. Terence shuddered and palpitated. The faintness was coming over him again, and the worst moment was yet to be faced. He knew not what relics of poor Mary’s dead romance were to be laid before him. No thinkable ordeal could have been more painful to him than this that was coming. Even Doidge himself, had he known all, could hardly have devised for his enemy a sharper punishment.
