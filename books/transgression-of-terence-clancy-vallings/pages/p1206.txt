“Terence,” she asked timidly, “has something come between us? Is it to be the old miserable story? Are we going to prove the justice of the world’s miserable jeers about wedded love? You look so—so unlike yourself, and I’m so wretched, Terence. . . .”

Here she fell from quiet weeping into violent grief, so that Terence was fain to call himself a brute as well as a traitor. Yet he was incapable of soothing or caressing her. He sat with his own head bent, feeling in his degradation that he was unfit to touch her.

“Kiss me, Terence,” she sobbed; “kiss me once and say that you still love me.”

But he could make no response—and burning tears were falling from his own eyes.
