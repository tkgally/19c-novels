Mary Pethick was dressed coquettishly in a cream-coloured frock with pink ribbons peeping out of its cunning-simple folds; but her manner was dull and depressed. The pretty curves of her mouth drooped like those of a tired child as she shook hands with Terence.

“Was the pink and cream designed for me or Ezekiel?” thought he. “What ails ye at all?” he asked aloud.

“Oh, I hate the world, and every one in it—that’s all!”

“That’s a good sweeping statement, Mary, and I hope it’ll ease your mind.”

Terence perceived that his <i>rôle</i> must after all be that of listener, that his grievances must needs wait awhile.

“Pray don’t laugh at me, Mr. Clancy. I can’t bear it now, I am so miserable!”
