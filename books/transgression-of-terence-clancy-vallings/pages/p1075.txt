Just below the town the Bickington road was raised above the low-lying meadows by an embankment, down to which ran the allotment gardens pertaining to Simon’s new row of cottages. Upon reaching this point Nell and Terence looked down upon a curious scene.

The whole breadth of the gardens was converted into a muddy lake, scourged and flawed by the strong west wind; the neat row of cottages stood waist-deep in water.

Many persons had warned Secretan of such a possibility as this, but his genial agent, Jackson, had recommended the site strongly—doubtless for reasons having roots in his breeches’-pocket—and Simon’s fatal good nature had been disastrous as usual.
