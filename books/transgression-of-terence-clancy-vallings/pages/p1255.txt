“I don’t know; I don’t know,” she repeated, patting the child with her quivering hands. “My husband’s relations are offended with me; they will have none of my child. I have no relations left of my own but that leathern-hearted housekeeper of yours at the hall. She has offered to take my Rose. Mrs. Henley will not let her hunger—except for love—will see that she’s well fed and clothed—and miserable. The little one will be brought up to hate my memory. When she grows big her mother will be held up to her as a warning, and happiness will be kept from her like a poison!”

“I begin to understand——”
