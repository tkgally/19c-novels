It was a great moment for a man who had killed many trout, but never a salmon; every nerve quivered as his bright-hued lure swept round. But no boil came, no glorious jar to the tense nerves—the big fish refused to stir. He reeled up with a shaking hand and waded cautiously ashore.

“I must give him a rest,” he muttered, “then try him with a smaller fly.”

He lighted his pipe, and spread out his flies on a flat rock. Kate, quite familiar with the flies most favoured in these parts, discussed them with him. They talked together most amicably; for the immediate prospect of “killing something,” or seeing it killed, will soothe even English spleen.

After a pause, the angler waded in once more to try a small dark fly of Kate’s recommendation.

Again the rhythmic sway of the tapering greenheart; again the lengthening process.
