To-morrow was market day at Chillington, and everybody would be abroad. Here was just the opportunity his present mood craved for showing his high scorn of these false accusers. And perchance—this thought underpropped his harder ones—there would be found some few loyal enough to come forward and show that they believed in him still.

Next morning, his reckless, heroical mood still holding good, Simon proceeded to carry his promising plan into execution. Having breakfasted and received Mrs. Henley’s morning report of Sir Hamo’s health, he buckled on his proof-armour of pride, mounted his tall bay, Philanthropy, and headed him northwards. And probably no haughtier-looking gentleman had ever ridden along the ancient road to Chillington; for Simon’s bitterness, finding its way to his face, seemed to have limned it anew with a harsh hand.
