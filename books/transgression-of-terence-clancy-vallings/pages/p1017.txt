In this his dark hour a great longing towards his father took possession of Simon; he felt a strong outrush of the heart towards the old man who would now surely cease to be a stranger to his only son. A hope sprang up in Simon’s sore heart that the ice of estrangement might now at the eleventh hour be broken; that he might yet, before the old eyes closed in death, see in them the affection for which he had never ceased to look. There had been faults on his side, too; his pride had helped to estrange his father, he had exacted too much, had allowed too little for an invalid’s weakness and irritability—and all this he would freely confess.
