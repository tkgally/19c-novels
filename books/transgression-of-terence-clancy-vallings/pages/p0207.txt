While Julius was away soldiering, however—that is to say, during the greater part of the year—these servants of his took care that the old man should know his place, and keep to it. For the art of giving pain to inferior people is not confined to the upper classes; and the Squire of Bickington—whose long experience in this relation surely entitled him to form a definite opinion—at heart considered his butler, Mr. John Children, the hardest and most merciless of all his critics.

Never was conventional poor governess more systematically crushed by hard employer than was this old gentleman by the servants whom he only kept up for his son’s sake. Indeed, upon one occasion the memory of his wrongs stirred up the gentle old squire almost to the point of rebuking a fellow-man.
