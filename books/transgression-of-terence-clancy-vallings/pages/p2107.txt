Towards these little red-headed fellows Nell had played the part of a true mother, but their trickiness had somehow diluted the affection she had tried hard to give them. They were rough, noisy, and without any of Terence’s physical refinement; indeed, to be perfectly frank, they were common looking. But, had they been honest boys, Nell felt that she could have loved them well.

This kindly moral weakness of her husband’s sometimes produced a dull ache in Nell’s mind, as well as a sense of alarmed expectation. There were possibilities about him not hitherto suspected. The mind which had been to her as an open book was discovered to have dim corners into which she feared to penetrate.
