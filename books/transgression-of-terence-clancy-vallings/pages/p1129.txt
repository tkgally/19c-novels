This concentration of his thoughts upon a subject that could hardly possess much innate interest for him—seeing that his offer of a piece of meadow land had some time since been refused by the embryo club in favour of Mr. Secretan’s proferred site, and that the club was in fact a creation of Simon’s—was somewhat strange. It might have alarmed Terence, had not his thoughts for the present been strongly drawn in another direction. But for the moment he was all medical man; there were points in this patient’s physical condition that puzzled him. Allowing for the man’s morbid mental state, for the severe emotional strain lately put upon him, for some constitutional weakness easily discernible, there remained an unaccountable something, a dark corner in which Terence’s subtle mind was at present blindly groping.
