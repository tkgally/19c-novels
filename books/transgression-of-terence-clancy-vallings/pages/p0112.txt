Never had Terence been so witty and brilliant as this evening. A far duller man might have made a success if borne along, as he now was, by such a tide of favouring conditions. His courage had been stirred, his self-esteem set a-glowing, by a skilled hand; cordial greetings had made his nerves tingle pleasantly; he was thoroughly conscious of being among friends, yet sufficiently a stranger to retain the first gloss of interest and piquant newness which familiarity so quickly makes rusty; and the sharpest of all spurs to effort was pricking him—the desire to warm one special heart and dazzle one special pair of eyes.
