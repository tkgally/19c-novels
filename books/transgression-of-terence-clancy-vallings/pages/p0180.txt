Ezekiel would do nothing then; she did him the justice to believe that he would never move a finger to bar her happiness, but until then he was not to be trusted. After all, it seemed safer, wiser, almost more right, to let him continue in his mistake.

Mary felt something like a return of sympathy towards Ezekiel when she rejoined him on the bridge. There was no visible self-pity about him, and his stoicism always provoked her respect. No one ever knew this man cry out when he was hurt; his weak health—probably especially irksome to a man of such vigorous spirit—was never hinted at by himself.
