Nothing should ever drag from him the admission of his treachery to Simon; he could neither abase himself so low, nor exalt Simon to such a pitch. There was jealousy involved as well as fear; the two together over-mastered him now, and would continue to do so to the end of the chapter. His fit of despair was passing, too. The mere giving way to his emotions and outpouring of his heaviness had given him relief; a sanguine streak was already illumining his depression. He began to perceive that he had too readily given way to panic and pessimism. What danger worthy of all this fright was attachable to a few faded flowers and a book with half-erased annotations?
