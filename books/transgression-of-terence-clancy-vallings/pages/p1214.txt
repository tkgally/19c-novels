He had not, as many people imagined, been turned out of house and home by his father, for Sir Hamo had a superstitious objection to any such harsh, Roman-fatherly proceeding. Though he had never found much difficulty in bruising his son’s heart, he could not bring himself to the point of turning his person out of doors. The note delivered by Mrs. Henley in the avenue had intimated Sir Hamo’s acceptance of his son’s guilt, as proved by his silence under public accusation, and had requested that Simon should in future confine himself to the west wing of the hall, and spare his father the pain of further communication with him.
