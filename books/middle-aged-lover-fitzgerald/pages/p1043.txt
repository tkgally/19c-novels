I dare say,” added the young man, suddenly softening, as it began to dawn on him that Mr. Doughty, after all, might have been unselfish from the beginning, and was willing to sacrifice himself to see Corinna happy; “no doubt you mean well, and have acted more chivalrously than I could do, but still you don’t make allowances. You see I am in such a hole, badgered right and left, up and down; my mother, you, her family, the wretched people at Brickford—all at me. You must make allowance for a young fellow in my position. And the best of it is,” said the young man, after a struggle, “or the worst of it is, that I believe in my soul that she does not care for me after all.”

A curious eagerness came into Mr. Doughty’s eyes.
