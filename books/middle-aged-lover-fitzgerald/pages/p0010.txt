Brickford was a new, but rising manufacturing town, blushing scarlet in the healthy bloom of its cheeks; while its freshly turned clay was being newly baked into long lines of houses. The villas and substantial suburban houses of the gentry were, of course, disguised in grey plaster paletots, and furnished in a stately fashion, but there had been hard work with “the grounds,” which can never be extemporised by contractors, and could only be coaxed into what seemed a well-worn green table-cloth, fringed round with some stubbly tufts and bunches.
