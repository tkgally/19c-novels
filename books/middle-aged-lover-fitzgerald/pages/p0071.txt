Mr. Nagle then took out a rather greasy little memorandum book and searched through various pages of close writing, which affected to record the crowded engagements of the coming week. “Monday—two. No, <i>that</i> won’t do. Tuesday—four—six; hardly—though we could put <i>her</i> off. Wednesday—no we must manage Monday somehow. There,” added Mr. Nagle, in desperation at the inconvenient crush, “let us say Monday—at twelve. You shall have your appointment for that hour.”

During this pecuniary arrangement, Corinna, unlike other heroines, looked on calmly. She saw nothing indelicate or unromantic in the discussion. The young man was a little surprised. But he did not yet know her character.
