“So ends the infatuation,” he said. “Poor old fool that I have shown myself.”

“Old!” murmured Mrs. Gardiner. “What nonsense! She was not worthy of you.”

“Yes, old and imbecile. It serves me right; I could see that she considered my liking and admiration a sort of affront. What right had an elderly, commonplace being, to think of her, the divine Corinna, who keeps her affections for the young and the handsome?”
