The stagger of Mr. Nagle was really dramatic; his bewildered stare in the direction of the murderous gun told his emotion. There the whole story of the admiral’s bleeding wounds might be traced; the voice faltered; the fingers, like tottering limbs, feebly limped from note to note; the sounds seemed to choke in his throat; all the woes of England seemed to be borne on his sorrowing head. This, however, was relieved by the triumphant fashion with which he proclaimed the issue of the glorious day—the noble confession of the mother country that every man that day had done his duty—had done his d’yewty!
