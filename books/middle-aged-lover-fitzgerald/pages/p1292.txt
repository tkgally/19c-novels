“No one could grudge him that,” said Corinna. “He is the most generous and noble of men. I suppose you have heard how ill he has been?”

Mr. Dodd had only heard some rumours of this matter, and eagerly asked for details.

“But when I say,” he went on, “that, I don’t grudge him his money, I <i>do</i> take some merit for magnanimity; as there are some people who would never forgive being ‘cut out,’ as it is called, by a friend. Our common friend, who died possessed of all this wealth, assured me, only a month before his death, that he had made his will, and had left me everything. To be sure, friend Doughty saw him in the interval, and I suppose made his hay when the sun shone, that is, when he could.”
