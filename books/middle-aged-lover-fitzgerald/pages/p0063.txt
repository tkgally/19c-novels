It can hardly be said that Mr. Nagle came for devotional purposes, his attention being absorbed by a rite that was going on at the other end of the building, namely, the performance on the organ, which “old Humphries” was handling. The demeanour of the critic was infinitely scornful. At first he would look back with curiosity, as though he were disturbed at his devotions by something strange going on at that end of the building. Then he seemed to grow certain that something was wrong—something almost barbarous. His expressive face conveyed all this by a certain pantomime—elevation of the eye-brows, pitying smiles, shaking of head; with then a resolute but vain attempt to fix his attention on his devotions.
