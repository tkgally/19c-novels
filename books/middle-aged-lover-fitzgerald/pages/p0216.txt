[[cont]]watching from afar off her flushed cheek and the pride of her cavalier, bit his glove and stamped his foot impatiently.

The people about, who knew him by sight as well as they did the market cross, were looking at him with curiosity, and, as he fancied, with enjoyment, to see “how he took it.” All this was infinitely mortifying and irritating. The spectacle of Corinna, thus followed with admiration and envy, led up by her devoted admirer, to be dazzled by such homage with all the splendours of wealth about to be laid at her feet, was too much for him. He was not a profound analyst of his own emotions, or, indeed, of emotions in general, and he set this feeling down to deep passion, and perhaps to jealousy. Perhaps it was the supremacy of his rival which amused, and was seen by, that large assemblage, that really disturbed him.
