“A telegram, miss,” he said; “sent on here from your house.”

“Good gracious, Miss Nagle,” said the musical young member of the Macallum Minstrels. “I hope there’s nothing wrong. ’Pon my word, should be so sorry.”

In much trepidation, Corinna hurried into the cloak-room, and read:—

| “From | To |
| William Gardiner, | Corinna Nagle, |
| Brickford. | London. |

“They have seized on poor Doughty, and to-morrow are going to take him away, and confine him in a mad-house. They have had me arrested to get me out of the way. There is no one here to help him or to save him. Come down at once, like a brave, honest girl, and I believe that you will defeat them all.”
