All that I have passed through during these few weeks would have fitted me for a mad-house. Don’t irritate Doctor Spooner,” added he, shading his eyes. “He will visit it on me if you go away.”

“I am not going away,” said Will Gardiner; “never fear. I shall return, and stay here for the night. Let anyone dare keep me out at their peril. You wish me to be with you?”

“Yes, yes. I have plans, and a great deal of business to arrange. I want to devote such little money as I have to a charitable purpose. Before I die I suppose I shall end by doing like other weak-minded beings, endow cats, and dogs, and colleges of all kinds.”
