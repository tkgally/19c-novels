But they hoped to be repaid by some such recommendations as we have described. Dobson, indeed, had won the favour of his patron by his remarkable self-abnegation and humility, and was spoken of in Nagle circles as “a very worthy, hard-working creature, to whom it was a charity to give a job. No one could be better if you wanted a puddler”—a metaphor, it may be presumed, drawn from one of the most laborious operations of the iron manufacture.
