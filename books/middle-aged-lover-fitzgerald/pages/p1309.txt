The whole position of affairs, indeed, now offered the strangest contrast to the state of things when our characters were first introduced to the reader. An amiable virtuoso, whom nobody thought much of—a humble music-master and his daughter timorously trying to make their way with some polite average ladies and gentlemen of society clustering round—these were the acting figures. Now, the “amiable virtuoso” had become the victim of a conspiracy; the music-master’s daughter had become a heroine, and been driven out on the world, while the average ladies and gentlemen had changed into fiercely contending parties, each striving to carry out their ends without scruple or remorse.
