Braham Nagle was a right good fellow, and they must ask him to dine the next time, and not have him coming sneaking in in the evening, like a fiddler coming to play at a ball. As a matter of course, Nagle must “form” the voices of the Pollys, Lucys, and Marys of the family, who were, indeed, already under instruction at the hands of Miss Parker, the governess; and Will was now pressing his brother Jack to secure Mr. Nagle’s musical services.

“Have Nagle,” he would say to every one; “have Nagle at once, if you want style, finish, and the true touch. He was taught by Braham. By the way, I’ll tell you as good a story as ever you heard in the whole course of your life about Braham, which Nagle told us the other night.”
