[[cont]]warm discussion was likely to arise between father and daughter, and having accomplished the charitable object of her visit, now took her leave. Mr. Nagle then broke out with considerable heat.

“Oh, fiddle-de-dee!” he said. “I can’t have this nonsense! I want no stepping-stones, as you call ’em. My name and reputation are pretty well established. I have fought my own way, ma’am. Ask anyone who Braham Nagle is. I am not quite so foolish as you would make me out. The whole failure is owing to your own fault. We might have had Old Doughty at this moment, I firmly believe, only for your high-flown romance.”

“Yes,” said Corinna, “and at this moment he would have despised me, and have been persuaded that we wanted him only for his money. I could not have endured that. I cannot endure it!”
