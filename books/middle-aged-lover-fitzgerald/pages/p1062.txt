Will Gardiner’s mouth suddenly took a sour expression.

“I saw her,” he said, “in the hall. You have lost no time, ma’am; but I ought to mention that Mrs. Gardiner has chosen some one who belongs to the place, who is more at home here. She attended her in her illness.”

“Now that was so thoughtful of her! So like her, always thinks of everything! After all, it is not much matter, provided it be a safe person. And—she was ill? Dear me! not a fever, I hope?”

“No, her confinement,” said Mr. Gardiner, readily.
