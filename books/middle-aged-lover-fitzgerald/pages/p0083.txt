He came once or twice to dine, literally as overpowered by the obstreperous pressing of the impetuous Will, as though he had been seized, bound, and brought away to the dinner-table, but he did not make a favourable impression. He had a small, clear, sharp voice, and his comments on the rallying speeches of the ladies were cold, distrustful, and even sarcastic. He was pronounced to be “a curmudgeon,”—he had “snarled” and “yapped” all through dinner. When one of the girls went to the piano, he gently expressed his disapproval.
