“We are in,” he would say with a sort of unction, “for a downright serious piece of business.” Everything was cast upon his shoulders; he would have to see to everything. And as a visible, tangible sign of responsibility which he styled “awful,” as well as a sign of his being in possession and clothed with office, a little bed, or, as he called it, “a stretcher,” was sent up that very afternoon to the house—an article of furniture that figured conspicuously in the many dramatic narratives of the exciting crisis that he furnished to his friends.

Mrs. Nagle was despatched to relieve guard, when duty compelled his own absence for a short while. But it must be said that he threw his professional duties completely overboard, save in a few choice instances where, though he attended, he did not give much satisfactory instruction.
