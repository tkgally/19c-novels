In seclusion he looks upon himself and others as seen afar off, in a more imposing form. ‘<i>L’isolement grandit tout</i>,’ as they say in the language of this country.

My cottage and its surrounding fields are separated from the town of Lausanne by some undulating hills, that shut out from me the importunateness, but not the convenience of the crowded haunts of man. My vines and pastures slope southward to the lake, in beautiful and convenient inequalities. A miniature sea-shore, strewn with fine sand and gravel, sweeps round my little territory, receiving, not repelling the gentle waves that scarcely oppress a wild flower with their angriest spray.[[fnref *]]
