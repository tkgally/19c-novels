The rider was somewhat bent, but he kept his saddle like one long used to it; his white hair floated on his shoulders, between his broad brimmed hat and the cloak peculiar to his calling; his countenance wore an expression of resignation peculiar to itself, and had often interested me. I now ventured to ask him whether it was fatigue or the news of the morning that made him look so grave.

“Nay, noble sir,” he answered, “my old limbs have become almost as unconscious of fatigue, as my mind of fear. I know indeed that the people do furiously rage together against our anointed king, but I do also know that they imagine a vain thing.”

“May it be so!” I exclaimed; “but tell me, reverend Sir, how long is it since you have lost your coadjutor at the Castle?”
