“So well,” I replied, “that I acquit you of all blame. Let us, then, as true comrades, forget all else for the present, except how can we best stand by and assist each other. If I fail you, believe me to be the villain that you thought me yesterday.”

For the first time since our acquaintance, Arnold’s features assumed a kindly—almost a gentle expression.

“I would, that I had known thee better and sooner;” he said, as with a sorrowing but a manful gaze he examined our forlorn position. He shook hands with me in silence; wrote some lines on a leaf of his tablet and placed it carefully in my hunting frock. Then, he said, solemnly:
