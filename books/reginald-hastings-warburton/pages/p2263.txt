# Chapter XIII.

<verse>
No resting could he finde at all,
No ease, nor heart’s content;
No house, no home, no biding place:
But wandering forth he went
From towne to towne in foreign landes.
With grieved conscience still,
Repenting for the heinous guilt
Of his forepassed ill.
</verse>
[[right]] <sc>the wandering jew</sc> (<sc>ancient ballad</sc>).

I<sc>n</sc> the painful sense of vacuity that followed, I turned with feverish interest toward the Puritan, in order to distract my thoughts. For the last few days I had ministered to his wants in silence, with which he seemed well satisfied. I now told him that I had been attending the funeral of a dear friend, but for the future that I could devote more time to my duties as his host.
