It was sometime before I could realize to my imagination, that I was actually, not only disinherited, but that I owed to another family all the money, justly theirs, that I or my father had ever spent. We had even supplied the King’s necessities with another’s wealth; but this alone I did not feel called upon to regret. Had it not been for our loyalty, our home had never been pillaged and ransacked, or our false position revealed to us by the discoveries that were then made there.

For some time the sudden consciousness of poverty weighed very heavily on my thoughts, but I soon flung off that care; I felt proudly that I was able to work my own passage across the Sea of Life. Nine-tenths of my fellow-men were doing the same thing, cheerfully and manfully,—and why not I?
