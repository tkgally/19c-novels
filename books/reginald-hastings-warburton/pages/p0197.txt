# Chapter XIII.

<verse>
Fain would I go to chafe his paly lips
With twenty thousand kisses, and to drain
Upon his face an ocean of salt tears,
To tell my love unto his dumb deaf trunk,
And with my finger feel his hand unfeeling.
</verse>

[[right]] <sc>Shakspere</sc>.

<verse>
Mas invidia he de vos, conde.
</verse>

<verse>
I rather envy thee thy doom,
Than mourn for thee, or pity thee;
For such an honourable death
Is glory’s immortality.—<sc>Lope de Sosa</sc>.
</verse>

W<sc>hen</sc> I returned to consciousness, I found myself lying on a bed of leaves, over which a soldier’s cloak had been carefully arranged. I was in a tent, and through the foldings of the canvas door I could see the stars, with freezing brightness, shining down upon the battle-plain below.
