# Chapter XIII.

<verse>
Whether first nature, or long want of rest,
Hath wrought my soul to this I cannot tell,
But horrors are not now displeasing to me.
I like this rocking of the battlements,
The lightning’s glaring and the thunder’s roar,
Give pleasure to my eye and music to my ear.
</verse>

O<sc>nce</sc> more, borne back by recollection and too faithful memory: I am young, hopeful, and enterprising, once more I imagine myself imprisoned in the Castle of Nottingham. The langour of long sickness has passed away, and my cell is grown quite cheerful in my eyes; for expectation has shone into it, and the means of escape are open before me.
