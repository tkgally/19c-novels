I once thought otherwise,—I once thought that when my spirit was frozen by despair, it was a calm from pleased Heaven that brooded over it. When I smote that unhappy man, I felt even so! I walked contentedly in front of the tumultuous crowd that gathered round him. I felt that they could not harm me—and they did not. In vain was the bitter anger and vengeance of England’s King; in vain was torture threatened; I told my Lord of Dorset, who thought that steel and fire could wring secrets out of a soul like mine! I told him that he was the only man I should accuse—and he threatened it no more.
