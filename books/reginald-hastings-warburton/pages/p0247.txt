For Lady d’Aubigny he had some tale of a political intrigue, whose dramatis personæ were made to amuse all the company, whilst its purport was confidentially dropped into her private ear. To Hugo he told, in most touching terms, the sad story of the gallant young Fielding’s unhappy failure and his doom. Even for me, on whose brow he saw a cloud lowering, he endeavoured to frame a palateable eulogy for the late affair at Lichfield; but finding me ungraciously disposed, he turned haughtily away and addressed himself to Phœbe. Then I became more deeply interested, and argued ill from the very fact of his postponing his address to her as to the least doubtful hearer in the room.
