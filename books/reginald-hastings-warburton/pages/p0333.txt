Did the soldiers fire too soon and mistake their aim—or what? A volley was heard; Ditchley, with a bullet in his brain, fell prostrate at my feet: the musketeers disappeared; shots came quick and fast and all around me—then loud shouts, and the tramp of cavalry with Rupert’s war-cry; then I knew that the royal horse were in among the enemy, and that I was saved. Rough and stern, however, was the conflict all around; when, out from the mass, burst a bold horseman, who swept his gleaming sword above my head, high in air, and cut the accursed cord whence my poor page hung suspended almost over me. All this passed far more rapidly than I can tell, and before the brief strife around was ended, I had Bryan’s form resting in my arms, and the faint struggles, that had filled me with horror, now thrilled me with delighted hope.
