When I had stopped to breathe my horse, or been abandoned by the Roundheads for some easier opponent, there was Blount, too, quiet and observant; wiping his huge moustache, it might be, with his well-worn gauntlet, or arranging some strap about me that had been disordered.

It was during some such momentary pause that Goring came sweeping by, his horse all foam, and maddened with many a wound; but he himself calm and collected.

“Ha! loiterers!” he exclaimed, with an unimpassioned, but tremendous oath. “Is this a time for rest? Follow me, as ye love honour and fear the Provo-Marshal.” I dashed forward, and found myself among some fifty troopers, that the General had collected along the field. Robinson the player rode beside me.
