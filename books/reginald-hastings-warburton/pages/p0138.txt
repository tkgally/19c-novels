“There be our picket,” exclaimed the Roundhead, in a loud voice, that was meant less for information than alarm. In a moment the Pedlar stepped lightly back, leaped into Bryan’s saddle, and was off like lightning.

“Pursue the villain!” I cried to Blount, at the same time placing myself between the fugitive and the little party who were about to fire. “Back to your post!” I shouted to their leader, “and give the alarm.”

Sir John Gell’s musqueteers were not then so ready with their weapons as they afterwards became, and for a moment they paused irresolutely, notwithstanding the voice of their sergeant, who roared out hoarsely: “Fire! fire! fire on the sons of Belial, one and all!”

As I plunged into the hollow, a scattered volley rang about my ears, and long afterwards I could hear the loud voices and ringing armour of the Roundheads borne on the breeze.
