Our ride thenceforth was exhilarating in the highest degree: the cool, bright night of a warm day affords delicious travelling; our spirits were raised by our late success, and I observed to Digby, that danger afforded an almost intoxicating sense of pleasure. “Ay,” he replied: “<i>le péril est comme le vin; il monte la tête</i>, as we say in France.”

It was past midnight when we approached Lincoln, which being in the hands of the insurgents, we were obliged to avoid, and make a détour by Hartisholme, where we crossed the Fesdik. On the outskirts of the village we stopped to breathe our horses, as we had still at least thirteen miles to travel before we could reach Newark, and we had scarcely drawn rein since we parted from Phœbe, by the forest of Beaumanoir.
