Meanwhile, Bryan had unbuckled his saddle-bags, and produced therefrom a couple of wedges of deer-pie, some marchpane, and a case bottle of rare old wine, on which we supped right heartily. Blount, who had quite resumed his old soldier habits, lighted his pipe, and led the horses to and fro, as he kept watch. Bryan all this while was employing himself busily with my hat, and when he returned it to me, to my surprise, the lost plume waved over it in all its former pride.

“There’s a small taste of it missing,” said the boy, apologetically; “but I thought as the heron that wore the feather before your honour wore it, was killed by the lady’s hawk, you wouldn’t like to have it trodden under foot by the canting old rebel that shot it off you.”

“Then you were by my side, you foolish boy, when the Roundheads fired on me!”
