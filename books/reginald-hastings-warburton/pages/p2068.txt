I omit the details of our voyage and our adventures, afterwards, on our road to Oxford. There, we found great changes impending, but no more prospect of a termination to the war than the former year had opened with. The Scots were about to enter England on the North, in order to combine with the Parliamentary forces under Fairfax; but then a stout reinforcement of troops, recalled from Ireland, nearly counterbalanced the new danger. A parliament of sixty peers and three-hundred Commons had assembled in Christ-church, and voted one hundred thousand pounds, which was nearly raised.
