The northern front, however, of Castle Bifrons had been completed after the new power of the Puritans had displayed itself, and no contrast could be stronger than that exhibited between this recent building and the former one. It consisted indeed of nothing but a flat brick face, parsimoniously pierced with narrow windows, not an ornament, not even a mullion, could be there detected. In front of it was a square space of close-cropped turf, surrounded, as by a wall, with tall stiff dark trees; and varied only by formal gravel walks. The sun never shone upon the high and narrow hall door that opened out upon these solemn precincts, and there was never seen a living thing that could regret his absence. No bird was ever known to sing, or grasshopper to chirp there: some dismal old rooks, with a few pensive owls and bats, were the only creatures that voluntarily addicted themselves to what Sir Janus considered the true Puritanical taste in architecture and landscape gardening.
