Thus ended the story of my brother’s fate. Since then I have been an altered man. The war that I loathed has become my great interest; my heart is wrapped up in it. I love fighting for its own fierce sake, and my name is become a terror to the enemy. I seem to bear a charmed life; bullets glance from my armour, swords wielded by the stoutest arm give way before the sweep of mine, and my vengeful war-cry finds no return. All things seem possible to me in battle; I chafe only because there is so little to be done, and that little seems such child’s play.

At the storm of Bristol I led the forlorn. As we rushed along, I heard the death-cry of half my party. We bounded over the fallen stones, and won the breach. A new parapet had been raised within, and my stormers recoiled from the steady line of fire, and the palisado of pikes that defended it. That <i>was</i> a moment of high excitement, and it carried me through. I rushed at the parapet to leap it, but a halberd thrust me back without a wound.
