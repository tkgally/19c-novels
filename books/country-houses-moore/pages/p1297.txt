Lord Honiton was, when he married, a very young man, deeply enamoured of his wife’s beauty and fascination, and whilst his father was lord-lieutenant, she was the star of the vice regal-court. Soon after that Lord Ottery (as he then was) stood a contested election for Honiton, and lost it, which greatly added to his father’s embarrassment; and his dying soon after, his son, with his beautiful wife and a young family, found himself obliged to live with them in great retirement at Broadcliff, in Devonshire.

Had Lady Honiton been capable of educating her children, it would have been a fortunate and favourable situation, but beyond speaking French, she could not even assist; her own occupation was chiefly beautiful needle-work, she had therefore no other idea of education than having a French governess; the one she got was a good creature, spoke pure French, and could teach a little geography and music.
