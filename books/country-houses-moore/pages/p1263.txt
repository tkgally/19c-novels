Lord Cockermouth encouraged her, but she did not do much better with her other arrows; he shot more than once into the bull’s eye.

Great skill was shewn by many of the party. Lady Adelaide was the last, she liked that, because it increases the interest; she shot better than her sister, but was so much more intent on doing it gracefully, and gaining admiration for the elegance of her figure, that her utmost effort only put one arrow in the petticoat.

The whole company saw with surprise, not the devotion of Sir Godfrey Blisset, for Lady Adelaide might claim any devotion; but the way in which it was received by her ladyship, and the easy and familiar footing in which he seemed in the family of a proud and ambitious woman, for such was the character Lady Honiton bore.
