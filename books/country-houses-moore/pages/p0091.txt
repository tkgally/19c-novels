When the party from the library returned to the drawing-room after prayers, they found those they had left, that is, the foreigners, and Major MacVenom, in a grand discussion on the everlasting topic of the London University, the latter maintaining the advantages of knowledge <i>without religious principles</i>, and extolling the <i>liberality</i> of that education which was unencumbered with the <i>prejudices</i> of mankind, and roundly asserting, in contradiction to sound reason and experience, that those who <i>selected their own mode of faith</i> were likely to embrace it with more ardour than those on whom it was imposed in their early years, forgetting the unwillingness of human nature to impose on itself painful restraints, and the probability that, in such a
