“Do, Lady Tintern, let me go up with you, if you are going to see Miss Legh, I am a most excellent doctress, I am sure all this is too much for her, and you know <i>I</i> can read her part to night to save her.”

All these apparently kind offers Lady Tintern decidedly refused, saying,

“Really Mrs. De Cameron you are making a mountain out of nothing, I have seen Miss Legh, who is only a little tired with her ride, as I often am, and indeed better horse-women; my little mare is not so easy as she appears to be; I must go up to her, not to enquire after her health, for that I am persuaded is perfect; but I desired her not to dress, as she was not coming down to dinner, but to put her costume as Cathos on at once, and I am going to see if that is as it should be; but if you will be so good as to go to the theatre, and see how they have managed the lamps, I will join you there, before I change my dress.”
