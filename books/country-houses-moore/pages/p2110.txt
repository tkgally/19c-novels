Not very long after Miss Talbot had taken her leave, Miss Briggs came to remind Mrs. Beaumont that they had engaged to go and hear a lecture given at Willis’s rooms on the Greek and Italian poets, and to settle a dispute of whether the Thessalian Mythology was or was not <i>imaginative</i>.

“Oh,” cried Mrs. Beaumont, “I cannot go, I am sick of such questions, they only puzzle me, I have really got a horrid nervous head-ache, which only quiet will cure, but do you go if you like it.”

“Shall I take Emily?”

“Oh, no, I may want her to answer notes, I begin to think it will do her no good, as she is to make her fortune, as it is called, in the India market, to fill her head with <i>science</i> and <i>belles lettres</i>; in the country to which she is destined, women have not energy enough <i>to think</i>; but the carriage is at your service; you will be home, I conclude, before five o’clock, for I think perhaps a little stroll in Kensington Gardens before dinner may relieve my head.”
