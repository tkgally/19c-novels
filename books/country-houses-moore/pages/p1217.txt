“That,” replied Mr. Blinco, “was the only proof he gave of bad taste, that was a superb gem, indeed! fit for an Emperor.”

“Pray,” said Mr. Musgrove, “may I ask what sort of a gem the Noah’s ark was?”

“Something unique,” replied Mr. Blinco, “a sardonyx, with the animals in white, the yellow left for the ground.”

Mr. Musgrove laughed, and, on Mrs. De Clifford asking him why he laughed, said, “he could give the history of that same sardonyx, and if he had the power, it might be made as amusing as the opal of Anne of Giernstein.”

“Pray let us have it,” said Mrs. De Clifford, “I doat on a romantic history.”

“I cannot give it till I know what interest Mr. Blinco has in the gem,” said Mr. Musgrove.

“Oh, none in the world, I only thought it worth any man’s money.”
