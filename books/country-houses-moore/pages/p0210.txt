# Chapter V.

P<sc>ope</sc> says that “little hearts will flutter at a beau.” What must the fluttering be of one so new to the world, so unsuspicious of its treacherous ways, as Emma Legh? Those who may be disposed to doubt the ignorance and simplicity of mind in a young girl who had been at one of the fashionable <i>finishing schools</i> in London, where nothing is pretended to be done to regulate the heart, where only <i>shewy</i> accomplishments—<i>shewy</i> manners, and a good carriage, are attended to, by the lady at the head of such establishments, and where the inferior parts are filled up chiefly by foreign teachers.
