“But I forget,” said Hawker, “they left Brighton above a week ago, and are gone to spend Christmas somewhere, I forget where; and, faith! I believe it was the loss of Lady Henshawe’s dinners, and Agnes’ smiles, that made Brighton so dull.”

They parted, and Frederic, on his way to his hotel, pondered on this account of Brighton, and of Lady Henshawe and her daughter, and he was puzzled to the greatest degree. When arrived at Stevens’s, and safe in his room, he took out the letter, the post mark was certainly <i>Brighton</i>, though it was a little blotted. Yet, according to Hawker’s account, they were not there, and he said nothing about Lord G——’s attentions. He must find him out again, and elicit something more on the subject. But he did not know Hawker’s lodgings, and he was such an any-where-ian he hardly knew where to look for him.
