“I am glad you find him so,” said Miss O’Brian. “Adieu <i>au revoir</i>.”

Mr. Dallas settled the boat business admirably, and Lady Honiton found him so knowing in archery, having been at several of the great meetings in Cheshire, that when he took leave of them at their own door, after Kensington Gardens, as he had an engagement, it was arranged that he should get some bows and arrows from Larner’s the next day, and write to Cheatham Hill for a further supply; and come to them for an hour, before they set off for Coomb, and make them a little <i>au fait</i> to attitudes, &c., and they could practice before they returned to town.

Miss O’Brian only came in for one moment whilst Mrs. Barton waited at the door.

The following morning Mr. Dallas gave the promised lesson, and as every house in the square, on that side, was empty, they practised in the garden, and it would be a capital amusement where they were going.
