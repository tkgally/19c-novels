Miss Briggs ran herself out of breath on the subject of the Muse, and before Mrs. Beaumont could get in a word, had another wonder to talk of, Madam de Coütza, she was a phisiologist, craniologist, physiognomist united; could tell from seeing the model of a <i>nose</i> the disposition of a person; from that of a <i>forehead</i> the strength of the understanding; and from one of the <i>mouth</i>, the temper. A large table at Miss Jenkins’ was covered with models, and drawings also, of these features, eye-brows, that displayed the sensibility of the owner; but above all, for curiosity, were the models of <i>ears</i>, a feature in general scarcely thought of, but now described to be of the greatest importance in making the character; a round small ear indicated a great <i>amiability</i>; a long ear <i>understanding</i>; an ear setting out from the head, and large, displaying <i>folly</i>; in short, Miss.
