“Oh! no!” cried Mrs. Beaumont languidly, “I am tired of those sort of meetings, they don’t get one on, at least in the circle I wish to move in. But do <i>you</i> go if you like it; make my excuses.”

Miss Briggs knew her friend too well to urge the thing.

“You will ride to day, Emily, will you not?”

“If you please, dear aunt.”

“I always please that you, and every one I love, should be amused in their own way.”

“But <i>you</i> will be alone all the morning!” said Miss Briggs.

“So much the better, I don’t feel the thing, <i>quiet</i> is the best for me, and I dare say I shall have people drop in.”

“For heaven’s sake, if you feel the least unwell why not send for Warren?” replied Miss Briggs.

“Oh, he would only laugh at my complaints, as he does at any that are not dangerous.”

“Then let Western see you, he may think of some little nervous medicine that may restore you.”
