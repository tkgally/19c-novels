By the way though! who is it we are to have here this year, to <i>make believe</i>, as the children say, that we are <i>merry</i> at Christmas? I must know what fare <i>I</i> am to have, or rather who is to cut up <i>with</i> all the beef and mutton, hares and pheasants, collected in the larder, and so disgustingly exposed to view when one passes near the offices, before I can determine whether I shall stay here, or go with George Neville to Brighton; though, to own the truth, the former would best suit my pocket and my stud too, for Judy is as lame as a tree; I have blistered and turned out Flibbertygibbet; and I don’t believe the brown horse can go the journey alone.”

“When you have finished your soliloquy, or rather thought aloud for the edification of the rest of the family,” said Lady Vernon, “I am ready to answer your question, if you really wish for an answer, though perhaps you have only been killing your own time, and interrupting mine. We expect Mr. and Lady Lucy Latour, and their two daughters.”

“Precious souls! Duly primed I suppose, by my Lady, to set their caps at me.”
