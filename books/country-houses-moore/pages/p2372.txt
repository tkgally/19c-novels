“She would, indeed, and I believe he has wished it most sincerely, and used his utmost endeavours to induce her; but she can never marry, I am persuaded she never can.”

“But why?”

“Because her affections are buried in the grave of one who would have made her happy had his life been spared.”

“May I ask who?” said Caroline.

“Have you never heard of Mr. Carleton, to whom your aunt was attached, and he to her from their earliest years? He had no fortune, but he had considerable abilities, and he exerted them in that most arduous profession—the law, and had almost arrived at the point when even <i>prudence</i> might have united them; when, on the circuit, he was siezed with a fever which prevailed in the county jail, and in three days, before any intelligence could reach your aunt, he was no more!”

“Oh, my poor dear aunt,” exclaimed Caroline, “how could she support such a calamity?”
