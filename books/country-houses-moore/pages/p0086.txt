Her sister, under the instructions of Grace, was learning to model figures in a composition only to be got at Paris, of which we shall have more to say hereafter, and was making great progress in painting in <i>guauche</i>, an art little known in England.

When Emily Vernon, Louisa Grey, and Margaret Sedley, were gone to the schools, the rest of the party (with the exception of Frederic, who went home to <i>do nothing</i>, only <i>pretending</i> to write letters) walked to view some ruins of which Grace Latour had made a sketch from memory, and she wished, cold as it was, to take another look at them, to ascertain if what she had done was correct.

As Frederic walked from the stable to the house, and from the fire to the window of the library, he could not help wishing that he <i>could</i> be amused by such trifles as other people were—was it that he was wiser, and required something that had more mind in it? Alas, we are compelled to confess that this feeling was not superior <i>wisdom</i>, but a sort of selfish fastidiousness
