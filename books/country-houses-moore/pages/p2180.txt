He bought at unheard of prices, <i>statues</i>, <i>pictures</i>, <i>mosaics</i>, and <i>cameos</i>, and had now, finally, taken a house in the Regent’s Park, to deposit, and to shew off his treasures. What a <i>treasure</i> he was in himself! rich! a widower! and only two small children, a girl and a boy; really a lady of a certain, or rather <i>uncertain</i> age, like our amiable Briggs, must have been more than mortal to resist such a combination. How very, <i>very</i> fortunate it was, that <i>she</i> should go to the Opera on that particular night; and how doubly so that she should have on <i>that</i> very beautiful becoming hat. This singular piece of good fortune completely reconciled her to not being invited to Mrs. Ormskirk’s ball, and the Duchesse de Dindon’s <i>ambigu</i>; they might have done nothing for her!

She was seen, hat and all, from the pit, and the Van himself speedily made his way to Mrs. Beaumont’s box; he paid his respects to <i>her</i>, as its mistress, and asked permission to wait on her, which was readily granted; but it was Miss Briggs he was eager to seek, she had been his principal acquaintance abroad, Mrs. Beaumont had merely tolerated and laughed at him.
