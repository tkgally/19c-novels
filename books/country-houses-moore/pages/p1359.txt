“Why, to be sure,” said Mrs. Bartlet, cautiously, “there has been some finishing and fitting up.”

“And who is to be the new Countess?”

“Really,” returned the housekeeper, “that is more than I can tell, and that she is to be the Countess, is more than I can guess.”

“Bless me, Mrs. Bartlett, you don’t mean to insinuate you are expecting an <i>improper</i> lady?”

“God forbid, sir!” replied Mrs. Bartlet indignantly, and colouring “I wonder how you could suppose <i>I</i> should think of staying a moment under such circumstances; indeed, my lord is much too good to have such an idea, not that <i>I</i> should very much like a young countess, I who have been, as I may say, for almost twenty years mistress here, I should not like to be hectored with the whims of any young lady.”
