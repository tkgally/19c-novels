“Is he of that sort?” asked his companion?

“Yes, I believe so; he has fought half a dozen duels, and, probably, carries a stiletto.”

“But you did not used to mind those trifles?”

“Nor do I now, if the thing is worth running the risk?”

“But is it not so; the world says she has twenty thousand a year?”

“Pretty near I dare say, but she would not have it, if I made her Mrs. Lyttleton, and gave her the future advantage of being a Baroness.”

“How so?”

“Why, man, you are as dull as a drum, she loses her jointure if she marries!”

“Oh! oh! then Beaumont Hastings comes in for it, does he?—then, why does not he marry her, it would be the best possible speculation, he would then be <i>tenant in possession</i>.”

“Just because he is, or rather was, her husband’s <i>nephew</i>; so it is against the canonical law.”

“Then why does not he make some bargain with her, release her from the bond, for half her revenue.”

“That is no easy matter, though it might be done, if she had a very strong fancy to marry.”

“Well! and has she not, to that count what do you call him, or does he not know the circumstances she is under?”
