Mrs. Villiars’s grief had all that uncontrollable emotion, that persons used to give way to every impulse indulge in: one of her first desires was that her dear Julia should be written to—but as she was in Dublin, and her letters of course at the mercy of the winds, Miss Shirley suggested the good sense of waiting a day or two for more favourable intelligence.

Mrs. Villiars caught at the idea of more a favorable report, and, by degrees, in the course of the evening, though she suffered much from her paroxisms of grief, she clung to hope, notwithstanding she was made fully sensible of the dangerous situation of her son; but there are some bouyant natures that “hope where hope is lost,” if those they love are concerned, and who may be said to <i>believe</i> on all occasions as they <i>wish</i>.
