She had besides a smattering of science, sufficient to talk of Botany, Chemistry, and Geology—knew a few terms in each—and always asked questions for <i>information</i>, which flattered those who knew more, and was most scrupulously careful never to commit herself. She generally found it answer best to devote her attention to some distinguished <i>savant</i> or <i>would be savant</i>—who wanted a listener, and on the authority of such people, being pronounced a <i>very clever woman</i>, she lived on that reputation. She avoided women’s society as much as possible, affected to despise her own sex, and when she did mix in general society, usually talked to the least informed—took a decided and dictatorial tone with—Perhaps you do not know this! or have never seen that! Well then, <i>I have</i>, and can tell you. Of course the women of her own standing <i>hated her</i>—the younger ones laughed at her—the young men either joined in the ridicule, or fled from her as they would have done from a scorpion.
