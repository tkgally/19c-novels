Emma thought she should be a better judge when the house was fuller, which it would be for the play, a few nights hence, and she could tell if Lord John’s attention to her was more than the common place of his usual habit.

She could not very well analyze his conversation, she was not yet much used to be talked to by young men, especially of his grade; she did not know how far they would go, and yet <i>mean nothing</i>:—she was determined to err on the right side, and believe <i>too little</i> instead of too much, and however some of his conversation might have stolen its way to her heart, her understanding reasoned against it.

She wished for her father’s protection, and yet he would be an awkward person in the society where she was now placed, and he would not understand its ways, as she was beginning to do.

She rose this morning fortified with the idea that she had better, as much as possible, avoid Lord John, till she was <i>sure</i> he had nothing of the Cobra di Capella about him.
