“But,” said Lord Ottery, “we can have no <i>absolution</i> from God, nothing to <i>comfort</i> us, nothing to <i>assure</i> us we are pardoned.”

“And what,” returned his father, “is the word of a man, of a Pope—or any of his clergy? Every man sees things through the medium of his own understanding and passions, and by them he judges others.”

“That I deny,” said Lord Ottery, “they have <i>infallible</i> rules of faith, by which they judge.”

“By whom were their rules or articles of faith constructed?” said Lord Honiton. “By Pope Pius IV., who was as fallible, though perhaps not quite so vicious, as some of his predecessors.”

“But,” replied his son, “there is <i>comfort</i> and <i>security</i> in the Catholic faith, you have no idea of; you have, under that, <i>no doubts</i> whether your sins are pardoned, your pardon is signed and sealed by the vicar of Christ and his delegates.”
