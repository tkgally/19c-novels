Under these almost hopeless circumstances, she suggested to Mrs. Forester that she thought, if the reports continued so unfavourable, it would be better that General Forester should be informed that his presence in England might be necessary.

“Oh no!” exclaimed Mrs. Forester. “Why should you send for <i>him</i>, he can do no good, I think he is much better where he is; and I am sure I don’t know what I should do with Julia and her governess, if he brings them over, and he cannot leave them behind in Dublin.”

The tone in which this was said, even more than the words, gave Miss Shirley an unfavourable impression, but she dropped the subject.

Colonel Wetheral came as usual, and, before he went away, told both Miss Shirley and Miss Neville that he felt he had seen his poor friend Villiars for the last time; that his medical attendant thought him sinking fast; that his lower extremities were paralyzed, which perhaps had somewhat relieved the head.
