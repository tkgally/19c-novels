[[cont]]“your <i>treasure trouve</i> is of small value, perhaps the letters may tell whose are the portraits.”

But on looking at them, one packet was in a manly hand directed to Araminta, and the other in a delicately small female one, inscribed to Christobel.

“I don’t think love letters, for such they seem to be, will yield you, my men, much profit, though they might be turned to some account in the hands of the great Scotch author, but here is a sovereign for you, which I fancy is something more than any Jew broker would give you for these pictures, or the silver of the casket, and take my word for it, if they turn out worth more, or find an owner, you shall have the advantage of it.”

The carpenters took the money and withdrew; and though a quarter of an hour before they would have thought it great good luck to divide a sovereign between them, so quickly had imagination magnified the prize they had found, that they returned to their work disappointed, to find themselves only twenty shillings the richer for the adventure. Imaginary wealth is always boundless, nothing short of Aladdin’s lamp.
