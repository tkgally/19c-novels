To lose a beloved and amiable son is a grief only parents can estimate. This affliction to Lord Honiton had deep aggravation. He was not so intolerant as to doubt but that a sincere Catholic might be an object of God’s mercy, and obtain everlasting life. He hoped that a merciful God would pardon the errors of his creatures; but were his son’s <i>involuntary</i> errors? had he not been brought up in a <i>purer</i> faith, and had not <i>love</i>, an overwhelming affection, destroyed his principles, and perverted his mind? this was a bitter reflection, but he earnestly prayed the God of all mercy to pardon him.

The first person who visited Lord Honiton in his affliction was the good Bishop of Carlisle. His conversation had as much comfort in it, as the case would admit of.
