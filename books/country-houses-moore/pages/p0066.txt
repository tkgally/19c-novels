Some of the country neighbours denominated natives will be occasionally added to the party: of these a Sir John Sedley, (for, as Pelham says, “Baronets hang together like bees,”) his eldest son and two daughters came to dinner the day after Christmas day; the young ladies passably agreeable, for young women who had been educated in the country, went little to London, and <i>never to Paris</i>. And unexpectedly, as the dinner bell rang, arrived Major Mac Venom; these gave the party a new turn; the worthy baronets talked over the probable business at the quarter sessions, to which they were going next day.

London was empty, but Major Mac Venom was brimful of the London University, and the numerous advantages those who could neither read nor write gained by lectures on every abstruse science, and how learnedly the London mechanics talked of hydrostatics, pneumatics, and a hundred other <i>attics</i> whose names they could neither spell nor pronounce.
