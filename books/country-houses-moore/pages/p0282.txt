# Chapter IX.

As Mr. Morton, the rector of Stoke, was walking in the morning after the dress rehearsal, as it was called, he met Farmer Blunt, and, after talking to him on parochial business, said,

“Well, farmer, how did you and your family like the entertainment last night? for I saw you all there.”

“Why, your reverence,” replied Blunt, with some hesitation, “though to be sure it was very kind and condescending of my lord to ask such as we, and very condescending too, I must say, of my lady to let herself down to play antics to please we, yet, if I must own the truth, I did not much fancy it. Punch and Judy to my mind is worth two of it; one can understand Punch beating his wife, and throwing his child out at window
