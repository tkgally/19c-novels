# Chapter VI.

T<sc>here</sc> is nothing more difficult in the attempt, nor more hopeless in its execution, than to administer consolation to those who <i>will not</i> be consoled; who consider every evil that happens to them, not as the decree of Him who cannot err, nor as the consequence of their own folly, and its just punishment; and, who therefore submit to it with humble resignation; but, on the contrary, resist it, complain of it, and imagine themselves to be <i>marked</i> out for suffering; who, in one moment of anguish, forget all the blessings they possess, and perhaps have possessed, without one thought of the <i>Great Giver</i>; and bemoan themselves as if they were the <i>most</i> unfortunate, and the <i>only</i> unfortunate, people in the world.
