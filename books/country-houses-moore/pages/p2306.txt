I will take a lesson from Lady Laurel, and Mrs. Harlow; I don’t know how it was, but I never did get into any society the years I lived at the Abbey before; Mr. Beaumont had no taste for any companions but sportsmen and agriculturists and such dull people.”

“My dear aunt, if you will forgive me for saying so, don’t you think Miss Briggs helped to keep people away from you, with her <i>blueism</i> and her <i>talents</i>?”

“Perhaps you are right, my good Briggs had many excellent qualities, but she did not understand how to get up an acquaintance, she was always getting hold of some supremely clever horror; but you must do <i>your</i> best, Emily, to help me, and together we may manage it.”
