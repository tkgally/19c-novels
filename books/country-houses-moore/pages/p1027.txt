I have ordered the scene to be replaced; the drop scene is down, but that crab Fitz-Gibbon would not sing one of his comic songs to put the people in good humour; however the Count has undertaken that, and I hope he will be ridiculous enough to amuse the audience.”

Lord John was walking up and down the room, with the hasty step that betrayed extreme suffering—he intreated haste might be made to resume the performance, he was quite able to go through his part. Mr. Duprée, who was a cooler judge than any one of the party, urged this also, seeing that Lord John was determined to have nothing done till the play was over, and therefore in a very few minutes all was declared ready for the renewal of the performance.

The audience in general had not been aware that any one was hurt, not even Lord Tintern suspected it; he had only therefore attended to the replacing the scene, and making it secure from any future accident.
