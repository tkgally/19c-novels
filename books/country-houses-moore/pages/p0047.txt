“I am so glad Mr. Vernon,” she exclaimed, “that I have at last discovered your turn, I felt sure you wrote Whims and Oddities, and had some hand in Sayings and Doings! I was certain your expressions were so familiar to me I must have met with them in a book. Let me know when you have another volume on the stocks; I can help you to some capital stories, even to banditti, if you like them.”

Frederic was all gratitude, and would not fail to remind her of her promise, if he ever should be guilty of writing a book, but she was very safe at present, as he professed himself a decided plodder.

Turning over one of the splendid little volumes we have just described, she found a puzzle composed of angles and squares.

“Now you positively must assist me, Mr. Vernon, you cannot have forgot Euclid; do tell me what all these mathematical figures mean, for I perceive you are a matter-of-fact-man, perhaps you can draw my horoscope?”
