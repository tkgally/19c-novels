For one moment the whole party looked a little aghast, but Lady Vernon, with her usual good humour and tact, said,

“I am sorry Major Mac Venom that you are so displeased with a joke which has already gone half round the table; and I dare say if you had not destroyed the Gazette, <i>I</i>, and <i>my schools</i>, and <i>pensioners</i>, would have come in for our share; and I hope, I should have joined the laugh even against myself.”

The angry major, though he looked a little ashamed at this amiable and gentle reproof, could only bow, and envy her ladyship her calm and happy temper, and every one else was anxious to finish breakfast.

The ladies had much to wind up. Grace had a last lesson in painting and modelling to give Jane Sedley, and her sister had verses to copy in her album. And Miss Sedley had costumes to colour. Major Mac Venom was preparing to depart, and would have thought himself too happy if he also had gone before breakfast.
