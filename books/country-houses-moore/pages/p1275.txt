“Jesu Maria!” exclaimed the astonished abigail, whose adoration of family was not inferior to that of her mistress, for she was herself a Milesian, and had due respect for the long line of kings the O’Brians numbered in their pedigree, and considered her own consequence greatly augmented by the circumstance, and thought her own dignity compromised by mixing with the servants of a <i>parvenu</i>, and in one moment her aimable friend Mon. Dujeu, Sir Godfrey’s man, fell one hundred per cent in her estimation, by living with a man of yesterday, but giving vent to, “thoughts that burn,” she said, “I am sure Monsieur does not know this, he is too good a Catholic to have told me false, he takes his master for <i>mi Lor Anglais</i>, as he calls him.”

“Well then,” said her mistress, “don’t you be a busy-body-thankless, and undeceive him, he will get as well paid, and perhaps better, than with one of a more illustrious race.”
