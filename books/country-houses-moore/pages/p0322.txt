The party having seated themselves in a group on some adjoining tombstones, and requested Mr. <i>Everard</i> Price to give them the Poems, the Ode ‘on a distant view of Eton College’ was the first read; but previously, Mr. Price pointed out the different scenes that were alluded to in the poem. He read, or rather recited, with the important air of a school-boy, declaiming “Ye distant spires, &c.”

At one end of the same stone, sat Miss Amelia Riley and Mr. Duprée, and at the other, Emma, with Lord John reclining behind her, so that he had the command of her ear, without Mrs. De Cameron, who was the person from whose observation he wished to escape, knowing when he addressed Emma; when Mr. Price came to the line

<verse>
‘And catch a fearful joy.’
</verse>

Lord John said in a low tone, “how that describes my present position!”

Emma made no answer. When was read,

<verse>
‘The sunshine of the breast.’
</verse>

He added, “may you, Miss Legh, <i>ever</i>, as now, enjoy <i>that</i>, however deeply you may inflict

<verse>
‘Sorrow’s piercing dart.’”
</verse>
