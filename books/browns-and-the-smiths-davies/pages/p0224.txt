[[cont]]Brown either. But Miss Richards, being a Smithite, did blame Mr. Brown. He had acted in a most unchristian manner. But “what could you expect from a man who thought week-day sermons out of place?” She would not, however, join in the censure on Hannah. She had “always liked the poor girl, though, perhaps, she had flirted a little lately.” Miss Richards had, besides, very exalted notions of what was due to parental authority. So she comfortably laid all the blame on Mr. Brown, and, whenever his name was mentioned, sighed, and added—

“And a man of his age, too! Ah! it is very sad.”
