But, at any rate, I feel sure—almost sure—change would do Hannah good,” said Clara, whose intuitions were generally much more correct than her arguments.

“Well, if you think so,” with desperate resolution, “let her have a change. She has done much for me, and I must do something for her. I am an old man, and miss anything I am accustomed to. Still to miss her for a few weeks,”—he stopped and added, looking out from the dining-room window on the churchyard, and the great box-ottoman tombstone, with the iron railings round it,—“I must be there first, Clara.”
