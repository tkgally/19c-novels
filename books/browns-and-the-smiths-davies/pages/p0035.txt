[[cont]]crême, of Goslingford, did not, for a time visit the Smiths. It was even hinted that they were pushing and vulgar; but we all know, ever since we wrote in copy-books, that perseverance, like faith, removes mountains. And so the perseverance of the Smiths removed even the mountain of country-town exclusivism. The Smiths were a race of people who never lost anything for want of trying for it. Their pluck—the Brownites called it brass—was wonderful. Where the Browns were too proud or too lazy to befriend themselves, the Smiths stepped in preventing them. They ought to have been celebrated in Mr. Smiles’s book as remarkable instances of Self-Help. But, notwithstanding, the Smiths were good people enough—at least the Smiths of the generation of which I am writing.
