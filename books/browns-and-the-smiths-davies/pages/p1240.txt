This may perhaps partly explain her discomfiture when, on entering the shop, which was a large one, she perceived, sitting at a counter opposite, Mary Westcote, gaily dressed in the floweriest and most transparent of muslin dresses, with a stylish pink bonnet on her head, and a gay parasol in her hand; while Edgar Smith was standing beside her, and seeming to bend over her, while she smiled up in his face. Hannah felt, with a sudden spasm, that Mary was very pretty, had a brilliant complexion and a fashionable air, while she was painfully conscious that in her plain morning dress, her last year’s bonnet, and her mantle covered with dust, she must present a mortifying contrast.
