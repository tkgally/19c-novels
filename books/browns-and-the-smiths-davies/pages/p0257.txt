“And the confusion of ranks,” said Mr. Brown, “is so [[corr detestable.”|detestable.]] There is nothing so levelling as these trains. Nothing, in my opinion, has contributed so much to the popular democratic notions of the times, that the taxes should be imposed by those who don’t pay them, and that the ignorant should rule the educated.”

“I have no doubt, Mr. Brown, it is exactly as you say. When our aristocracy never travelled except in a carriage and four horses, they were much more looked up to than they are now; and respectable clergymen’s and lawyers’ daughters were in a very different position in a post-chaise and pair. I suppose one could hardly go in a post-chaise now?”
