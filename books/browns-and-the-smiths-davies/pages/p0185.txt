As it was a moot point in Goslingford, Hannah Brown had taken it into consideration whether or not it was right to dance. Now Hannah had rather an uncommon way of deciding such points—differing, for instance, from that of the Miss Smiths. When she was very fond of anything she was not at all ingenious in discovering reasons in its favour, but generally suspected its harmlessness, so singularly was it a fixed idea in her mind that self-denial rather than self-indulgence was the root of all virtue.
