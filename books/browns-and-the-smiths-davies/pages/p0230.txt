But Mrs. Splint knew that her eldest son, Fred, admired Mary Westcote, and at their party she had encouraged him not a little; and Mrs. Splint herself had looked favourably on the budding courtship. Now, she felt very angry with Edgar Smith for interfering with Fred’s affections and prospects, and full of pity for poor Hannah Brown. In spite of her husband, she would never again sit silent when Hannah Brown was accused of heartlessness. Mrs. Splint “was sure <i>she</i> was not heartless. She had not flirted with anybody else since.”
