[[cont]]as he feared to lose her, that she was the light of his eyes and his sole hope on earth. And with anxiety for her health mingled another anxiety which he hardly acknowledged to himself, but which seemed occasionally to find some expression when he spoke to Clara Wellby.

“Girls are often delicate at Hannah’s age, are they not, Miss Wellby? She is just twenty-one now. Her mother was very delicate when she was twenty-three. Don’t you think it is the spring winds? She exposes herself too much to them. It must be the spring winds, it cannot be anything else?”

“I am sure I don’t know, Mr. Brown. Hannah used not to feel the spring winds. She is very reserved, and I sometimes think Hannah has feelings she never mentions.”
