They set off in silence. Hannah did not see how she could have avoided going, and yet she had a sense of something like guilt on her conscience because she had gone. She was neither hard-hearted nor soft-hearted enough to act quite justly towards the Rev. Edward Edwards, and she was somewhat angry both with him and with herself; but as they walked up the hill, the glory of the evening and the beauty of the prospect were not long in having a soothing influence. Then Mr. Edwards’ manner was so gentle and unobtrusive, she could not but feel softened towards him, particularly as he had done nothing to merit her displeasure, but the reverse.
