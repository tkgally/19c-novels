When I have been in London—be not astonished, dear reader, narrow-minded and antiquated as my notions are, I have been in London, and in Paris, too—it has often struck me that the inhabitants of our street or square have been no more to us than the doors on their houses—provided they did not annoy us by playing pianos, or keeping little spaniels that barked all night—and I have been called on to admire the freedom from gossip which was the result of this state of affairs.
