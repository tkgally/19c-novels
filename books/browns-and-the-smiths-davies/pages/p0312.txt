It rained the next morning when the Welsh car stopped at the door of their little lodging to convey the two ladies to the nearest railway station. From thence it was several hours’ journey to Buttonborough, and, as the reader knows, still further to Goslingford. But in spite of the rain, they found Mr. Edwards standing under a tree, just where the road to the village turned in to the highway, and half a mile from the parsonage, with a large umbrella in one hand, and a bouquet of flowers in the other.
