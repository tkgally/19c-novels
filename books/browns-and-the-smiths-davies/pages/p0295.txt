“I should not have thought you were, Miss Brown; but I should think—I am sure you must have a feeling for the beauty of holiness.”

“I trust I have, Mr. Edwards; but surely the beauty of holiness consists rather in the charity which suffers long and is kind, than in ceremonies or decorations.”

“Oh! Miss Brown,” said the young man, looking much hurt. “You cannot surely suppose that I think otherwise.”

Hannah coloured up with vexation.

“Forgive me,” she said, “if I seemed to insinuate that you did, for indeed I do not think so. I have heard and seen too much of your work in this parish.”
