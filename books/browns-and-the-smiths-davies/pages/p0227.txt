[[cont]]Westcote was lost in astonishment at the heartlessness of Hannah Brown. “<i>She</i> could not understand any woman acting so to the man she loved. If she loved anybody, she would never give him up—she would die for him—she would live on bread and water for his sake—she would gladly jump into the sea for him—she considered it a woman’s privilege, as well as duty, to sacrifice everything for the man she loved.” These sentiments were maintained everywhere vehemently by Miss Westcote, and a more or less garbled account of them reached the ears of Edgar Smith himself.
