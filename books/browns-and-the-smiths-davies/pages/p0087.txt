But thoughts of a certain class are so much akin to feelings, that one can scarcely draw the boundary line. The difference is that the feelings are common, but are only translated, or shaped into thoughts, by the few. Thus, I have no doubt, William Brown, in a vague, unconscious way, experienced feelings akin to those described by the poet, as he sat in the afternoon sunshine, in the old garden where he had played as a child, amid the old familiar scenes where the sand of his hour-glass had run so noiselessly.
