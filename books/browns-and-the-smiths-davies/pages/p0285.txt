And then he put up a fine carved stone to her—a very idolatrous thing, the Ranters say, with a cross on it, and a round thing, and a three-sided thing, which all mean something Popish; and he goes to pray there every day, and he visits more than ever, and he’s always a-talking of his wife.”

“Is he rich?” asked Miss Clara.

“No, ma’am—not as I knows of. He is a gentleman born, and can trace his family, they say, in the female line, to Owen Glyndwr.”

Both the ladies were much interested in this narrative, though Miss Clara shook her head over the cross on the tombstone, and thought it almost as Popish as the Ranters did. She feared Mr. Edwards was a “Puseyite.”
