When Mr. Brown and Mr. Smith met each other, they bowed with great politeness. So did Miss Brown and the Miss Smiths. They were Browns and Smiths, you see, and not Montagues and Capulets; and whoever heard of two respectable elderly attorneys drawing their swords, or even doubling their fists, English fashion, at each other, however great their antagonism?

Yet there is nothing new under the sun, and the very same spirit animated the commonplace Browns and Smiths in our own vulgar days, that wrought on the Montagues and Capulets “in the brave days of old.” That pretty little table in Mr. Smith’s drawing-room, at Tudor Lodge, is, after all, only a bit of maple-wood, with exactly the same properties as any other bit of maple-wood. <i>Cotelettes de</i>
