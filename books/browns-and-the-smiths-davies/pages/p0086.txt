Mr. William Brown had never heard of Keats, and would most likely have despised both him and his poetry if he had. But that old ancestral garden of his—for Browns, even when attorneys, really have ancestors as well as Capulets—was the very place to experience such thoughts, as no other writer, prosaic or poetical, has so well expressed—

<verse>
“And calmest thoughts come round us—as of leaves
Budding—fruit ripening in stillness—autumn dews
Smiling at eve upon the quiet sheaves—
Sweet Sappho’s cheek—a sleeping infant’s breath—
The gradual sand that through an hour glass runs—
A woodland rivulet—a Poet’s death.”
</verse>
