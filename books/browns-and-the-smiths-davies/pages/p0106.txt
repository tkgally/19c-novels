“Not much danger of that, I think, sir.”

But though Mr. Smith had not spoken before, he had been listening to all that had passed. Now, Mr. Smith was a clever man—a man whose mind at once embraced the bearings of most things—a quality which had made him a good lawyer. He was also really an affectionate father, and like most men who have made money, he was by no means indifferent to the glittering dross—the world said he was fond of it. He had never wished to quarrel with old Brown. It was old Brown who had insisted on quarrelling with him—most unreasonably. Was there any lawyer who would not take all the respectable business he could get?—and if some of old Brown’s clients had come over to him, was he going to refuse them?
